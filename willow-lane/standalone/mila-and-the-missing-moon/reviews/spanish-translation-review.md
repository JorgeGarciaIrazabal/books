## Required local repairs, in priority order

References are to manuscript/es/ unless specified. Line numbers identify this snapshot.

1. **Chapter 9, line 23: adapt the visible label count.** “había tres palabras” precedes “AL JARDÍN.” English TO THE GARDEN has three words; Spanish has two. Change to **“había dos palabras.”** Retain “cuatro letras” for LUNA at line 35: that count is correct. This is a definite concrete discrepancy at the solution reveal.

2. **Chapter 3, lines 3 and 25: restore whom the accusation and correction address.** English “Why did you take our moon?” addresses Bea alone; “¿Por qué se llevaron nuestra luna?” makes the alleged taker plural, contradicting the following singular exchange and diluting Mila's direct accusation. Use **“¿Por qué te llevaste nuestra luna?”** Dad's response to Mila likewise changes from singular to plural: “Ustedes las encontraron… ¿Qué más saben?” Use **“Sí, las encontraste —dijo papá—. ¿Qué más sabes?”** Preserve the later apology as written.

3. **Chapters 7–9: keep boxes concrete.** English *carton* repeatedly becomes *cartón*, readily heard as cardboard material/sheets, despite the containers having depth, lids and contents. Repair these six occurrences to *caja*, with agreement adjustments:
   - Chapter 7, line 55: “tres cartones anchos” → **“tres cajas anchas”**; subsequent “junto a ellos” → **“junto a ellas.”**
   - Chapter 8, line 9: “un cartón de papel doblado… el siguiente” → **“una caja con papel doblado… la siguiente.”**
   - Chapter 8, line 77: **“Una caja profunda y angosta no la habría contenido. Una ancha y poco profunda, sí.”**
   - Chapter 9, line 13: “un cartón ancho” → **“una caja ancha.”**
   - Chapter 9, line 21: “otro cartón. Era poco profundo y cuadrado” → **“otra caja. Era poco profunda y cuadrada.”**
   - Chapter 9, line 43: “Pusieron el cartón plano” → **“Pusieron la caja en posición horizontal.”**
   These repairs protect the dimensional comparison that lets the children narrow the search. Retain genuine material uses such as the cardboard tab and clean cardboard sheet.

4. **Chapters 7, 9 and 12: distinguish reaching toward something from obtaining it.**
   - Chapter 7, line 63: “Mila se acercó hacia la esquina más cercana” changes reaching toward the tray to walking toward it. Use **“Mila extendió la mano hacia la esquina más cercana.”** This preserves the immediate reason for June's two-adult-lift intervention.
   - Chapter 9, line 27: “Ella no alcanzó el nudo” suggests inability to reach. English says she did not reach for it: she deliberately waits to read the opposite label. Use **“No extendió la mano hacia el nudo.”**
   - Chapter 12, line 29: “Mila alcanzó el lápiz” suggests she got it, directly before she cannot find it. Use **“Mila extendió la mano para tomar el lápiz.”**

5. **Chapter 11, lines 43 and 49: retain the mixed group.** “caminar con nosotras” / “Bea se unió a ellas” excludes Noah and Dad grammatically, though both are carrying the same bar. Use **“caminar con nosotros”** / **“Bea se unió a ellos.”** The following “de mi lado” already makes Mila's invitation specific without changing the group.

6. **Chapter 12, line 13: preserve ownership of the drawing.** English distinguishes “her first notes” from “his drawing of the yard.” Spanish repeats “sus primeras notas, su dibujo del patio,” leaving the drawing attributed ambiguously to Mila or Noah. Minimal repair: **“Después venían las primeras notas de Mila, el dibujo del patio de Noah…”** This matters because the ending acknowledges both children's contributions.

7. **Chapter 1, line 15: restore the librarian's statement.** “¿Son ustedes justo quienes necesito?” turns “You're just who I need” into an uncertain question. Use **“Ustedes son justo quienes necesito. ¿Movieron la luna después del almuerzo?”** The second question correctly remains plural.

8. **Four short usage repairs for clear oral comprehension.**
   - Chapter 4, line 55: “Tocó un mensaje en su teléfono” → **“Escribió un mensaje en su teléfono.”** English tapping means composing the message here.
   - Chapter 7, line 49: “Pero no era” → **“Pero no lo era.”** Restore the predicate referring to the apparent promise.
   - Chapter 10, line 83: “Los faroles alineaban las mesas” → **“Los faroles estaban alineados sobre las mesas.”** The lanterns occupy the tables; they do not arrange them.
   - Chapter 11, line 89: “Mila la miró hacia arriba” → **“Mila alzó la vista para mirarla.”** Keep the following look at their own moon. The present construction tangles the object of looking with its direction.

## Optional refinements and queries

These are not missing clues or grounds for replacing the voice.

- Chapter 1, line 39, “Yo cerré la tapa yo misma”: remove the first *Yo* to avoid an accidental stumble.
- Chapter 11, line 99, the boy says “La mía necesita una cola.” His earlier *forma de papel* permits a feminine reading, but the immediate noun is *farol*. **“Mi farol necesita una cola”** would remove the uncertainty without imposing a disputed gender antecedent.
- Chapter 12's *pava*, *pileta* and *frazada* create a noticeable regional cluster within otherwise mixed vocabulary. They are valid Spanish, not grammar errors. For the broad-neutral assumption, consider **“Papá puso agua a hervir,” “fregadero,” “manta.”** A target-region decision could also justify retaining them. Words such as *alféizar* and *arriate* can remain with parent support; age eight does not require baby talk or universal simplification.
- Chapter 9's “una palabra hermosa y corta” preserves the source image, although *Encontrada* is appreciably longer than *Found*. Dropping *y corta* is optional; it is not a factual count error.
