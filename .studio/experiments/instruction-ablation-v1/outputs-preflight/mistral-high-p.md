## F1-D
The ticket room smelled of lamp oil and the lake. The lamp pooled warm on the counter; the token stayed cold in Neri's right coat pocket. Olin leaned on the counter the way he used to lean on their father's workbench, all elbows and patience gone sour somewhere between then and now.

"You said you had to see someone," Neri said. "Say who."

"I'll tell you on the boat."

"There is no on-the-boat for two." She did not say: tell me, and maybe I will care. She touched the token through the wool. "One trip. One."

Olin smiled like a man holding a winning hand he was not allowed to show. "Then be careful whose pocket it sleeps in."

At 17:24 the lamp flickered. Neri watched the clock the way she used to watch the apprenticeship gate: every minute a door closing. She still wanted him to ask. She still wanted, stupidly, for the asking to cost him something. "I earned that gate," she said. "Not you. Not tonight."

"Give it to me, Neri."

"I would rather lose you than lose it." The words came out dry, and she hated that they were true. The token stayed in her right pocket; she pressed her palm flat over it, feeling its edge bite. "I am walking to the landing. You can walk too. But the token is mine, and the ferry leaves whether you explain yourself or not."

Olin's jaw tightened. He had no token, no second boat, no argument that was a reason. "You always picked the thing over the person."

Maybe. Neri did not answer. At 17:28 she turned toward the door, the token locked in her right coat pocket, and let the lamp-oil smell be the last thing between them. The commitment was made and it was costly: she would board with it, and he would stand on the near bank without a reason.

## F2-D
The key sat on the desk like a dare. Rafi Kapoor had twenty minutes and one booth, and the only thing between him and his audition was the door.

Leena arrived with her daughter and no apology. The violin rode in its case. Pia leaned on the jamb and watched the key the way you watch a thing you are not allowed to touch.

"Twenty minutes," Rafi said. "That is the booking. After that the next group has it, and they do not move."

"We could have half an hour," Leena said, as though wanting it might change the booking.

"You could have fifteen. Pia could have fifteen. Not both." Rafi kept his hand near the key. He wanted her to ask for him. She asked for the room. "Just the room. Not forgiveness."

Pia shifted her weight, then the other. "I have to run the concerto," she said, not looking at either of them. "Once. Clean."

Rafi looked at his sister. She was good at standing in doorways. She was better at not saying why she had left the band, and he was done guessing. At 12:03 he picked the key off the desk. The metal was warm from the lamp. He put it in Leena's palm and closed her fingers over it.

"Pia records," he said. "Fifteen minutes, uninterrupted, done by 12:20. My audition—" He stopped. The math was simple and unfair: one booth, two takes, both due at 12:30, and only one fits. "It ain't a favor if you keep an account," Leena said, but he was already turning away, already hearing the next group in the corridor.

He had been useful when they needed the room. He would not be useful now, not for himself. At 12:05 he stepped back from the doorway and let them have the room, the key, the fifteen minutes. The concerto, once, clean.
