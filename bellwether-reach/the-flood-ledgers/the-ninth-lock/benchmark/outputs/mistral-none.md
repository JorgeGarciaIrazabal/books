## P1

With the warning bell at 17:30, the high tide at 18:00, and a post-bell change that takes forty minutes, the book’s question is not only who allowed the duplicate but what can still be saved, and what must be chosen.

Proposed local custom: the Tide Ledger. At the warning bell, the clerk must lay the current assignment beside any disputed certificate on the public counter. If the papers conflict, the older assignment stays physically chained to the ledger until a hearing, so paperwork has literal weight without adding gates, pumps, or magic.

Series engine: each installment turns a civic instrument—a ledger, seal, permit, or tide table—into a physical constraint. A competent outsider or insider must trace how a legal document became an impossible promise, then act before a deadline while old loyalties test the act.

## P3
The corridor smelled of boiled glue. Sera kept her splinted left arm against her ribs; the second chair was still out, though she would not use it.

“I need to see the South original,” she said.

“I need you to remember what you were before you decided I was beneath your time.”

She did not answer that. With her right hand she took the North certificate from her coat and set it on the desk. “It is registered,” she said. “So is yours. Put it beside mine and we can both look at the same lie.”

Ivo opened the drawer. He lifted the South original and held it above the desk, then lowered it without letting go. “You left the apprenticeship with your name in my book and a line through mine,” he said. “I have kept the line.”

“You kept the part that did not need doing,” she said.

He looked at her left wrist, at the splint. “You cannot hold this with that hand.”

“I do not need to hold it. I need to keep it from being the only one.”

The South certificate touched the desk. Ivo’s fingers stayed on it. “If I let you see it, you will take it.”

“If I wanted to steal paper, I would have come with a stronger right hand and a weaker conscience.”

For a moment he did not move. Then he slid the certificate two inches toward her, still under his palm. “There. It is in the light now. Say what you came to say.”

Sera looked at the certificate, then at the brass key in her right coat pocket. She did not reach for it. “I came to say that the lock does not care which of us is right. It only cares which paper is oldest.”

Ivo’s mouth changed, almost nothing. “That is not the thing I needed.”

“No,” she said. “But it is the thing that is true.”

He withdrew his hand from the certificate, leaving it uncovered between them. The bell was not due for twenty-six minutes.

## P4
At five o’clock the Registry smelled of boiled glue and cold iron. The public counter was scarred where a thousand flood files had been opened, and the air tasted of paste and wet wool. The tide clock over the counter read 18:00; the paper bell schedule beside it named 17:30. Sera came in with her left arm held against her side, the splint white under her sleeve. She did not reach for the second chair. Ivo looked up from the ledger, and his hand went still on the drawer handle. He had not moved the chair back under the desk.

“You have the South original,” she said.

“I have a registered original,” Ivo said. “A professional courtesy is not a legal instrument, and you are not here as a customer.”

Sera took the North certificate from inside her coat with her right hand and laid it on the desk. The paper made a dry sound. Her brass inspection key stayed in her right coat pocket; she did not need it yet. “The seals match,” she said. “Show me the second one. I am not asking for your trust.”

Ivo opened the drawer slowly. He lifted the South certificate and set it flat on the desk, but his palm came down before the paper settled. He did not look at her. “If I let you hold it, I become the clerk who lost it. If I do not, you call me the clerk who hid it. Both are true enough for a hearing. I did not ask to be the man who has to choose which truth gets wet.”

“Then do neither,” Sera said. She rested her splinted wrist on the edge of the desk without gripping it. “Let it lie in the light. I need to see what your seal actually promised.”

He lifted his palm just enough to show the paper. Both certificates bore the same Registry seal. Both said Paid for. All of it. The two originals lay side by side, and the lock beyond the wall could answer only one basin. North Basin held her brother. South Basin held the communal seed store. The twenty-minute walk, the forty-minute change, the bell at 17:30: there was no arithmetic that made both papers true. She felt the incompatibility in her teeth, before she could put it into a report.

“This is a freezeable dispute,” she said.

Ivo’s fingers tightened on the South certificate. “A freeze keeps the old assignment. It does not make room for water.”

“I know what it keeps,” Sera said. She could accuse him and give him time to make the second paper disappear. She could seize the certificate and turn a dispute into a theft. She could freeze the assignment and pay with the only authority she had. The license was not a symbol; it was the only instrument that could hold the dispute still. “I choose the paper staying where the public can see it.”

With her right hand she took her inspector’s license from her breast pocket and laid it beside the two certificates. The lamp made the photograph on the license look older than she was. She took the pen Ivo pushed across the desk and signed the surrender line. Her signature was steady because her left hand could not help it.

Ivo watched her sign. When the pen stopped moving, he registered the freeze and turned the assignment slip face down. “North Basin remains protected,” he said. “Until the hearing. Until the tide. A change after the bell takes forty minutes. You have bought time, not water.”

Sera did not pick up either certificate. She did not know why Ivo had registered the second paper, and he had not told her. She signed her name a second time, then set the pen down on top of the license. Her right thumb smelled of ink. Outside, the warning bell was still twenty minutes away; the tide was fifty.
