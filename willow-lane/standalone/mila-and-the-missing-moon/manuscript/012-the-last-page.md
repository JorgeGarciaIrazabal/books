# 12. The Last Page

At home, Mila took off her shoes and found a tiny dry leaf stuck to one sock.

She set it on the kitchen table beside Noah's notebook. It was shaped a little like the damaged trolley wheel, if the wheel had been green and had a stem.

Dad put the kettle on.

“Milk?” he asked.

Mila nodded. She was thirsty in a sleepy sort of way. Her legs felt as if they were still walking.

She opened the notebook to Noah's sums. Then came her first notes, his drawing of the yard, the wheel with a bite, and the box with its two messages.

At the bottom of that drawing was the word she had pressed so hard into the page.

*Found.*

She ran a finger lightly over the marks on the other side. She could feel them without turning the paper over.

Dad set a cup of milk beside her.

“How's the last page?”

“Empty so far.”

Mila reached for the pencil.

It wasn't beside the notebook. She checked beneath the leaf and under the book. She patted both pockets.

“Oh, no.”

“What?”

“I've lost Noah's pencil.”

Dad took a sip of tea. “When did you last have it?”

“At the library. No, here. I had it when we came in.”

She pushed back her chair. Perhaps it had rolled under the table.

Dad was smiling into his mug.

“What?” she asked.

He touched the side of his head, just above his ear.

Mila put her hand to her own head.

There was the pencil, tucked behind her ear like Mrs. Bell's.

“I knew that,” she said.

Dad raised his eyebrows.

“I knew it just now.”

They laughed, and Mila sat down again. She laid the pencil flat across the open notebook where she could see it.

“This one was a short case,” Dad said.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

Mila drank some milk. Then she turned to a clean page.

She drew the moon hanging in the library window. Her circle wasn't as round as Noah would have made it. She added the crooked paper join, the little star patch, and the strings along the bottom.

Below it she wrote, *Mila and Noah found the moon.*

She looked at the sentence for a moment.

Then she added, *Bea helped. Dad helped. Mrs. Bell, June, and Mr. Moss helped too.*

“That's quite a page,” Dad said.

“There's still room.”

At the bottom, she drew the trolley. This time she remembered all four wheels, including the one partly hidden behind the others.

Dad rinsed their cups. Water splashed softly into the sink.

Mila didn't write anything more about Bea. She had said the important part to Bea herself.

She closed the notebook and put the pencil through its elastic loop. Tomorrow, she would return them together.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

Upstairs, Mila washed the last stickiness from her fingers. Dad found a clean pair of pajamas while she brushed her teeth.

She could hear him moving around her room, putting a book beside the bed and drawing the curtains partway across.

“Leave a little gap,” she called through her toothpaste.

He did.

When she climbed into bed, a strip of moonlight lay across the blanket. She put her hand into it. The light didn't feel different from the rest of the room, but she liked having it there.

Dad sat beside her.

“A story?”

Mila looked at the book he held, then shook her head. “Tomorrow.”

He kissed the top of her head and switched off the lamp.

Through the curtain gap, the real moon shone above the rooftops. Their paper one was safe in the library window, with its small star showing.

Mila curled her hand beneath her cheek.

She knew where both moons were.
