# Chapter 3: The Kitchen Table Deal

Saturday morning started with hot coffee, a giant unfolded paper map of Spain, Marissa's yellow legal pad, and one tiny robot trapped under an upside-down ceramic mug.

"Mmph! Unfair! Let me out!" Garbanzo's muffled voice vibrated against the wood table. "I only suggested buying one tiny castle with a drawbridge! Think how happy our savings chart would look next to a moat!"

"Sit quietly under the cup until you learn basic subtraction," Marissa told him cheerfully, tapping the bottom of the mug with her pen.

She drew a thick line straight down the center of the yellow legal pad. On the left side she wrote **WHAT WE WANT**. On the right side she wrote **WHAT IT COSTS IN TIME**.

"Okay, mister spreadsheet genius," Marissa said, sliding the pen across to Jorge. "No guessing games today. Every cool dream we put on the left has to pay its real price on the right. Ready?"

Jorge cracked his knuckles, grinned, and scribbled `BIGGER CAR + SPORTS SEATS` at the top left.

Marissa flipped two pages of real math in her notebook, tapped her calculator twice, and wrote firmly on the right: `TEN EXTRA MONTHS OF MANHATTAN TRAINS`.

Jorge blinked. "Ten months just for nicer leather seats?"

"Plus insurance and Spanish parking," Marissa replied, sipping her coffee. "Want to add a fancy stone villa in the pine mountains too?"

She wrote `TEN MORE WINTERS` before Jorge could even reach for the cap of the pen.

"Ouch," Jorge muttered, rubbing his temples. "Put it like that and a flashy car suddenly looks like a rolling prison cell."

He crossed out `BIGGER CAR` and wrote `NORMAL RELIABLE HATCHBACK`.

*PING!*

Behind them on the counter, the copper pot chimed once, soft as a wineglass. The golden chickpea glowed a brighter amber.

Garbanzo kicked inside the upside-down mug until Marissa lifted it two inches so he could peek out like a tiny turtle under a shell.

"May I speak?" Garbanzo whispered dramatically. "No confetti! I swear on my green metal toes!"

"Go ahead," Jorge laughed, lifting the mug completely.

"Thank you, Boss," Garbanzo beamed, brushing imaginary dust off his golden-tan shell. "What if we take all our savings, count it the normal way once, and buy a modest, sunny house where we do not need two jobs?"

"Now you are talking," Marissa said. She wrote **THE THREE HOUSEHOLD RULES** at the bottom of the yellow sheet in neat, bold letters.

Rule One was **Fifty-Fifty Fair**. Neither adult ever had to beg the other for money to buy a coffee, fix a bike, or visit grandparents. After selling their New Jersey house, each partner got an equal share to spend right out of the same honest plan. Savings meant for when they grew old stayed safely locked away.

Rule Two was **A Real Safety Cushion**. "No living on razor edges," Marissa said firmly. "We keep steady cash sitting safely on the side so if the stock market has a lousy winter, we don't panic and end up right back under fluorescent lights."

Rule Three was **Living Streets Only**. Marissa tapped the paper map beside the butter dish. "Listen closely, Jorge. I do not want an empty house in the middle of nowhere just because the yard looks cheap online. I want sidewalks and kids on bikes. I want a bakery that smells amazing at eight in the morning, good schools for Clara, lively cafés, and big stores within twenty minutes."

Jorge leaned over the map, tracing the highways spreading out from Madrid. Some sunny beach towns down south looked cheap online, though Marissa worried they might turn quiet and empty after summer. Pretty mountain towns near Madrid sounded lovely, too, if prices didn't swallow their whole cushion.

"Both of us sign?" Jorge asked, holding out the ballpoint pen.

"Wait," Marissa smiled, sliding the yellow pad toward the counter.

Six-year-old Clara wandered in wearing polka-dot slippers and dragging her favorite stuffed yellow unicorn by one ear. She climbed onto a chair, squinted hard at the map, and grabbed a purple marker.

"Do I get a signature too?" she asked.

"Only if you follow the rules," Jorge teased. "Rule Four belongs to you today, kiddo. Think carefully and make sure Spain passes inspection."

Clara scribbled a shaky star next to `MARISSA` and `JORGE`.

The instant her marker lifted off the paper, warmth swept smoothly from the copper pot across the kitchen counter. The golden chickpea shone like honey held up to sunlight.

Even better, a thin thread of soft silver light curled down from the sunny windowsill near Poki's gray stone. Cool as night air, it drifted quietly over the coffee pot. It brushed straight across the paper map north of Madrid's snowy ridge, lingered for two seconds over a valley marked **Valladolid**, and vanished.

Jorge and Marissa looked at each other, wide-eyed.

"Did you see that?" Jorge asked softly.

Clara leaned down and smelled the map right where the silver gleam had faded. "Smells like clean fur and sunny porches," she said cheerfully, hopping down off her chair. "Now clear out. My Official Kid Inspection starts upstairs right before dinner!"
