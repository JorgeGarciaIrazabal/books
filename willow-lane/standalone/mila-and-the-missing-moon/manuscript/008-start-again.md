# 8. Start Again

Mila wrote *TO THE GARDEN* across the clean page.

“If it said that, why did Mrs. Bell use it?” she asked.

Noah sat beside her on the low wall outside the shed. “Maybe it wasn't the same box.”

Inside, June and Mr. Moss had opened a carton of folded paper. They were making space to reach the next one.

Dad put his bag down beside Mila. The tip of a paper star poked out.

“We should take these back,” she said. “They're getting bent.”

She smoothed the point with her thumb.

“And ask Mrs. Bell about the box?” Noah said.

Mila nodded. She wanted to begin at the empty table again. Now she had a different question to bring to it.

Dad told Aunt June where they were going.

“We'll keep checking here,” her aunt said. “We can lay the flat boxes out once we've cleared this side.”

Mila lifted the bag herself.

Back at the library, the paper fish had gained shiny tails.

Mrs. Bell was helping a little boy stick scales onto one with the back of a spoon.

“We've found lots of boxes,” Mila told her. “Mr. Moss moved one that said TO THE GARDEN.”

“He may have moved mine, then,” said Mrs. Bell. “I borrowed the box from June yesterday. It was empty.”

“Did you write anything on it?” Noah asked.

“MOON.”

Mila set Dad's bag on a chair.

“On the lid?”

“No. The lid had an old picture on it. I wrote on a thin side, where there was space.”

She held her hands apart to show the shallow edge.

Mila thought of the STARS box and its rows of pots. One old word could stay on a box while something quite different went inside.

“Did you check the other sides?” she asked.

Mrs. Bell put down the spoon.

“I'm afraid I didn't.”

Noah drew a box. On one edge, he wrote MOON. On the opposite edge, he wrote TO THE GARDEN.

Mila stood a book on the table to be the shed wall. Noah held his drawing beside it.

“If this side faced the wall—” he began.

“We couldn't read it,” Mila said.

“But Mr. Moss could read this one.”

She turned the notebook around. It was such a small turn.

Mrs. Bell brought over a clean sheet of cardboard. Mila and Noah spread the paper stars on it so they wouldn't curl in Dad's bag.

“The moon filled most of the box,” the librarian said. “There was hardly room for my fingers beside the frame.”

Mila remembered their wide round frame on the worktable. It had been thin, almost like a huge coin.

A deep, narrow carton wouldn't hold it. A broad, shallow one would.

She circled Noah's drawing with the pencil.

“We need to look at the flat ones,” Mila said. “And read every side.”

“They're opening boxes already,” Dad reminded her.

“I know. But they might not need to open them all.”

Mrs. Bell helped Dad slide the stars into a drawer to keep them safe.

“Tell me as soon as you find it,” she said. “If it's there.”

Mila liked that last bit less than she wanted to. It left a little space for being wrong.

She left it in her head, too.

Outside, the shower had stopped. The mint leaves shone. Someone was carrying the lantern table back into the yard.

Noah walked beside her, turning the notebook so the damp corner could dry in the air.

“Your page is flapping,” Mila said.

“It's trying to fly.”

She caught the edge before the wind could tear it.

Then they walked with the notebook held between them, all the way to the shed.
