# Mila and the Missing Moon — creative brief

Create the complete book requested by the author: a detective story for an eight-year-old girl, read aloud by her parents, using easy words without making the story simplistic. Approximate length: fifty pages.

Working choices (reversible assumptions): English; an eight-year-old girl detective; a fictional present-day neighborhood; gentle suspense, humor, family warmth; no violence or frightening criminal threat. These choices are the studio's, not specific author requests.

Mila investigates the disappearance of a large handmade moon lantern before the neighborhood lantern walk. Her friend Noah contributes practical tests and drawings. Mila makes an understandable wrong accusation, repairs the harm, and solves the mystery by testing evidence rather than choosing a person to blame. The missing moon is found and the lantern walk happens. The solution is an accidental move involving a reused box; no secret villain.

A satisfying, fair mystery: show the important clues before Mila combines them. Children cause the discoveries; adults offer permission and practical help without supplying the solution. Keep clues concrete and the timeline consistent. Include sensory detail and natural dialogue. Avoid a closing lecture.

Target 7,000–8,500 story words, twelve chapters, four designed pages per chapter, plus title and optional read-together page: fifty PDF pages total. Each chapter is a natural bedtime stopping point. Prose must stand without illustrations. Do not pad pages to meet a count. Small line drawings may decorate the edition.

Close third-person past tense through Mila; familiar words, varied sentences, occasional richer words explained through action. American spelling. No baby talk or assumption that girls prefer any particular color, hobby, or temperament. No marketing or developmental claims based on unperformed research.

Complete manuscript and printable PDF are required deliverables. Editable Markdown is also supplied. This is an original fictional story; no outside factual research is needed.
