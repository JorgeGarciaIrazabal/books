# kimi-k3:cloud: independent design consultation

My philosophy here is that structure should serve the human author, not constrain them. Since both Codex and OpenCode are fundamentally just LLM frontends executing Markdown prompts over the filesystem, the *repository is the software*. We must avoid the trap of simulating persistent staff; the human remains the Creative Director, and agents are stateless, highly specialized consultants who read the repository to gain context and write back to it.

Here is my proposal:

### (1) The 14-Role Roster (`.agents/`)
Agents are defined via standard Markdown files with YAML frontmatter for system instructions, ensuring they can be loaded by both Codex and OpenCode using simple scripts.

**Strategy & Canon (Worldview):**
1. **Worldbuilder**: Architect. Outputs `universe/canon/bibles.md`, `themes.md`, `shared_rules.md`.
2. **Fact-Cheker / Researcher**: Nonfiction lead and fiction verifier. Outputs `evidence/` citations, claims audits, and `research/`.
3. **Showrunner** (Series Planner): Outputs `series/arc_plan.md`, `release_strategy.md`, `book_summaries.md`.

**Manuscript & Narrative (Production):**
4. **Plot Architect**: Outputs structural outlines, beat sheets (`book/structure/`).
5. **Character Psychologist**: Outputs deep character profiles, arcs (`universe/characters/`).
6. **Scene Drafter**: Prose generation. Outputs `book/drafts/scenes/`.
7. **Dialogue Polisher**: Focuses purely on subtext, banter, and voice.
8. **Continuity Guard**: The canon enforcer. Reads prior scenes, updates `book/timeline.md`, flags contradictions.
9. **Knowledge Boundary Setter** (Crucial for Fiction): Decides who knows what. Outputs `book/character_knowledge.md` and `spoiler_boundaries.md` to prevent AI characters from inexplicably sharing secrets.

**Quality Assurance (Reviewing):**
10. **Structure & Pacing Analyst**: Reads the draft against the Plot Architect's plan. Outputs pacing reports.
11. **Prose Stylist & Line Editor**: Evaluates tone, sentence variety, removes "AI-isms" (e.g., overuse of "tapestry", "testament"). 
12. **Subject-Matter Reviewer** (Nonfiction): Verifies logical flow, pedagogical soundness, and accuracy against the Fact-Checker's data.
13. **Sensitivity & Risk Reader**: Checks for harmful biases, repetitive clichés, and plot logic holes.

**Meta:**
14. **Editor-in-Chief** (The Router): The agent the human talks to. Reads all outputs, synthesizes next steps, and assigns tasks to the other 13 roles. Does not write books.

### (2) Essential Artifacts (The Filesystem Truth)
Since agents are stateless, *files* are their memory. We need a strict hierarchy:
- **Universe Level**: `universe/canon/characters/`, `universe/canon/timeline.md`, `universe/canon/rules.md`. 
- **Series Level**: `series/overview.md`, `series/vision.md`.
- **Book Level (`{universe}/{series}/{book}/`)**:
  - `promises.md` (Plots foreshadowed that must be paid off).
  - `research/` (Nonfiction source material, or fiction historical/setting docs).
  - `knowledge_matrix.md` (Tracking what the reader knows vs. what characters know).
  - `voice_and_tone.md` (Specific to this book).

### (3) Creative Quality & Canon Acceptance Workflow
1. **Human Gatekeeping**: The human *always* commits the code. 
2. **The "Two-Pass" System**: Any major creative generation (e.g., a chapter) is generated by a *Drafter*, reviewed independently by the *Continuity Guard* and *Prose Stylist*, and then presented to the human. 
3. **Canon Acceptance**: If a Drafter invents a new character or magic system rule on the fly, the Continuity Guard flags it: "NEW CANON DETECTED." It only gets moved from `book/drafts/` to `universe/canon/` via a deliberate human command (e.g., `accept-canon.md` script) so the universe doesn't mutate accidentally.
4. **Versioning**: Standard Git. Every major beat, phase, or canon acceptance gets a commit tag.

### (4) Concurrency & Cross-Model Debate
Because LLMs are slow and context windows are precious, we do not want them endlessly chatting.
- **Asynchronous Handoffs**: Agents do not talk directly. They write structured Markdown reports to a `handoffs/` or `reviews/` directory. The next agent reads these files.
- **Cross-Model Debate (Adversarial Review)**: Use the best models for the right jobs. 
  - Have GPT-6 Astra (Codex) act as the grand Plot Architect and Worldbuilder.
  - Have Mistral Large 4 (OpenCode) act as the Scene Drafter to generate prose.
  - Have Kimi K3 (OpenCode) act as the aggressive, hostile critic of Mistral's prose. 
- **Bounded Debate**: Limit these debates to exactly 2 turns (Draft -> Critique -> Defense/Final Draft). Beyond this, you get token bloat and diminishing returns.

### (5) Nonfiction Adaptations
The filesystem structure holds perfectly, but the artifacts shift:
- `promises.md` becomes `core_arguments.md` (The thesis statement and expected takeaways).
- `knowledge_matrix.md` becomes `reader_assumptions.md` (What the reader is assumed to know by Chapter 3).
- The *Continuity Guard* becomes the *Evidence Tracker*, ensuring every claim in a chapter maps to a source in `research/evidence.md`.

### (6) The Anti-Overengineering Starter Path
Do not build complex JSON schemas, databases, or automated Python CI/CD pipelines on day one. Markdown and Git are all you need.
Start with **5 core agents only**: `showrunner`, `drafter`, `continuity-guard`, `researcher`, `editor-in-chief`. 
Your initial workflow should be:
1. Human talks to Editor-in-Chief.
2. Editor-in-Chief writes an outline.
3. Human approves outline.
4. Drafter writes Chapter 1.
5. Continuity Guard reads Chapter 1, extracts new world facts, updates canon. 
6. Human commits.

**Critical Warning to the Author:** The greatest danger of an AI-first studio is homogeneous, soulless prose and over-planning. Keep the agents in tightly bound boxes. Give them strict stylistic limits, force them to critique each other aggressively, and ensure the human is the ultimate arbiter of taste.
