# Chapter 9: Twelve Dragons and One Big Stew

By late May, the Garbanzo house looked like a cardboard fort struck by a joyful hurricane.

Sealed brown moving boxes stacked three high down the hallway. Two cheerful young buyers had signed the closing papers earlier that week and kindly let the family stay one final Friday night before dropping off the house keys on their way to Newark Airport.

Everything went smoothly right up until Jorge tried to zip Clara's bulging pink unicorn suitcase.

"I swear she sneaked bricks inside here," Jorge grunted, planting both knees gently onto the hard plastic shell to wrestle the metal pull upward. "Clara, what did you pack, bowling balls?"

*ZIP-PING!*

The metal zipper popped completely apart. With a loud *BOING*, the overstuffed lid flew open like a jack-in-the-box. Out rained twelve plush dragons, three unicorn headbands, a sparkly silver crown, and fourteen mismatched wool socks tumbling down the stairs!

"Hey! Watch out for Captain Sparklewing!" Clara yelped from the bottom step, diving to catch a pink plush dragon before it landed nose-first in her boots.

From Jorge's carry-on backpack by the door, Garbanzo peeked over a sweatshirt and shook with squeaky giggles. He had already switched into Airplane Power-Save Mode so airport security wouldn't mistake him for a noisy metal thermos.

Friday evening brought their big farewell dinner, packed with loud laughs, tight hugs, and the best smell on earth drifting out of the kitchen.

Aunt Lidia ruled the stove with a cheerful iron hand. She stirred a huge steaming pot of *cocido madrileño*, her famous Madrid chickpea stew brimming with tender beef, carrots, cabbage, and soft golden chickpeas.

Whenever Jorge wandered near the stove just to sneak a sniff, Aunt Lidia turned calmly and pointed a dripping wooden spoon right at his chest.

"Out!" Aunt Lidia barked fondly, shooing him toward the dining room. "Remember the rule, Jorge! Wire my porch light or play your bouncy wall-tennis! Touch my *cocido* pot and your dinner goes to the seagulls!"

Everyone roared with laughter.

Clara tore around the chairs playing dragon-tag with her playful young cousins Denny and Isma. Their sneakers drummed cheerfully across the bare wood floor. Nearby, smiling grandparents passed warm bread and ladled out bowls while sneaking extra hugs whenever Clara paused for air.

Saying goodbye at the front porch was the hardest part of all.

Grandpa clapped Jorge warmly on the shoulder. "Text me the minute you wire that Spanish porch light, engineer."

Grandma tucked two small squares of dark chocolate into Clara's hand before folding Marissa into a long, tight hug. Aunt Lidia wiped her hands cleanly on a checkered dish towel, blinking hard as she smiled at the whole crew.

"You really built something wonderful together," Aunt Lidia whispered to Marissa and Jorge on the front steps. "Save me a warm chair near that Spanish bakery, okay?"

"Guaranteed," Marissa promised, hugging her back hard.

Isma and Denny bounced beside the mailbox until the last minute. "We're visiting next summer!" Denny yelled.

"Deal!" Clara called back, clutching Captain Sparklewing tight. "Bring extra marshmallows!"

When the quiet house finally settled for their last night, Marissa walked softly over to the kitchen window. She folded Poki's silver cat collar bell and smooth gray stone inside clean white cloth, slipping them gently into her carry-on bag. Across the room, Clara tucked her warm white backyard pebble safely into the zippered star pocket of her pink travel backpack beside her favorite book.

Next morning, they handed over the house keys with a wave, and Saturday's flight out of Newark lifted right on schedule. The silver jet climbed above bright white clouds and turned smoothly east toward morning sunshine in Spain.

Two hours over the dark Atlantic Ocean, cabin lights dimmed blue. Wrapped in an airline blanket beside Marissa, Clara cracked her plastic window shade two inches. Clear stars glittered above the wingtip.

Holding her white backyard pebble against the cool glass, she whispered softly into the sky, "Goodnight, Poki. See you by the sunny garden wall."
