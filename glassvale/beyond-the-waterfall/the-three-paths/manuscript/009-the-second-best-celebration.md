# Chapter 9: The Second-Best Celebration

Two nights later, every candle in Glassvale's Great Hall burned warm amber for the Autumn Feast.

Long oak tables groaned under roasted apples, buttered rolls, honey tarts, and tall silver-rimmed pitchers steaming with spiced cinnamon cider.

Headmistress Monica tapped her spoon cleanly against her porcelain teacup, and the cheerful roar faded to quiet grins.

"Our Crown Seal rests safe in deeper wards," Monica told the hall, her silver spectacles gleaming warmly in the candlelight. "Our pipes run hot, our great oak stands green, and three stubborn first-years reminded all of us why Glassvale has three Paths instead of one. In honor of tonight's feast, I ordered the *second* most expensive spiced cider in Mistmarket. Naturally, the apples are far sweeter."

The whole hall erupted into laughter and raised their mugs high.

Across the table, Sam carefully floated a sugar cookie onto his plate without wobbling a single crumb, beaming proudly behind his crooked glasses until Maya clapped him so hard on the back that the cookie hopped into his lap anyway. Pebble glowed a happy turquoise on the rim of Maya's cider cup and let out a contented chirp.

Halfway through dessert, Jorge strolled over to Clara's bench with grease still smudged politely behind one ear and set a small, gleaming brass pocket-compass beside her plate.

"Tested four times," Jorge said with a warm grin. "Never freezes, never spins in mist, and always points back toward your own Path tower if a storm rolls in. Not bad for a Pure Magic kid, huh?"

"Thank you, Jorge," Clara said, holding the cool brass case carefully between both palms. "I counted every turn."

Right behind him stood Marissa with Pip curled sleepily around her shoulder. "Tomorrow morning," Marissa said softly, smiling at both Clara and Maya, "the Lantern-Foxes usually sun themselves along the Living Dome's upper roof. You are both welcome anytime, even if you bring violet sparks."

Even Victor lingered awkwardly near the oak pillars on his way out, hands tucked inside his pockets. "Look, first-year," he muttered quickly without quite meeting Clara's eyes. "That shield in the courtyard was not terrible."

Clara bit back a grin and nodded solemnly. "Thanks, Victor."

After the feast wound down, Clara stepped out onto the open stone balcony overlooking the canyon. Down below, the great Mistgate waterfall glowed pale silver under the rising autumn stars, humming softly like a distant lullaby.

Heavy, unhurried footsteps approached behind her, and Clara turned to see Peter standing in the archway in his plum-and-gold robes, looking out across the valley with that warm, steady smile that had cheered her through her very first week.

"It has been quite a first two weeks, Clara," Peter said gently, resting one large, reassuring hand on her shoulder. "You walked into Pure Magic with wild bright sparks, and tonight you learned how to weave them with makers and living roots alike. You have made me very proud."

"We have four more pieces of the World-Glass to keep safe, though," Clara said, looking up at him earnestly. "Silas said The Hollow Circle is not done."

"Then we will be ready for them," Peter promised warmly, his kind eyes crinkling in the starlight. "Rest easy tonight. Glassvale is well guarded."

He gave her shoulder one last encouraging pat, wished her good night, and walked calmly back into the candlelit corridor, the small antique brass sand-timer at his belt catching warm golden light like an ordinary pocket watch.

Clara leaned happily against the stone railing, spinning her new brass compass between her fingers and watching the Mistgate shimmer below.
