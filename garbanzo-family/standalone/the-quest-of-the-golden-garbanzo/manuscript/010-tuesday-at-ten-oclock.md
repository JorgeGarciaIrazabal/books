# Chapter 10: Tuesday at Ten O'Clock

At seven-forty-five on their first ordinary Tuesday in Valladolid, warm sunlight poured across the bedroom floor instead of a six o'clock alarm buzzer.

Jorge padded into the bright kitchen wearing shorts and comfortable sandals. Smiling at Marissa, he wisely stayed three respectful steps clear of the frying pan while she flipped crisp cinnamon toast.

On the low kitchen windowsill beside pots of fresh green mint rested two familiar treasures from New Jersey: Poki's silver cat bell and his smooth gray river stone. Down the hall on Clara's shelf, her white backyard pebble sat safely next to twelve plush dragons and four thick English and Spanish chapter books.

"Wait up, sleepyhead," Jorge grinned at Clara as he slipped on his sunglasses and scooped up her brand-new green school backpack.

Outside on the bright front stone walk, Jorge pulled his house keys out of his shorts pocket. Right next to the silver keys dangled the small gold sunburst charm from their New Jersey copper pot.

He turned the key in the black iron gate. *Click.* Crisp morning air drifted through the yard, smelling faintly of toasted pine and fresh bakery bread two streets away.

Marissa stepped onto the sunny stone patio with her coffee just as their neighbor Elena walked by carrying a warm loaf of crusty bread.

"¡Buenos días, Marissa!" Elena called cheerily across the gate. "Meet us for coffee on the plaza this Friday with the school moms!"

"I'd love to!" Marissa called back, raising her mug.

Holding Clara's warm hand, Jorge strolled toward her new school three blocks down smooth, bike-friendly sidewalks. Halfway through the leafy park, Clara balanced along the low stone curb and launched into a speedy bilingual tale.

"So the grumpy frost giant stole all the clocks," Clara announced brightly, spreading both arms wide. "Then *la princesa* unicorn called *un dragón* wearing purple skates, and they smashed his ice door! *¡Pum!* Right off the wall!"

Jorge laughed hard enough to make a passing baker smile. Tuesday morning was twenty-five minutes old, and he hadn't checked a clock once.

After kissing Clara goodbye at the bustling school gate filled with waving kids and bike bells, Jorge walked home unhurried. Around nine-thirty, he rolled up his sleeves, grabbed his blue tool bag from the garage, and climbed a two-step stool to wire a new solar porch lantern.

"Watch a true electrical pro at work," Jorge bragged toward the kitchen window, snipping two bright copper wires clean.

He clicked the cover shut and pressed the test switch.

*POOF!*

A comical puff of harmless pale blue smoke drifted up past Jorge's nose like a cartoon mustache.

Behind the glass, Marissa laughed so hard she nearly spilled her orange juice. "Sure glad we kept you off pancake duty!"

Grinning sheepishly, Jorge flipped the cover open, swapped two crossed wires cleanly into the brass clips, and tapped the button again. Crisp warm solar light beamed straight through the frosted glass.

"Boom," Jorge chuckled, wiping soot off his thumb. "First try. Almost."

Right then, sitting on the shady patio table beside an empty saucer, a palm-sized golden-tan robot gave one soft, bright chime.

*Ding!*

At **10:00 a.m. sharp**, Garbanzo lifted all four green metal feet smoothly off the tile, straightened his crooked unicorn sticker, and blinked calm amber eyes. His stomach screen glowed clean green without spilling a single scrap of fake confetti onto the roses. Humming softly inside, his belly slot slid out one crisp gold paper slip no wider than a bookmark:

*Tuesday Morning: Free and Paid For.*

That evening, dusk painted the white house walls soft orange while all three of them lingered outside over crusty bread, olives, and savory chickpea stew nobody rushed to finish. Inside the quiet hallway, every laptop slept dark. On the patio table sat only dinner plates and warm twilight.

Clara paused mid-bite with tomato sauce on one cheek, pointing quietly toward the back garden wall.

Sitting peacefully on the warm red brick in soft evening silver light was sleek, shiny Poki, glossy dark fur gleaming in the quiet dusk. The cat curled his tail neatly around his paws and gave Clara one slow, affectionate blink. He stayed resting calmly on the warm brick while the whole family laughed together below.
