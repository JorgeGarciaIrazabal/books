# Chapter 6: The Valladolid Bounce

Early Thursday morning, the family drove two hours north past the mountain ridge into bright Castilian sunshine and reached **Valladolid**.

Over the next two days, they explored quiet, leafy suburbs tucked right beside the city: **La Flecha** and **Zaratán**. Everywhere Marissa looked, people were actually outdoors enjoying the crisp afternoon sun.

Kids zipped down wide sidewalks on scooters outside warm bakeries. Neighbors wearing knit scarves walked small dogs past lively café tables. Twice, families carrying grocery bags smiled and waved them across crosswalks. Less than twenty minutes down the road stood **Río Shopping**, a huge, bright center packed with clothing stores, groceries, restaurants, and family hangouts.

"Now this passes inspection," Marissa said, beaming out the car window as two kids rolled past eating chocolate croissants. "Real streets, real schools, and warm bread ten minutes from your pillow."

On Friday afternoon, they arrived thirty minutes early to tour one last house near La Flecha. Across the street from a green park stood a glass-walled *padel* club buzzing with sharp thwacks of yellow tennis balls.

Jorge pressed his forehead right against the fence like a kid outside a candy shop. Inside the crisp blue court, players bounced fast shots off thick clear back walls in a lively mix of tennis and squash.

"Whoa," Jorge grinned. "Want to bet I can still play?"

Before Marissa could stop him, two friendly local players named Carlos and Sofía waved from court three. Their fourth partner had gotten stuck in traffic, and they needed someone quick for one tiebreak set. Ten minutes later, Jorge was out there in rented court shoes and a borrowed racket, diving across turf to test "the visitor from New York."

Up on the bright bleachers, Clara cheered loudly while Garbanzo poked his round golden-tan head two inches out of Jorge's sports duffel.

"Hit the glass wall, Boss!" Garbanzo yelled, forgetting he wasn't supposed to talk in public. Luckily, loud laughter from the next court covered him cleanly. "Remember geometry!"

Down match point at six to five, Carlos smashed a fast spinning corner shot straight toward the back glass. Everyone thought the point was over.

Jorge sprinted past the net and leaped toward the corner. Waiting one split second for the ball to bounce off the clear back glass wall, he whipped a smooth lob high over both rivals. It dropped gently inside the far line and rolled dead against the fence.

"Point!" Carlos shouted, dropping his hands to his knees before laughing hard and slapping Jorge's palm across the net. "Okay, New York! If you move here, you play on *my* side next Tuesday!"

Still sweating and grinning from ear to ear, Jorge jogged down the quiet side street with Marissa and Clara toward their four o'clock showing.

Halfway down a sunny block lined with young olive trees, Clara suddenly stopped and pointed.

"Look!" she whispered.

Perched calmly along a warm brick garden wall stood a sleek dark cat in a narrow beam of late-autumn sun. Only sleek dark fur caught silver in the light as the cat curved a quiet tail around his paws. Before Marissa even blinked, the cat stepped smoothly behind a wooden garden trellis and vanished.

Behind that exact brick wall stood a black iron gate. It opened onto a bright, modern one-story house with three sunny bedrooms and clean white walls. Dark blue solar panels gleamed on the roof. A grassy backyard gave Clara room for cartwheels, and a roomy garage waited for Jorge's tool chest.

Over the low fence next door, a smiling older neighbor in a warm wool vest paused from raking crisp autumn leaves. "¡Buenas tardes!" he called warmly. "Checking out the sunny corner house? The kids on this street ride bikes until dark every afternoon!"

Inside, afternoon light filled big clean windows ready to move into without knocking down a single wall.

Standing in the bright living room, Jorge pulled a folded page from Saturday's yellow legal pad out of his pocket and checked the math. Normal small car: safe. Emergency house-repair cushion: untouched. Yearly travel budget and fifty-fifty split: clean. Every single number fit without breaking a single rule that kept the Golden Garbanzo pot warm back home.

"Neighbors who smile, Río Shopping twenty minutes down the road, glass *padel* courts down the block, and no three-year wait on muddy pipes," Marissa said softly, running a hand along the cool kitchen counter.

"And solar panels," Jorge added, already eyeing the backyard wall with a grin. "And no steep stairs to climb."

Clara tested the grassy yard with one big twirl in her sneakers. "When do we move in?"
