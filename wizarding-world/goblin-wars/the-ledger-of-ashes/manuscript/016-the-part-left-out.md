# Chapter Sixteen: The Part Left Out

For a long time after Kelm left, Neri listened to the path.

Ragna sat with her back to the kiln wall. The stone held the cold. She had put her coat under Neri's injured leg and was trying not to watch the swelling above the boot.

“He may not come back,” Neri said.

“He may not.”

“He took the healer's address.”

“Yes.”

“If he goes to her, he has to say who he is.”

Ragna looked towards the gap in the wall. Kelm could disappear into the woods with no paper tying him to them. At the healer's address, he would have to explain where he had come from.

A jay called, then another. Neri shifted her leg and could not hide the pain quickly enough.

“Stop looking at it,” she said.

“I wasn't.”

“You were.”

Ragna looked at the ground. After a while Neri gave a small, unwilling laugh.

“My mother does that. Looks somewhere worse.”

They heard wheels before they heard voices. Kelm came through the trees carrying a folded blanket. The healer followed him, then a carrier with a narrow stretcher. Sella was behind them. Her coat was unfastened and there was flour on one sleeve.

She went straight to her daughter.

“What can you move?”

“The rest of me.”

“Show the healer, not me.”

Neri tried to smile. Sella put a hand against the back of her head and held it there while the healer examined the leg.

Ragna got up too fast. The trees tilted. She caught the wall with both hands and waited until the path stopped shifting.

Kelm stood beside her without touching her.

“I told them where,” he said.

“I can see that.”

He nodded as if she had answered something else.

The healer said Neri must be moved without walking. She would examine the injury properly in her rooms. She was not promising to have Neri back at a bench tomorrow. Neri began to argue about the fitted orders; Sella stopped her with a word Ragna could not hear.

They carried her out. Kelm took one end of the stretcher until the path widened enough for the carrier to relieve him. Ragna walked behind with the papers.

At the healer's door, Sella asked for the receiving counterfoil.

“I'll take it home,” she said. “You stay until she says whether you need looking at too.”

Ragna handed over the original and the collection authority, then reached for the vehicle return copy. She stopped. She had thrown that receipt towards the gate porter.

“The return receipt is at Thorn,” she said. “I let it go at the gate.”

Sella checked what she actually held. “This is enough to list. We won't list what isn't here.”

She went inside to tell Neri before taking the papers away. Ragna stood on the step until she came back.

Mere arrived in the afternoon.

She had hired a light wagon because the cart they had gone to fetch was still at Thorn. She asked to see Neri, then was told the healer had put her to bed. Mere sat in the workshop and listened to Ragna's account instead.

When Ragna finished, Mere said, “So I have paid for a brake repair on a cart I do not possess.”

“I am sorry.”

“Are the tools still with it?”

“Neri's roll. Yes.”

“The one she needs to work with.”

“Yes.”

Mere took off her hat and put it on her knees. “I told you what I was authorising.”

“You did.”

“And you used the cart to bring another person out.”

“We tried. We left it when Neri was hurt.”

“I know. I am not asking you to go back and try the journey again.”

Ragna sat very still. She had expected anger, not a hire bill she could not pay.

“I will put what I saw in writing,” she said. “The guard struck the wheel. The brake pin held. I don't know the final damage.”

“Write the earlier damage too. Don't make me look as though I sent you a good cart.”

“I won't.”

Mere rubbed at the edge of her hat. “I'll claim against the depot. My cart was in their custody when the first damage happened, and at their gate when the second did. If they refuse, I will need another cart while I argue. Somebody pays for that.”

“I haven't money enough now.”

“I had gathered.”

“The fund isn't mine.”

“I am not asking for hungry people's bread.”

Ragna let out a breath she had been holding.

Mere continued, “I will give you the hire bill. Not the whole price of a new cart because I'm angry. The actual hire. We'll see what can be recovered. I am not forgiving the bill by waiting to present it.”

“I understand.”

“I hope you do. People keep saying that before they go away with something.”

Sella came in from the healer with news that Neri would stay there overnight. She must not put weight on the leg. The healer was more concerned about the crush than a simple twist. Sella said this in a level voice, then put a cup down so hard it cracked.

No one spoke until she fetched a cloth for the tea.

Mere put her hat on the table and helped wipe it up. When they had finished, she asked to see her carrier's counterfoil. Sella brought it from the cash book, separate from the ledger fragment. Mere compared the order number with her own copy of the invoice.

“All forty,” she said. Ragna nodded.

“Then I can answer for what was sold, paid for, counted and sent. What happened to the money at the bank is yours to establish.”

She made a copy for her claim and left the original with Sella by agreement. She would ask for it again if the depot disputed her account.

Kelm had been waiting by the back bench. Mere looked at him.

“Hask says he knew your father.”

“He knew him at the old furnace.”

“He has asked whether you need a room.”

Kelm looked at Ragna, then at Sella. “I can't pay for it tonight.”

“He didn't ask about tonight. There is a room at the foundry. You can speak to him about the rest.”

“Does he know what I've done?”

Mere's expression changed. “I know you signed for my bars and they arrived. If there is something else, don't make me discover it from a visitor at the gate.”

Kelm looked towards the door. It was unbarred.

“I took the corner rail out at Blackwater,” he said.

Sella stood beside the broken cup. Her face did not move.

“Say the rest.”

He stayed where he was. “If I tell it, will it be written?”

“If you agree,” Ragna said. “You may hear it read before anyone else takes a copy.”

“And if I want something changed?”

“You change your words. We won't change what we saw.”

He pulled out a stool. It scraped against the floor and caught at a seam. He lifted it over the seam, set it beside the open door and sat.

Dorrik had come at the end of the shift to ask about the twenty bars. Kelm had told him the second cart had been redirected. He had said he could not give him a receiving receipt because he did not have one.

“He said a release wasn't a delivery. I told him to take it up with the office.”

“Did he?” Sella asked.

“He waited. Harrow was there to see the later load. Forty from Mere, going through the lift and on to Thorn. Dorrik waited until I could fetch the first note.”

The load on the narrow cart would pass the lift entrance, but not turn easily between the post and the corner rail. Harrow had said the consignment could not wait for a different vehicle. Kelm had removed the four bolts and lifted the rail out of its sockets.

“Dorrik told me to put it back before I worked the lift.”

“Where was he?”

“Beside the winch. He was looking at the brake. I had asked him to look because the pawl was catching.”

The words came faster now. Ragna wrote what she could and left gaps rather than substitute something tidier.

“Harrow said keep it moving. I said the cart needed room. He said we'd made room, so what were we waiting for? I pulled the control. The near wheel caught the board at the lip. The load shifted to the open side.”

“Towards the missing rail?”

“Yes.”

“What did Dorrik do?”

“He reached for the shaft. I think to keep the cart from turning. I don't know. He didn't tell me what he was going to do.”

Sella gripped the edge of the bench.

“I saw his hand on it. His other hand went towards the brake. Then he was gone.”

Kelm stopped. Ragna's pen made one last stroke.

“Did the cart fall?” she asked.

“No. The wheel caught. I shut the control. We got it straight.”

“And Dorrik?”

“I couldn't see the bottom from where I stood. Brann was below. He called up.”

“What did he say?”

Kelm pressed the heels of his hands into his eyes. “I don't want to put words in his mouth.”

“Then don't.”

“He said Dorrik was there. I knew what he meant.”

Ragna wrote Kelm's words exactly.

Harrow had asked who ordered the rail removed. Kelm had said he had removed it to move the load. Later he was shown an account stating that the barrier was in place and Dorrik had gone into the closed working area after the bell.

“I said that wasn't what happened.”

“Who showed it to you?”

“Pell. Not that night. At Thorn, when I was still waiting to go home.”

“Did you sign it?”

“No.”

“Have you signed any account of the fall?”

“No.”

Mere had sat down without Ragna noticing. She was looking at Kelm's hands.

Sella asked, “Why did you keep the lift moving after he told you not to?”

Kelm looked at her for the first time since he began.

“Because I was told to. Because I thought I could do it. I've done worse turns with heavier loads. That is what I thought.”

“Not because you didn't know.”

“No.”

Sella took a step towards the door, stopped and went to the stove instead. She did not offer him absolution or ask Ragna to make the answer easier.

Ragna read the whole account aloud. Kelm corrected the side on which he had stood. He insisted on the gaps where he had not seen the fall's end. He did not remove the sentence saying he thought he could do it.

He signed each page. Mere and Sella witnessed the signature, each noting that she had not seen the fall. The receiving counterfoil remained a separate sheet.

Before Mere took Kelm to the foundry, Sella said, “Neri has to hear it.”

“She will,” Ragna said.

“All of it.”

“Yes.”

Kelm stood by the door. “I can tell her.”

Sella looked at him for a long time. “She decides that.”

Ragna went to the healer's rooms before they closed for the night. Neri lay in a narrow bed behind a curtain, with one boot on the floor and the other nowhere Ragna could see. Her foot had been wrapped and supported. She looked younger without the apron.

“He came back,” she said.

“He did. With help.”

“Did he say it?”

Ragna set the folded account on the chair beside the bed. “He signed what he remembers. Your mother heard it.”

Neri watched the paper.

“Was my father alone?”

“No.”

“Read that part.”

Ragna read from Dorrik's arrival to the point where Kelm asked him to look at the brake. When she reached the rail, Neri turned her face towards the curtain.

“Not now.”

Ragna stopped. A door shut somewhere along the passage. The healer asked someone to move a bucket before she tripped over it.

“With my mother,” Neri said. “When she comes tomorrow.”

“All right.”

“Can I have water?”

Ragna poured it. Neri took the cup herself, both hands around it, and drank in small sips. Ragna moved the signed account from the chair to her bag so she could sit beside the bed without holding it between them.

After a while Neri asked whether the foundry had sent a bill.

“It will.”

“My little file has a chipped handle. They'll throw it away if they don't know it is mine.”

“I'll put that in the request for the tools.”

Neri nodded. She looked at the empty place where the paper had been and let Ragna stay until the lamp in the passage was lowered.
