# Chapter 7: The Dragon of the Atlantic Toll

Finding the sunlit house in Valladolid felt like conquering half a kingdom, until the family stepped onto the Atlantic Storm-Bridge on the journey home and met the tollkeeper waiting in the thunderclouds.

Between towering sea-squalls stood Lord Double-Count in a cape sewn out of tangled tax forms, gripping the leash of a roaring three-headed beast whose scales rattled like spilled coins.

The **Cross-Border Tax Hydra**.

"Halt!" thundered Lord Double-Count above the gale. "No household leaves the towers early without answering my three heads! Meet **Winter Market Frost**, who bites early travelers when markets dip in year one! Meet **Two-Kingdom Knot**, who tangles two countries' tax laws around your boots! And meet my personal favorite..."

Lord Double-Count snapped his gloved fingers.

Straight from Jorge's satchel popped Garbanzo, eyes spinning dizzy purple spirals. Under the Hydra's spell, the familiar's Double-Counting Bug flared ten times brighter than ever.

"Wonderful news!" Garbanzo shrieked, blasting blinding pink fireworks into the lightning storm. "By sliding two hundred gold coins from the House Vault to the Groceries Drawer, and counting the New Jersey house twice before selling it, we have infinite treasure! Retire tomorrow! Buy a yacht made of ice cream!"

"Stop listening to him!" Marissa shouted over the howling wind as the storm-bridge swayed beneath their boots. "He is mixing locked age-vaults with spendable cash and throwing a parade over transfers!"

Jorge stumbled forward against a blast of cold freezing sleet from Head One. If Garbanzo kept spraying illusion numbers, they would never know whether their plan actually held water or rested on fairy arithmetic. Worse, the Hydra's second head breathed blinding parchment ribbons labeled *Wealth Rules, Pension Bridges, and Currency Swings*, threatening to tangle all three of them into paralysis.

"Marissa, hold the bridge line!" Jorge yelled, dropping straight to one knee on the wet flagstones. "Clara, keep the wind off my hands!"

Clara did not hesitate. She swung her paintbrush-wand overhead in a crisp violet arc, shouting "Starlight!" Instantly a towering, silver-maned unicorn burst out of her pocket spark, hooves planting squarely across the bridge stones, throwing a shimmering dome of warm dry light around Jorge's workbench.

Out of his Tinker's Satchel, Jorge yanked a pocket soldering iron and a fine copper needle. His fingers shook in the storm cold, yet the Pipeline Architect knew this beast inside and out. He popped Garbanzo's top brass hatch, dodged two stray confetti sparks, and pressed the glowing needle right onto the third emerald GPU crystal.

"No double salary," Jorge muttered through clenched teeth, slicing away the noisy duplicate wire. "No transfer illusion. Separate liquid bridge gold from locked vaults. No pretending distant inheritances are cash in hand."

*ZAP!*

A sharp snap of clean emerald fire shot upward. Garbanzo sneezed once, blinked clear amber eyes, and shook soot off his brass ears.

"Report corrected!" Garbanzo piped cleanly, his voice crisp as morning air. "Internal transfers zeroed! Dividends measured honestly! Emergency cash buffer intact!"

"My turn," Marissa called out, lifting the twin 50/50 pouches alongside a sturdy oak buckler stamped **CASH BUFFER & REALISTIC MARGIN**.

When Head One roared a jagged bolt of icy frost across the bridge, Marissa planted her boots wide and deflected the lightning clean off the Emergency Buffer Shield. Not a single spark scorched their groceries or Clara's school bag.

"Now, Clara!" Jorge shouted.

Seated high atop her silver unicorn with wind whipping her braid behind her, six-year-old Clara leaned down and pulled the shiny brass travel compass token straight off the folding Desire Pan beside Marissa's shield. Instead of demanding big voyages every single winter, she tucked that luxury trip safely behind the grocery basket and her school bag, choosing a calm stay-home holiday during stormy years so dinner stayed warm.

Her unicorn's silver horn touched that honest swap, turning it into a blazing golden-silver spark.

Jorge grinned wide through the wind. "She just invented flexible spending!"

*BOOM!*

Golden sparks rained harmlessly across the sea. Lord Double-Count's cape deflated with a comical squeak, tumbling over the parapet into a snowdrift of old receipts.

Even better, the roaring three-headed Hydra did not vanish in cheap smoke. Instead, the monstrous scales folded neatly inward with the crisp snap of leather hinges until nothing remained on the bridge stones except a tidy, sensible brown binder resting beside a brass bookmark.

Jorge picked it up and read the clean stamped letters aloud:

*VERIFY FINAL CROSS-BORDER TREATY, HEALTH COVERAGE, AND TAX FILINGS WITH REAL HUMAN ADVISORS BEFORE CROSSING.*

Marissa snorted, wiping rain from her forehead. "Finally, a monster with decent manners."

Clara slid off her unicorn and tucked the tidy binder into Jorge's satchel. Overhead, the Atlantic storm parted into cold starlight, and for the first time, the family's copper pot back in New Jersey hummed faintly all the way across the sea.
