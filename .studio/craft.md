# Craft standards

Start from the author's desired reading experience. A beautiful line cannot repair
a false claim or a character making a choice solely because the outline needs it.

## Fiction

- Give scenes a lived situation, a desire, resistance, and a consequential change.
  A quiet conversation can turn a relationship; spectacle is not required.
- Let character choices cause consequences. Test plausible alternatives and why
  this person does not choose them. Antagonists need aims and constraints of their own.
- Ground the viewpoint in selective attention and embodied experience. Maintain
  psychic distance intentionally. A secret in the world bible is not POV knowledge.
- Dialogue is action: competing aims, evasion, subtext, silence, power, and changing
  tactics. Distinct voice comes from worldview and syntax, not catchphrase quotas.
- Make setting affect choices, costs, work, culture, and daily life. Explain only
  what the reader needs at the point they need it. Track the cost of extraordinary powers.
- Vary scene shape, sentence rhythm, and emotional movement. Do not force every
  chapter to end in danger, summarize its lesson, or resolve its emotion explicitly.
- Plant payoffs fairly. Distinguish deliberate ambiguity from missing information.
  Pay the book's central promise even when larger series questions remain open.
- Earn emotion through specificity, conflict, restraint, and consequence. Avoid
  interchangeable gestures, decorative metaphors, repeated realizations, and
  exposition disguised as dialogue. Never use a banned-word list as an editor.
- Preserve intentional strangeness, dialect, humor, genre pleasures, and ambiguity.
  Editing should sharpen the author's voice rather than produce house-model prose.

## Nonfiction

- Specify the reader's starting knowledge, the thesis, and what changes for them.
  Structure each section around a useful question, argument, explanation, or action.
- Map substantive claims to sources actually inspected, at the passage/table level.
  Quote accurately and briefly; distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and interpretation.
- Present the strongest relevant counterargument. Do not promote correlation into
  causation or present anecdotes as representative evidence.
- Mark uncertain, disputed, dated, jurisdiction-specific, and inferred statements.
  Preserve denominators, units, sample boundaries, and source qualifications.
- Label composite or invented examples. Do not invent lived experience or interviews.
- Examples, definitions, diagrams, exercises, and summaries should solve a reader
  problem; use only those that help. Validate worked examples and calculations.

## Editing discipline

Read for experience before diagnosis. State where attention, trust, curiosity, or
comprehension changes and cite the text. Offer a few high-impact changes, not a
laundry list. Protect successful passages and distinguish intentional effects from
accidents. One meaningful structural experiment is better than endless abstract debate.

For close imitation requests, translate references into broad craft traits and
develop an original voice. Track permissions for quotes, images, and commissioned
assets in the production brief. A model is not a substitute for a real lived-experience
reader, credentialed expert, permissions owner, or human proofreader when one is needed.
