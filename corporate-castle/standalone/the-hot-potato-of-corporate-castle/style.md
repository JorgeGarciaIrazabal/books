# Style and voice — The Hot Potato of Corporate Castle

## Narrative / explanatory approach
- **POV**: Third-person limited past tense anchored primarily in **Marissa, Royal Inquisitor**, paired throughout with frequent **comedic cutaway scenes** inside **Doctor Lois's**, **Baroness Orla's**, **Lord Sterling's**, **Sir Leo's**, and other castle rooms to heighten satirical dramatic irony (author-confirmed preference, `2026-11-25`).
- **Tense**: Past tense throughout.
- **Register**: Crisp, dry, legal-satirical fantasy. High-fantasy institutional nouns (*High Lords, ER Vault, Deep Magic, Okta Excommunication, Slack-runes*) collide deadpan with painfully real tech-company habits (ring lights, oat-milk cortados, calendar blocks, P&L spreadsheets). Never wink too hard at the joke; the comedy works best when the narrator treats the castle's mechanics as completely real.

## Original voice samples

### Sample 1 — Institutional satire & tonal balance (Prologue, cleaned per `DEC-CC-0005` & `DEC-CC-0006`)
> Corporate Castle was a bustling, glass-walled tech kingdom powered by endless Slack-runes, glowing Zoom lairs, and the quiet hum of The Equity Vault. High above the courtyard rose two very different spires. On one side stood the Tower of Vibes, a pastel fortress draped in cashmere and buzzwords, home to the Courtiers of Optics, the Human Capital partners whose primary craft was enabling and pampering the High Lords who ruled the castle’s four fiefdoms: Engineering, Product, Sales, and Customer Service. Across the moat loomed Liability Tower, the austere stronghold where the Counsel of Liability practiced Deep Magic to insulate the realm from subpoenas and regulatory fire.

*Why it works*: Establishes the world's geometry, caste system, and dry satirical tone in four sentences without em dashes or explanatory throat-clearing.

### Sample 2 — Quiet operational deadpan (Tab 4 Showdown engine)
Marissa does not lecture, vent, or argue with corporate spin. Under pressure, she raises **The Mirror Shield**: stony stillness followed by a single clean operational question that forces the other party to sit inside their own contradiction.

## Dialogue, rhythm, and imagery
- **Marissa**: Unhurried, economical, clinical, zero exclamation marks, devastatingly plain English against walls of jargon.
- **Doctor Lois**: Clipped, frosty, unsentimental on ordinary risk; slips into nervous filler and revisionist Illusion Magic (*"Maybe you already knew. It's okay if you did."*, *"Interim Captain"*) the moment her political cowardice is exposed.
- **Baroness Orla**: Ring-lit, exclamation-rich therapist/HRBP buzzwords on camera; frantic ALL-CAPS Slack-runes off camera.
- **Lord Sterling**: Breezy, ruthless P&L shorthand; speaks in quarters, headcount units, and gold coin.
- **Sarai & Sir Leo**: Grounded, warm, dryly perceptive voices that provide human contrast against the twin towers.

## House style
- **Spelling**: American English.
- **Punctuation (`DEC-CC-0006` — AUTHOR CONFIRMED)**: Strictly **zero em dashes** (`—`), **zero en dashes** (`–`), and **zero `--` stand-ins** in narration, parenthetical asides, or dialogue. Use full stops, commas, colons, or recast sentences for crisp rhythm.
- **Dialogue**: Standard English double quotation marks (`"..."`), one speaker per paragraph.
- **Inline text messages / Slack-runes / E-parchment**: Italics for short written dispatches and internal thoughts.
- **Lexicon formatting**: Keep running prose clean and unbolded (matching the Prologue). Use Title Case for canonical castle terms (*Deep Magic*, *Tower of Vibes*, *Liability Tower*, *ER Watch*, *The Codex*, *Formal Inquiries*).
- **Lexicon normalization** (for any later chapter revisions): Standardize older Tab 3 terms (*ER Dungeon*, *Protected Exile*, *practitioner of Deep Magic*, *Silk-Robed Sellswords of Outside Counsel*) once the author reexamines the back half.
