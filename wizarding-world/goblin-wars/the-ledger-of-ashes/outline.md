# Working outline: The Ledger of Ashes

A provisional causal map, not a finished manuscript. The novel's missing scenes, pacing and ending remain untested.

1. **The audit.** Ragna discovers a hidden entry while officials seize the iron ledger; Ilva passes her a fragment linking a transaction to the site of Dorrik's death.
2. **The household.** Ragna tells Dorrik's family about the missing wages. They need money now, and his widow disputes the official account of his fall. Ragna promises only to check the accounts.
3. **The works.** Tavin and Ragna trace the forty bars through delivery records. An altered tally points to a shipment made after Dorrik was supposed to have left work. A witness demands protection before speaking.
4. **The bargain.** Merrok offers Ragna funds to settle the wage claim if she stops asking questions. Taking the money would feed families, but requires them to sign away further claims. She refuses without speaking for them; the workers decide collectively.
5. **The missing record.** Ilva admits she helped obscure the ledger under pressure, but will not yet name her superior. A wizard clerk with access to Ministry shipment records has reasons to help and reasons to conceal his own complicity.
6. **The demonstration.** Workers demand the ledger and wages. A failed intervention by officials turns a peaceful gathering violent. Tavin helps people escape but is arrested. Ragna's earlier caution has left the organisers without copies they can use.
7. **The proof.** Ragna, Dorrik's family, Ilva and the clerk assemble independent evidence. They learn Merrok arranged the diversion to conceal a politically useful stockpile and preserve his own office. His choices, including any threat to him, require further development.
8. **The choice.** Recovering the ledger intact would protect private debts but allow the evidence to be sealed. Ragna gives the affected families the choice. They authorise a limited public accounting; the original is damaged in the escape and sensitive details are protected where possible.
9. **The reckoning.** The public evidence forces a payment mechanism and exposes those responsible for the immediate theft. Tavin is freed at a cost yet to be designed. Dorrik's death receives an answer grounded in evidence, not an unsupported assumption. The authorities' response to the disclosure sparks the next phase of conflict. Ragna has won the local case but cannot control the consequences.

Before drafting beyond the opening: develop the mechanism of the fraud, the financial remedy, Dorrik's actual fate, and the choices of the affected families; avoid making events hinge on convenient magic.
