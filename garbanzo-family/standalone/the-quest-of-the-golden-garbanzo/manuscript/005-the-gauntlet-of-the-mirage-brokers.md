# Chapter 5: The Gauntlet of the Mirage Brokers

Even with magic running inside their luggage, the Atlantic Sky-Portal still managed a forty-minute Cloud-Troll delay over Newark before dropping the Garbanzo family onto the sunlit tarmac of Madrid.

By noon they stood in a marble map-hall tucked behind Barajas, where the Phantom of the Unfinished Urbanización waited in a suspiciously shiny blue suit and crocodile loafers.

"Welcome, brave travelers!" the Phantom boomed, snapping his fingers so three floating watercolor castles bloomed in the air like soap bubbles. "Why toil over geometry when you can step straight into paradise? Behold, bargains so cheap your copper kettle will explode!"

"My familiar already handled the exploding part back in New Jersey," Jorge said, pulling his brass Cadastral Spectacles out of his jacket. "Show us the ground, not the paint."

First, the Phantom whisked their viewing mirror south toward the Sun-Baked Wastes of Murcia. From a distance, white villas glittered beside turquoise pools for half the price of a New Jersey attic. Clara gasped at the pools. Jorge leaned closer.

Then Marissa raised the Compass of Living Streets.

Its rose-gold needle shuddered, turned upside down, and dropped flat onto the glass with a dull metallic *clunk*.

"Look past the pool edge," Marissa said quietly, squinting at the barren scrub beyond the gates. "Zero tree-lined sidewalks. Half the shuttered townhouses belong to August tourists who vanish by October. Where would I buy fresh bread on a Tuesday morning? Where would Clara find friends after school?"

The Murcia scroll curled up at the edges and slipped harmlessly into the trash bin.

"Ah, picky customers!" the Phantom chuckled, sweeping his cloak north toward the cool pine ridges of the Sierra de Guadarrama. "Very well! Behold Collado Mediano! Mountain air! Granite peaks! Near Madrid!"

For a long second, all three stared fondly. The pines smelled of resin and crisp mountain morning. Families walked down stone lanes carrying school backpacks. Even Marissa's compass gave an approving hum.

Then Jorge unlatched the folding Brass Scale from his travel coat and set it down, touching the parchment to its pan.

*CLANG!*

Out of thin air fell a gleaming boulder labeled **FOUR HUNDRED THOUSAND GOLD**, trailed by a smaller, meaner stone marked **WINTER ROOF REPAIRS AND DATED PLUMBING**. The Effort Pan banged flat against the marble floor.

"Too steep," Jorge sighed, rubbing his temples. "Unless we want to spend five more years bowing to the Amber Terminal Citadel just to own drafty mountain rafters."

The Phantom's teeth flashed wide and shark-bright. "Then I have the ultimate secret! A vacant plot right around the corner! Almost free! Build a modern single-story dream house overnight!"

He unfurled a glowing parchment sketch of a sprawling estate surrounded by rolling emerald lawns, a basketball half-court, and a gleaming garage large enough for two sleighs.

Jorge slid his Cadastral Spectacles onto the bridge of his nose and snapped the side latches shut.

Instant blue survey lines flared across the Phantom's pretty painting, melting the watercolor away like sugar in hot tea.

Beneath the fake lawn lay a crooked, pinched four-sided trapezoid measuring barely two hundred fifty-one square meters, wider at the back than the curved street in front, with one corner of the painted garage slicing three meters straight through a neighbor's cypress hedge. Worse, thick mud bubbled softly beneath the grass: cursed *urbanizable* swamp instead of genuine serviced *urbano consolidado* ground, with zero sewer pipes, zero power conduits, and three years of municipal dragon-stamps standing between mud and brick.

"Whoa," Clara whispered, leaning over Jorge's shoulder. "His math broke worse than Garbanzo's."

"Hey!" squeaked Garbanzo from inside Jorge's coat pocket. "My math is merely overly optimistic! That gentleman is hiding a swamp!"

The Phantom backed away, his blue suit flickering like a bad lamp. "Nonsense! Only minor paperwork! Just put down a little gold deposit today and our construction elves will handle everything!"

Jorge folded his arms. He planted both sneakers squarely on the marble tiles and spoke the three Spanish words every mirage broker dreaded:

"Show us the *entrega de llaves*."

"The... the exact key-handover date?" the Phantom stammered, sweating translucent mist. "Well, as soon as the moon aligns, perhaps eighteen months, or thirty, give or take an aqueduct..."

A sharp crack echoed through the hall. With no real build permit and no honest handover date to hold it together, the glossy illusion scroll burst into dry yellow autumn leaves. The Phantom shrieked once like a deflated accordion and blew out through the skylight in a puff of lukewarm brochure ink.

Marissa tucked her compass carefully back into her coat and nudged Clara's hat straight. "Mountains too pricey, cheap deserts too lonely, and magic plots full of mud. Any bright ideas, spectacles?"

Jorge tapped his glass lenses, remembering a glowing silver paw print pressed into the family map back in New Jersey.

"North across the mountains," Jorge said, smiling for the first time since Monday. "Down into the Valley of Valladolid."
