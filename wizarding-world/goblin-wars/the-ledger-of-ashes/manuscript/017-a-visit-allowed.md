# Chapter Seventeen: A Visit Allowed

The note from Blackwater arrived with a basket of turnips.

Hedra read it in the passage outside the counting house. She held the basket on one hip and the paper close to her face. When she had finished, she gave the note to Ragna without speaking.

Tavin was permitted one visitor that afternoon. The visitor was to present the note at the gate and submit to a search. No papers were to be carried inside. The signature was Pell's.

“He asked for me?” Hedra said.

“It doesn't say.”

“No. It doesn't.”

She took the note back and folded it. Ragna watched her thumb press the fold flat. The canvas mending had left a small black stain beside her nail.

“Are you going?”

“Yes. But I won't go as far as the gate and be told it was for someone else.”

Ragna went with her to the Ministry court. The clerk who had accepted the petition took the note and asked them to wait. Behind the door's green glass panel, his shadow paused beside a taller one. He returned alone.

“The visit is for Miss Ragna.”

Hedra kept her hands on the basket. “Why?”

“I don't have that information.”

“Is my son ill?”

“I don't have that information either.”

The clerk looked at the turnips as if he expected them to help. Hedra set the basket on his counter.

“Watch these while I take a chair,” she said. “They're for the healer, and I don't want someone deciding they belong to the inquiry.”

She sat in the only chair. The clerk moved the basket out of the way of his papers. Ragna stood beside her, angry at the invitation and more angry that she meant to accept it.

“I'll tell you everything he lets me see,” she said.

“Tell me what you see. He may not let you do anything.”

Hedra stayed in the chair while Ragna arranged the cart. When the driver came, Hedra took the basket and asked him to stop at the healer's. If Sella was still at Neri's bedside, there was no use delivering their turnips to an empty workshop.

They rode together to the end of the lane. Hedra got down there. She did not kiss Ragna or wish her luck. She checked the position of the sun, told the driver she would wait in the shelter by the ruined wall, and paid half the fare.

At the gate Pell searched Ragna's coat and pouch. He kept her penknife, her pencil and the paper in which Sella had wrapped two pieces of cheese. He left her the cheese after she showed him she could carry it in her hand.

“Is that for him?” he asked.

“If you allow it.”

“I'll decide.”

He took her into the yard. The lift was out of use. A board lay over part of the opening, and the beam Neri had seen against the wall was gone. Ragna wanted to look for the bolt holes. Pell had her by the elbow before she had turned her head properly.

“Inside.”

He led her through a door beside the boiler shed. There were three rooms in the short passage. Tavin sat at a table in the middle one, with his hands free and a cup in front of him. The cup was empty. When he saw her, he stood so quickly that his chair toppled.

“Mother?”

“Outside the gate. She wasn't allowed in.”

He looked past Ragna at Pell, then righted the chair. There was a mark on his wrist where the binding had cut him. Ragna could see a strip of clean linen beneath his sleeve.

“Are you hurt?”

“Not much.” He touched the linen. “They looked at it.”

“Have you seen Venn?”

“Through the door. Yesterday. He asked if his aunt knew.”

“She does. And Ruth. Your mother has a receipt for your clothes.”

“I got them.” He looked down at the mended coat. “I thought she would send the other one.”

She told him that Kelm had been held in a locked shed at Thorn and had left with Ragna and Neri. She did not try to give him Kelm's whole account while Pell stood within hearing.

Ragna put the cheese on the table. Pell removed one piece and broke the other open with his thumb. He put the broken half back.

“Five minutes,” he said.

He left the door open and stood in the passage. Tavin waited until his steps had moved away from the threshold.

“I didn't ask for you,” he said.

“I know.”

“Did they tell you why?”

“No.”

He sat, and she took the other chair. A dark ring marked the table, its grain raised by damp. Tavin traced it once with a fingertip.

“They keep asking who told us to put the papers up.”

“What have you said?”

“That I did it.”

“Do they believe you?”

“They want a name they can put above mine.” He broke the cheese into two pieces. “I told them I used the ladder at the lower market. Pell asked who lent it to me. I said I didn't ask. It was leaning against the wall.”

Ragna nearly warned him against saying even that. But he had spent longer with their questions than she had.

“They say Fenn threw something,” he said.

“I saw a stool fall. I didn't see the whole doorway.”

“They told me he had a knife.”

“I saw no knife.”

“Did anyone?”

“Dask remembers a stool being thrown, but he isn't certain when. His wife didn't agree to his name being used.”

Tavin nodded. “Then don't use it.”

The door moved. Harrow stood behind Pell, carrying the black case. He did not sit.

“Mr Tavin has given us a very incomplete account,” he said. “I hope you can help him understand the difficulty.”

“I came to see him.”

“And you have.” Harrow put the case on the table. “The record Mr Venn retained is here. We have other materials belonging to the Ministry. One of them appears to have left Thorn with you.”

Ragna felt Tavin look at her. She kept her attention on Harrow.

“Mere's carrier's counterfoil was given to me by the carrier responsible for it.”

“Mr Kelm was not free to dispose of it.”

“Was he free to leave?”

“He was assisting an inquiry.”

“He asked to leave three times.”

Harrow opened the case. Venn's dispatch lay inside, protected by a sheet of blotting paper. Beside it was a second document bearing the Ministry seal. Harrow left it face down.

“I can release Mr Tavin today,” he said. “I can also permit Mr Venn to return to his work, provided he gives an undertaking to submit his papers to the inquiry. In return, the counterfoil comes back to Thorn. Miss Ilva withdraws her statement while the examination is conducted. There will be no public circulation of the accounts.”

Tavin pushed his chair away from the table. “You told me I was here for obstruction.”

“You are. The circumstances can be reconsidered.”

“Are you offering me a bargain or telling her what to do?”

Harrow turned the sealed paper over. It was a release form with spaces for two names. Ragna could see the word undertaking at the bottom. The pen beside it had a silver band at the grip.

“Mr Venn altered an official record,” Harrow said. “You interfered with officers. I am prepared to take a practical view.”

“Show me the other undertaking,” Ragna said.

“It will be prepared when you agree.”

“I want to know what Ilva must withdraw.”

“Her unverified allegations.”

“She certified the advance herself. Two partners have the book.”

“They may examine it privately.”

“And the workers?”

“Will be paid what is established as due.”

Tavin picked up the torn cheese, put it down again and wiped his fingers on the table.

“If she gives it to you,” he said, “does Kelm go back to the shed?”

“Mr Kelm is required to complete his account.”

“That isn't an answer.”

Harrow looked at Ragna. “You see the problem.”

“Yes,” she said. “I do.”

She thought of Hedra waiting by the wall and of the coat she had packed. She thought of Neri asking for the part of Kelm's statement she could bear to hear, then asking for water. Sella held the counterfoil for the foundry's claim. Ilva had made her own statement. Ragna could surrender neither.

“I can't give you their papers,” she said.

“You can bring them here.”

“I can ask them. I cannot agree for them.”

Tavin leaned forward. “I won't sign it.”

Harrow's expression changed very little. “You have not read it.”

“I can read the word undertaking. I can hear what you're asking her to do.”

“You could be home this evening.”

“I want to go home.” Tavin's voice rose, then he pressed his hands between his knees. “I want my mother to stop waiting by gates. But if you're holding me until she gives you a paper that isn't hers, write that down too.”

Pell took a step into the room. Harrow closed the case.

“Think about it,” he told Ragna. “The offer will not improve.”

He went out. Pell followed him, leaving the door open again. Tavin remained seated. Ragna could see that his hands were trembling. He put them flat on the table until they stopped.

“I shouldn't have put the papers up early,” he said.

She had imagined him saying it many times. It gave her no pleasure now.

“You should have asked the room to change its decision.”

“I thought they wouldn't.”

“They might not have.”

He looked at the empty cup. “I don't know if that brought him to the door.”

“Neither do I.”

He picked up the cheese again. This time he ate a little.

“Mother will ask if I'm eating.”

“I'll tell her I saw you eat cheese.”

“She'll say it isn't a meal.”

“She'll be right.”

Pell returned before either of them had anything else to say. He held out his hand for the remaining cheese and seemed disappointed when Tavin put it in his mouth instead.

Ragna stood. Tavin did not ask her to stay. At the door he said, “Tell her about the coat.”

“What about it?”

“That I know why she sent this one.”

Outside, the light had shifted across the yard. The gatekeeper gave Ragna her pencil and knife. He did not return the paper that had wrapped the cheese.

Hedra was waiting by the ruined wall. She stood as soon as the cart appeared. Ragna got down before the driver had stopped.

“He's wearing the coat,” she said. “He has a bandage on one wrist. He says they looked at it. I saw him eat cheese.”

Hedra's mouth opened, then closed. She sat down on the low wall and put both hands over her face. Ragna waited until she could speak.

“What did he say about the coat?”

“He knows why you sent it.”

“He always liked the other one better.”

“What is it about this one?”

Hedra pulled her hands away. “The lining. I put a pocket in it when he was small. He used to hide things there when he thought I wasn't looking.”

“What things?”

“Anything he didn't want his sister to find. Buttons. A dead beetle once.” She looked up. “Did you see the pocket?”

“No.”

“Good. Perhaps they didn't either.”

She got into the cart. Ragna sat beside her and told her about Harrow's offer, including what she had not been able to read. Hedra listened without interrupting.

“It's his decision about his name,” she said when Ragna finished. “Ilva's about hers. And Venn ought to hear what's being offered for him.”

“I couldn't speak to him.”

“Then we keep asking.”

They passed the checkpoint on the road home. The notice about metal had been replaced by a longer one. A carrier stood reading it while the guard waited, one hand on his wand. Ragna could not see the words from the cart. She saw the carrier fold his own delivery order and put it back in his pocket before turning away.
