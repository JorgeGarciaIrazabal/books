# Chapter Seven: The Source Column

Ilva came at seven, before the workshop door had been unbarred. Neri let her wait in the passage while she finished setting a pin in the vice.

Ragna was already inside. Sella had made tea but poured only three cups. The fourth stood empty on the shelf. It was not Dorrik's cup, and Ragna was grateful for that small absence of ceremony.

When Neri opened the door, Ilva stepped over the sill without catching her coat. She noticed the warning bell and looked away from it. Under her arm she carried a flat case wrapped in oilcloth.

“Before you begin,” Sella said, “Neri hears all of it.”

Ilva sat. “I assumed she would.”

“Good. We can agree on something.”

The case contained a narrow control book and a folded authority for an advance. Ilva laid them apart rather than one on top of the other.

“This is my examiner's record,” she said, touching the book. “It records a payment from the pooled workshop reserve. The authority is a copy. My endorsement on it is original.”

Ragna looked at the signatures. Merrok had signed for the advance; Ilva had certified that the authorised amount agreed with the payment entry. The sum was the price of forty bars, at the same rate as the household charge.

Sella stared at the amount. “Again?”

“Not the same iron,” Ragna said. “Not necessarily.”

Ilva shook her head. “A separate purchase. The order number is here.”

Ragna took out the copy made in Venn's aunt's shop. The references agreed.

“And the Ministry was the buyer?”

“The intended recipient.”

“Who was supposed to pay the reserve back?”

“The Ministry.”

“Then why did the money come from us?” Sella asked.

Ilva kept her eyes on the authority. “It was described as short-term finance. Merrok had an undertaking that the Ministry would settle when its stores allocation was released. He said refusing would bring a wider examination of the bank's metal business.”

“By Harrow?”

“Yes.”

“And you believed him.”

“I believed he could make the examination happen.”

Sella pushed the tea away. “So you gave him our money.”

“I certified the advance. I did not authorise it.”

“That distinction mattered to you?”

“It mattered to my duties.”

“What do your duties call the money?”

For a moment Ilva did not answer. The stove clicked as its little stone cooled.

“Reserve funds belonging to the contributing workshops,” she said.

Neri turned from the bench. “Which workshops agreed?”

“None was asked individually.”

“Which workshops agreed?”

“None.”

Sella gripped the cup in both hands, her knuckles pale. Yesterday it had been Venn explaining why she had not been told.

Ragna opened the control book. A red entry recorded the advance and the intended debtor. A later line transferred its recovery to the iron ledger. Beneath it Ilva had put a small tick.

“You examined the transfer.”

“Yes.”

“To our account?” Sella asked.

Ilva looked at her. “To the ledger holding the workshop accounts.”

“Mine has forty bars charged twice,” Ragna said. “Was the second charge the transfer?”

“That is what Merrok instructed.”

“Did you enter it?”

“No.”

“Did you see it after it was entered?”

“Yes.”

“And you left it.”

“I was told it would be reversed when the Ministry paid.”

The control entry named the Ministry as debtor. Ilva's tick confirmed she had checked the transfer; it did not show who had entered the charge under Dorrik's name. For that, they had Ilva's testimony and a ledger they could no longer inspect.

“What did you cover?” she asked.

“The reconciliation showing the advance beside the workshop posting.”

“Why?”

“Merrok said if Harrow saw a Ministry debt recorded against the reserve, he would widen the seizure. He told me the concealed page would protect the other books.”

“Did you expect the wages to be stopped?”

“I expected the totals to be copied.”

Ragna remembered standing with the knife between the pages, asking for two hours. Ilva had been in the room when Harrow granted one. He had taken even that away.

“You expected me to make a clean copy of the false charge.”

Ilva closed her eyes, then opened them.

“Yes.”

Neri picked up her hammer and set it down again. Its little knock sounded louder than it should have.

Ragna braced her hands on either side of the control book and wanted to shut it on Ilva's fingers.

Sella spoke very evenly. “Was Dorrik working on this purchase?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you know he was dead when you covered the page?”

“No. I heard before we went to the counting room.”

“And went anyway.”

“Yes.”

The kettle began its small ticking complaint. Ragna took it off the stove and set it on the hearth. She stayed there until her hands were steady enough for the cups.

“We have three quantities,” she said. “Twenty bars you say arrived here. Forty released from Blackwater under the first note. Another forty paid for out of the reserve.”

Neri nodded.

“The household was charged for forty, then forty again. We have a dispatch copy saying half the first release was redirected. We haven't seen the depot's receipt. We have a purchase authority and the control entry for a separate advance. We haven't seen the supplier's account or the receipt for those bars either.”

“You don't believe her?” Sella asked.

“Believing her is not enough to make the bank pay.”

Ilva's mouth tightened. Ragna let it.

“If both deliveries went where the dispatches say, the Ministry has twenty from the first and forty from the second. Sixty. You have twenty. Your account has eighty.”

Neri put her hand on the edge of the open control book.

“I understand the sum.”

“I know. I need to know which parts we can prove.”

Ilva turned the copy of the authority. “The supplier's bill is held with the payment voucher in the central cash file. This shows the authority, not final receipt. I can ask for the voucher.”

“Will they give it to you?”

“Not if Merrok has already told them not to.”

“Then ask in writing.”

Ilva looked down at her book. “He will know.”

“He knows we went to Blackwater,” Neri said. “You told him where to find our money.”

Ilva looked to Ragna. Ragna said nothing.

They copied the advance authority and the relevant lines of the control book. Sella insisted on including the dates of the later transfer and Ilva's examining tick. Ilva initialled the copies and wrote a statement of what she had seen and certified. She kept the control book; the others kept copies.

Before Ilva put the book away, Ragna asked about the reserve partners.

“Can they authorise payment from another reserve without the ledger?”

“They can authorise an advance against a liability they are prepared to recognise.”

“Then Merrok was wrong to say they couldn't.”

“He said they wouldn't.”

Ragna remembered the words exactly. Three partners, two approached, no underlying record. She disliked Merrok the more for having left himself room to be accurate.

“The reconstructed wages,” she said. “The withdrawal slips, the household tallies, workers willing to acknowledge what they've already received. They could be examined separately.”

“Yes.”

“And paid, with disputed materials held out until examined.”

Ilva looked at Sella. “It would mean someone standing behind the reconstructed balances.”

“I will stand behind mine,” Sella said. “But they don't get to pay only the difference on the iron. There are wages owing on top of that.”

“Exactly,” Ragna said. “The whole verified balance after the materials you actually received. The claim against the Ministry belongs elsewhere.”

For the first time since she arrived, Ilva took her own pen out. She wrote the proposal on a clean sheet and gave it to Ragna to correct.

“You should send it to Issa,” she said. “She is one of the partners. She is staying at the bank house for the quarter inspection. Halrik is expected tomorrow. You need both names.”

“Will you bring it?”

“I can put it before her. I cannot make her read it.”

“You can tell her why it matters.”

Ilva folded the statement she had signed. “She'll ask why I did not tell her earlier.”

Sella lifted her cup at last. “You appear to have the answer.”

After Ilva left, Neri shut the door and stood with her hand on the latch. Ragna waited while Sella rinsed the cups. The work seemed to require all her attention.

“I was angry with you for entering forty,” she said at last.

“You had reason.”

“You had a signed note.”

“I still didn't ask what the signature meant.”

Neri turned. “Neither did he.”

Sella set the cups upside down. She did not answer for her husband.

A cart arrived for the school hinges before noon. The carrier checked each bundle against the bill and gave Sella a purse with the payment. She counted it at the bench while he waited. It was less than the full price. The school had agreed to pay early in return for a discount, and she had accepted.

Neri said something under her breath.

“It pays Hirk,” Sella said. “It buys the next sand. We finish the other order.”

“And pays us less for being poor.”

“Yes,” her mother said. “Today it does.”

She put the supplier's money in one envelope and the wages in another. Ragna watched her pay the fitter who had stayed and write down what was still owed. There was no ledger from the bank, no red seal and no senior examiner. The man counted his coins and went back to the bench.

At the counting house that afternoon, Tavin brought the first three reconstructed balances for Ragna to check. Each had a household signature and the witnesses' names. One person had refused to sign because he was not sure about a small advance taken in the winter. Tavin had set the whole balance aside.

“Leave the doubtful sum aside,” Ragna said. “Not everything he earned.”

“He didn't agree to the total.”

“He agreed to the rest?”

“Yes.”

“Then write what he agrees to. We have been letting one doubtful number hold every other number prisoner.”

Tavin looked at her for a moment, then drew a line through the provisional heading and wrote two: agreed and disputed.

Edda brought her own tally near the end of the afternoon. The broken lantern was still hanging from her wrist.

“I took the bank money,” she said before Ragna could offer a chair.

“All right.”

“It isn't all right. It is what I did.”

She put the receipt on the desk. This one had the addition made at the meeting about the payment not being recovered. Beneath her signature, the clerk had drawn a line through the space left for dependants.

“He says I can't add my aunt now.”

“Does she live with you?”

“Since the cold weather. Her rooms have water in the wall. He says she wasn't registered before the offer.”

Ragna read the schedule. Dependants were calculated from the register, just as Merrok had said. It did not mean Edda's aunt had stopped eating.

“I can write to the clerk,” she said.

“He told me you'd say that.”

Ragna put the pen down. “What would help tonight?”

“Coal. I have food.”

“Tell Vessa. She holds that list with Sella and Fenn.”

“I signed his form.”

“The fund didn't ask you not to.”

Edda sat at last. She unclipped the lantern and stood it beside the tally. In the counting room's steady lamplight, its dented glass looked grey.

“He said the money was for the people on the register. I kept thinking I should have put her name down in the winter.”

“He did not ask what you were thinking. He asked who was on the register.”

Ragna put a clean sheet beside the receipt, copied the stated condition, and wrote a request for review. Before Edda left, she asked if she wanted a copy for the petition. Edda said yes to the rejected addition, no to the household's other figures. Ragna put only the first sheet in the public bundle.

They worked until the lamps burned brighter than the light from the passage. When Fenn arrived to count the fund, Ragna showed him Ilva's statement. She had left Venn's statement in a separate bundle until he agreed to its use. Fenn read slowly, touching the line of figures with his smallest finger.

“How many other accounts?” he asked.

“We don't know.”

“Then put that on the copy.”

She did. He put two more sickles into the fund, which now held enough for tomorrow's bread and not tomorrow's coal.

Ragna closed the lid over the coins. At the bottom of the reserve request, beneath her description of the agreed balances, she wrote her own name. She blotted the signature before closing the request.
