# Nell and the Pocket-Sized Storm

# Chapter 1: Weather in a Boot

## Page 1

Nell's left boot said, "Excuse me."

Nell stopped putting on her right boot.

"Grandma?"

"In the kitchen," called Grandma.

Nell peered into the left boot. Two small eyes peered back.

"There is a pebble in here," said a cross little voice. "And I appear to be sitting on it."

Nell tipped the boot upside down.

A pebble fell out.

Then the weather fell out.

## Page 2

It was a cloud the size of a bread roll. It had a gray belly, fluffy eyebrows, and a piece of yellow sock stuck to its bottom.

Nell pulled off the sock.

"Thank you," said the cloud. "It was itching."

"You're a cloud."

"A pocket storm, actually. My name is Grumble."

Nell held out her hand. Grumble settled on her palm.

Her sleeve immediately became damp.

## Page 3

"Do you live in boots?" Nell asked.

"Only by accident. I smelled toast, followed it down a chimney, and got lost."

"That happens to me with cake. Except for the chimney."

Grumble stood on tiptoe. At least, his bottom grew two little points.

Through the open window, Nell could see a line of clouds beyond the hill.

"My family," he said. "They'll cross the hills at sunset. I have to catch them."

## Page 4

Nell carried him into the kitchen.

Grandma looked up from buttering toast.

"Well," she said. "I thought that boot was unusually rainy."

Grumble bowed. Three drops landed in the butter.

"Sorry."

Grandma fetched a saucer.

"All guests get something to sit on."

Nell wanted to ask a hundred questions. Did clouds have birthdays? Did lightning tickle?

But Grumble was watching the window.

"We'll help you get home," she said.

## Page 5

In the garden, Nell lifted him over her head.

"Off you go!"

Grumble floated down and landed on her nose.

"Cold," said Nell.

"Sorry," said Grumble.

He peeled himself off.

"Pocket storms need a proper breeze to rise high. It's too still down here."

Above the houses, a weather vane spun. Its metal rooster pointed toward the hills.

Nell looked from the rooster to the shed.

She had an idea.
