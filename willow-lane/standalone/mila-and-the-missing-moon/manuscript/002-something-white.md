# 2. Something White

The moon was not in the reading room. It was not in the storeroom, either, though Mila found enough spare paper to make a second moon.

There wasn't time for that. The first one had taken two whole afternoons.

Dad carried his bag of lights and stars to the back door. Noah tucked his ruler into his pocket and held out the notebook.

“You keep it for now,” he told Mila.

She held it open as they stepped into the yard.

The library's back step was dark gray. Across it ran several pale lines.

Mila stopped so suddenly that Dad nearly bumped into her.

“Look.”

One line curved around the corner of the step. Another ran beside it. On the paving stones below, the lines spread farther apart.

“Wheels?” Noah said.

Mila followed them with her eyes. Something with wheels had come through this door. Something that could carry a very big box.

“Nobody step on them,” Mila said.

Dad lifted one foot and put it down somewhere else.

Noah crouched beside the step. Mila crouched too. The white stuff was dry at the edges, thicker in the middle. In one line, a little piece was missing. Farther along, there was another gap.

“Like a dotted line,” Noah said.

“A mostly undotted line.”

He grinned. Then he took his ruler from his pocket and laid it beside a gap.

Mila looked across the yard. At the far end, a path split around a low bed of mint. The left branch went through a wooden gate toward the community garden. The right branch went past the bakery's back wall.

Someone had put folding tables near the mint. Bright paper scraps trembled in a breeze.

Noah took back his notebook and began drawing the doorway, the tables, and the path.

“We need clues,” Mila said. “Not a picture.”

He kept drawing.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

The bakery door opened.

Bea backed out with a tall stack of metal trays. White dust covered the front of her apron. There was a pale handprint on one pocket.

Mila looked from the apron to the lines on the ground.

“Flour,” she whispered.

“Maybe,” said Noah.

Bea turned, saw them, and smiled. “How's our moon coming along?”

Mila didn't answer at once.

“We've lost it,” Dad said. “We're checking the yard.”

“Oh, no.” Bea shifted the trays against her hip. “I brought the biscuit trays into the library after lunch. The table by the window was clear, so I left them there.”

“The moon table?” Mila asked.

“If that's where it was, yes.”

Bea had been in the library. Bea had wheels for moving heavy things. Bea was covered in white powder.

Mila felt a small, hot fizz inside her chest.

Perhaps this was what finding the answer felt like.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

“We should look at her cart,” Mila whispered to Noah.

“And compare the white stuff,” he whispered back.

“It's flour. She works in a bakery.”

“But we haven't touched it yet.”

Dad was lifting the trays from Bea's arms. She rubbed her shoulder and thanked him.

Mila remembered Bea laughing yesterday when the moon frame rolled against a chair. She had called it a great clumsy pancake.

Perhaps she had wanted the table for her biscuits. Perhaps she had put the box outside and someone else had taken it. That would explain everything.

Well, most things.

Mila needed to ask before she lost the thought.

She shut the notebook over Noah's unfinished drawing and held it against her chest.

“Come on,” she said.

Noah stood up. “I still want to measure—”

But Mila was already crossing the yard. Bea stood by the bakery door, wiping her dusty hands down her apron.

Mila had her question ready.
