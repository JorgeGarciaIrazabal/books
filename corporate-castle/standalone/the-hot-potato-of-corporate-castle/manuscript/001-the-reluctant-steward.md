# Chapter One: The Reluctant Steward

Doctor Lois did not arrive as a champion. She treated the ER Watch like an inconvenient, inherited cost center someone had shoved onto her org chart overnight. Draped in a razor-sharp charcoal blazer with a glowing red fountain pen tucked behind her ear, she presided over her new wards from behind towering shelves of ironbound Spellbooks of Deep Magic, offering zero mentorship, zero advocacy, and the unmistakable posture of an aristocrat waiting for someone else to come collect the spare children.

Even so, although Marissa genuinely missed Lexi, moving to Liability Tower was not actually so bad. Professionally speaking, Marissa had no issues working closely with Doctor Lois and Sir Leo, Counsel of Liability. In fact, she did not mind it at all. She was a licensed practitioner of Deep Magic with a heavily tabbed set of Spellbooks of her own, and on a crisp Tuesday morning in October, a single thirty-minute Zoom lair reinforced why she preferred Lois's frostbite to the pastel circus across the moat.

The emergency call had been summoned with twelve minutes' notice by Shay, Sales Courtier of Optics, who floated onto the screen bathed in flattering ring light, clutching a lavender-scented copy of The Grimoire of Optics bound in ivory linen and dotted with tiny sparkle emojis. Behind her gleamed a miniature brass Quota Gong on a white credenza and a pastel neon sign reading *Always Be Aligning*.

"Thank you so much for jumping on urgently, team," Shay said breathlessly, already weaving a shimmering haze of Buzzword Hypnosis across the Zoom lair. "We have a five-alarm situation in Sales. One of our Warlords is deeply, deeply concerned and needs an immediate Okta Excommunication before Friday's end-of-quarter pipeline review. She discovered that one of her reps has been going completely rogue and giving away free trials like candy for weeks, even though the entire floor was told it was outlawed! Honestly, it's practically High Treason against the forecast. With Q3 pipeline tracking a touch soft, we really need to nip this in the bud today to protect the business and safeguard our high-octane revenue culture."

The air in the lair smelled aggressively of synthetic lavender. Across Corporate Castle, that sort of Illusion Magic could hypnotize an entire committee into nodding along. In a Zoom lair with the Royal Inquisitor and the Grand Archivist of Liability, it evaporated on contact.

Marissa's True-Sight needed no translation for a quarter "tracking a touch soft." Friday's pipeline review was closing in, the ledger was bleeding red, and somewhere upstairs a Sales Warlord was hunting for a sacrificial lamb.

"Was this new policy ever committed to a scroll?" Marissa asked lightly.

Shay's bright smile held steady, though the ring light caught a tiny bead of sweat along her hairline. "Well, it was shared verbally on the Monday stand-up! Totally clear alignment across the room."

"What about an E-parchment?" Marissa asked. "A follow-up Slack-rune?"

Shay hesitated for half a beat, her smile widening to compensate. "Not that I'm aware of, no, because the energy in the room was already so aligned!"

"What day was that announced?" Marissa asked.

"Three Mondays ago," Shay chirped, already launching back into her pitch. "Super crisp rollout, zero pushback, and then weeks later the Warlord catches him still handing out free trials to every prospect on his calendar. Totally undermines the whole floor's accountability."

While Shay kept talking, Marissa opened the rep's file on her second monitor and clicked cleanly through his profile: not a single Writ of Warning on record, and a bright blue sick-day block sitting right across that Monday on his time-off log. Twelve minutes' notice for a five-alarm emergency, and nobody in the Tower of Vibes had bothered to click a single tab. Marissa rubbed her face and waited for Shay to draw a breath.

"He has zero prior warnings, the policy change was never committed to ink, and he was out sick the day it was announced," Marissa announced dryly. "You cannot excom a rep for breaking a rule nobody ever told him existed."

Shay's smile faltered for half a second before snapping right back on. "Totally hear that, Marissa! Love the thoroughness. Doctor Lois, just thinking out loud from a risk-tolerance lens, what if the business is comfortable absorbing a little ambiguity here so the Warlord can move swiftly?"

In the corner square, Doctor Lois did not sigh, negotiate, or reach for a single buzzword. She tapped her glowing red fountain pen once against her mahogany desk.

"No," Lois said flatly. "He stays. Tell the Warlord to update her playbook and try sending an E-parchment next time. Good day, Shay."

Lois clicked out of the lair without waiting for a goodbye. Shay blinked at the empty square beneath *Always Be Aligning*, forced her smile back into place, chirped, "Love the clarity! Fully aligned on the hard no!" and vanished in a faint puff of lavender.

---

Three days later, two minutes before her Friday call, Marissa sat at her folding table in the cramped, unfunded alcove Liability Tower had spared beside the archive crates, scanning her witness notes.

A Warlord in Engineering had made a lewd comment to a female engineer. The witnesses had lined up cleanly, the record was airtight, and Marissa was ready to give the engineer her due justice and close the scroll before the weekend.

There was only one snag. The engineer had brought the grievance straight to Elliot, High Lord of Engineering, in front of her peers, and asked him to protect her. Every one of them had confirmed it, even the Warlord. Yet when Marissa had checked the ER Vault and every Courtier log in the Tower of Vibes, there was no record that Elliot had ever reported the grievance, as The Codex demanded.

All Marissa wanted from the next fifteen minutes was for the High Lord to acknowledge the miss, reaffirm his sacred duty under The Codex, and close the case cleanly. She clicked Join Meeting.

Marissa kept her voice mild and open. "What do you recall happening that day?"

Across the Zoom lair, Elliot leaned back with a matte-black espresso cup, his eyes flicking toward a second monitor as he half-listened. Behind him hung a sleek, minimalist print: *Disagree and Submit*. He wore the relaxed, faintly bored expression of a man used to being the smartest person in every room he entered, radiating the quiet contempt of a High Lord who had decided the woman on his screen was some compliance clerk wasting his Friday checking a box because she did not know who he was.

"I don't recall," Elliot said smoothly, taking a slow sip. "We were in the middle of a massive platform migration that week, and I was preoccupied with keeping the castle's infrastructure upright. Beyond that, I really have nothing to add."

"Okay," Marissa said. "Can you tell me who was there that day?"

Elliot smiled lazily, leaning an elbow onto his desk as if humoring a clever technicality. "Well, that's a different question. Do you want me to pull up the Royal Summons to see who was invited to the meeting?"

This was the moment Marissa decided Elliot had chosen The Hard Way. She could not force him to tell the truth or make him care. But if he wanted to play convenient amnesia, she would gladly let him take Extreme Ownership of incompetence. Her fingers stopped on the keyboard as she raised an eyebrow.

"You can't recall who your most senior engineers are?"

The smug half-smile slipped off Elliot's face. The faint boredom drained away, replaced by the sudden, uncomfortable realization that he had severely misjudged the woman on his screen.

"I... Marcus was there," Elliot said, sitting upright fast. "Devon. Priya."

Marissa did not give him a second to catch his breath.

"I received the concern that when harassment in your fiefdom was brought directly to you, an employee looked at you and said, 'Elliot, protect me.'"

Elliot swallowed, scrambling back for his smooth armor. "I don't recall anyone saying that to me."

"I also received the concern that when it was brought to you, you laughed and did nothing," Marissa continued.

"I don't recall that either," Elliot said, losing the easy sprawl completely. "I was preoccupied with keeping production alive. I know it sounds horrible. I don't recall."

"So you're telling me you didn't hear these things because you weren't paying attention?"

Elliot blinked, his jaw tightening. "That's correct."

"Do you have any thoughts as to why I would be receiving these types of concerns about you?"

He opened his mouth, hesitated, and said, "I don't know."

"Are you saying these people are making inaccurate statements about you?"

The air left the Zoom lair all at once.

Elliot leaned forward, then back, suddenly trapped between calling his own senior staff liars and admitting he had heard every word of harassment and laughed anyway.

"No!" he sputtered, color surging fast above his quarter-zip. "No, I'm not saying anyone is lying. I'm saying I don't recall."

Marissa let the silence hang.

"Elliot," she asked simply, "do you know what your obligation is as a High Lord if you receive this type of concern?"

His head snapped up.

For years, Illusion Magic had bounced straight off Elliot's skull. Deep Magic did not bounce. It cut straight through the title to the bone.

"To tell someone like you," he said low, staring straight into the camera, "so that it can be looked into, and you can determine whether it's a Crime Against the Codex." He swallowed hard. "And I'm glad you're doing what you're doing."

He had finally recited the letter of The Codex. But that was not enough for Marissa. True Deep Magic demanded more than policy compliance; it demanded accountability for the real people he had failed because he could not be bothered to care.

So Marissa held his gaze, keeping a tight leash on the anger in her voice. She was done being polite to his ego. As she thought back to the people she had spoken to, Marissa cast a Mirror spell, showing him the faces of his engineers in her gaze so he could no longer look away, knowing full well he remembered that day.

"You are a High Lord and a leader, Elliot," she said coldly. "Your team looked to you for leadership in that moment, and they felt that you dismissed them. Can you understand that perspective?"

Elliot looked down at his desk, suddenly small beneath *Disagree and Submit*. "Absolutely," he said quietly. "Absolutely. Now that you lay out what happened, I can see how I failed on all accounts. I can see how I did not do my job in that moment."

"Thank you for your candor," Marissa said coolly, typing the final line into her case log. "I will follow up once the Formal Inquiry concludes."

She clicked End Meeting. Fifteen minutes on the dot.

Before the echo of the call had faded, Marissa opened a Slack-rune channel to Sienna, Engineering Courtier of Optics, and typed a brisk message: *Finished with Elliot. He acknowledged the failure. Please connect with him today to reinforce strict non-retaliation. Keep it general about how to move forward with the team, do not name the complainant, and make sure he does not touch her scope or rating.*

Marissa hit Send and let out a quiet breath. Across her desk, the record was clean and unspinnable, the scales balanced and Deep Magic satisfied. Now came the part she quietly loved best: enshrining a finished inquiry in a Scroll of Findings and sealing it inside the ER Vault to settle into Ancient Precedent, where no Illusion Magic could touch it. 

---

Two hours later, high across the moat inside a frosted-glass lounge in the Tower of Vibes, Sienna stared at the wall clock in quiet horror as her oat-milk matcha went completely cold.

Across the blush-velvet sofa, the untouchable High Lord of Engineering sat with his elbows on his knees, clutching a lavender stress ball like a life preserver.

"I completely messed up, Sienna," Elliot said for the ninth time in ninety minutes. "I went in there thinking she was just checking a box! I tried to ask if she wanted me to pull the Royal Summons, and she shredded me right through the screen. She threw my own word *preoccupied* right back in my face, asked if I knew my obligation as a High Lord, and before I knew it I was staring at my desk apologizing! Fifteen minutes! I folded in fifteen minutes!"

Sienna flipped frantically through her ivory-linen copy of The Grimoire of Optics, scattering tiny silver sparkles across her lap while praying her ring light did not overheat. "Breathe, Elliot. We are holding space for this growth moment. You are *not* ruined. You just need to walk back onto the engineering floor with radical steadiness."

"What if I go pull her aside right now and apologize?" Elliot asked, half-rising off the velvet cushions. "Just clear the air! Show leadership!"

"Sit down!" Sienna yelped, dropping the Grimoire onto the coffee table as her soothing Courtier cadence snapped completely in half. "Do *not* pull anyone aside! Do not send a late-night Slack-rune, do not change her sprint assignments, do not look sideways at her code reviews, and do not mention the inquiry to a living soul unless you want to hand Marissa a retaliation claim before dinner!"

Elliot sank back into the sofa, staring blankly at a diffuser puffing synthetic eucalyptus into the air. "I have survived three board reorganizations," he whispered. "Nobody in this castle has ever talked to me like that."

It took Sienna another forty-five minutes, two fresh cups of chamomile tea, and every calming platitude in Chapter Six of the Grimoire to glue Elliot's ego back together enough to walk him safely to the elevator without triggering a federal lawsuit.

The moment the elevator doors slid shut, Sienna sagged against the glass wall and ran straight into Baroness Orla of Optics, gliding past in head-to-toe ivory cashmere with an oat-milk cortado in hand.

"Sienna, darling, you look positively frazzled," Orla beamed beneath her effortless glow. "Everything aligned in Engineering?"

"Elliot just spent two hours on my couch having a full existential meltdown," Sienna breathed, still blinking. "The Royal Inquisitor over in Liability Tower dismantled him in fifteen minutes flat. He admitted everything."

Orla's practiced smile froze halfway to her cheekbones. Every Courtier in the Tower of Vibes knew Elliot was notoriously immune to Illusion Magic; buzzwords bounced straight off his engineering skull like rain off granite.

"The investigator we punted across the moat?" Orla murmured, her eyes sharpening with sudden, glittering calculation. "Fascinating."

---

Three days later, Marissa closed Elliot's Formal Inquiry in a thirty-minute Zoom lair with Sir Leo and Baroness Orla.

On screen, Marissa walked cleanly through the Scroll of Findings she had prepared from the ER Vault: the harassment grievance in Elliot's fiefdom was substantiated as a Crime Against the Codex and carried a recommendation of a Writ of Warning for the offending Warlord; Elliot's dismissal of the concerns was also substantiated as a violation of his sacred duties as a High Lord.

Across the grid, Sir Leo did not ask to soften a single line, hedge a verb, or protect Elliot's feelings. "Clean record," he said approvingly.

In the third square, Baroness Orla sat bathed in her blinding ring light, staring at Marissa with something dangerously close to awe.

"Marissa, I just have to pause and honor this moment," Orla said, clasping her hands beneath her chin. "Sienna told me how Elliot walked out of your lair. That man has shrugged off every Courtier in this castle for years, and you broke straight through his skull in fifteen minutes flat without raising your voice."

Orla leaned closer to her camera, her eyes gleaming. "Between us, Elliot is up for a major elevation at the High Table next quarter. Would you mind if I share this outcome with Lord Sterling so leadership has the full picture before they move him any higher?"

Marissa looked back at Orla through the screen without a flicker of triumph or political heat. She had already neutralized the threat to Elliot's guild, held the harasser accountable, protected the complainant, and balanced The Credibility Scales. Whether Elliot climbed another rung on the castle's ladder was Sterling's business, not hers.

"Sure," Marissa said with an easy, unbothered shrug. "Go ahead."

Orla blinked, visibly startled that the woman who had just felled an untouchable High Lord had zero interest in cashing in the political trophy. "Amazing. Truly, such grounded clarity. Thank you, team!"

The squares winked out one by one.

Marissa performed Vault-Sealing on Elliot's Scroll of Findings, locking every verified note and exhibit inside the ER Vault, and fired off her Ink-Bind recap E-parchment copying Lois, Leo, and Orla so unwritten magic could not evaporate by sundown.

Ten seconds later, a private Slack-rune pinged from Sir Leo.

*Like a knife through butter,* Leo typed. *You just saved me at least six hours of cleaning up an Engineering lawsuit, and watching Orla's jaw hit her cashmere was worth the price of admission alone. Always a joy watching a fellow licensed practitioner cook.*

Marissa let out a quiet laugh. Leo was one of the few knights of Deep Magic in the castle who truly respected her craft as an equal, and his dry wit made gray mornings bearable. Then a second Slack-rune popped up right underneath.

*Also, did you hear Lois barely even glanced up when I chimed in on that call? She has been freezing me out all week since I flagged a gap in her vendor template. Honestly, Marissa, you have no idea how exhausting it is getting zero warmth or recognition from her up here.*

Marissa stared at the glowing text for two slow seconds. Above her left shoulder, the lone fluorescent tube in the ER Watch's basement alcove buzzed like a trapped wasp over four folding tables wedged between towering stacks of archive crates. The ER Watch did not even have a budget line for proper chairs, let alone recognition.

She felt that familiar, quiet prickle of irritation behind her ribs. Leo knew perfectly well how much heavy lifting she carried off his plate, and he gladly took advantage of the lighter load even while venting to her about frostbite from a sunlit corner office six floors up.

Still, she breathed out and let the sting pass. Leo was her friend, he genuinely had her back when real trouble hit, and putting up with his blind spot was simply the unspoken tax of his allyship.

*Truly tragic conditions up on the sixth floor,* Marissa typed back deadpan. *Let me know if you need us to haul up a space heater from the archive alcove.*

*Haha, fair,* Leo replied instantly. *Send me the next ugly docket file whenever it lands. I have got your back.*

---

Six floors above the archive alcove, Doctor Lois sat behind her mahogany desk in Liability Tower, reading Marissa's crisp Ink-Bind E-parchment with quiet, clinical satisfaction.

Not a wasted word. Clean chronology, airtight risk framing, zero emotional clutter. Having a licensed practitioner of Deep Magic parked downstairs had just defused an Engineering harassment time bomb without Lois having to break a sweat.

Sir Leo leaned in through her open doorway holding a stack of severance templates. "Marissa handled Elliot cleanly. By the way, Facilities pinged me about the ER Watch's space. Apparently they are still camped at folding tables beside the archive crates, and their budget code is still frozen under Vibes. Do you want me to send a note to Lord Sterling about moving them into the third-floor annex or unlocking a contractor stipend?"

Lois did not even glance up from her Spellbook. "No."

Leo paused. "Lois, they are literally sharing one power strip next to the 2019 tax binders."

"They are an interim reporting line, Leo," Lois said smoothly, uncapping her glowing red fountain pen to slash a clumsy clause out of a vendor contract. "Lord Sterling dumped them on my doorstep without a single gold coin. If I march over to Human Capital demanding desks and budget, Sterling will smile, thank me for taking permanent ownership, and weld their headcount onto my ledger forever. Leave them packed by the door. The less comfortable the arrangement looks, the faster Vibes will be forced to come collect them."

Leo studied her for a beat, recognizing the exact point where logic hit the stone wall of Lois's political calculus. "And in the meantime?"

"In the meantime," Lois said, crisp and untroubled, "Marissa has the docket well in hand."

---

Down in the alcove, Friday afternoon brought a steady barrage of frantic pings. Every ten minutes, another Courtier of Optics sent a Royal Summons for a "quick five-minute sync" about a High Lord performance plan, a messy reorg, or an impulsive Warlord looking for a shortcut around The Codex.

By three o'clock, Marissa rubbed the bridge of her nose and stared at her overflowing calendar. Across the cramped row of folding tables, Victoria and Riley were holding the line like two Squires planted squarely in the dust of a courtyard jousting ring, taking full-speed charges from Warlords on both flanks.

On the right, a fuming Sales Warlord's voice crackled through Victoria's headset loud enough to rattle the archive crates. One of his remote reps had hung up on two prospective customers mid-call that morning after her childcare fell through without warning, and the Warlord wanted an immediate Okta Excommunication by five o'clock.

"I hear that you are frustrated," Victoria said evenly, raising a Procedural Ward with her fingers steady over a blank Writ of Warning template. "Dropping live calls is a real issue, and we can absolutely address expectations and backup coverage with her. Even so, she has zero prior documentation. The Codex still requires coaching notes and a formal warning before we even discuss an Okta Excommunication. If you want to skip those steps over a sudden childcare emergency, that is your call. I am only giving you a recommendation. You are the decision-maker."

The Warlord sputtered, his lance dropping right out of his grip as all momentum drained from his voice, and muttered that he would start with coaching notes. Victoria muted her mic and pumped a triumphant fist toward the ceiling.

Two paces to her left, Riley was bracing against a charge of their own. On Riley's screen, a Customer Service Warlord had already shared a thirty-day Trial by PIP, Corporate Castle's time-honored ritual of handing an employee a glowing red-lined scroll and calling it a "growth opportunity" while the Warlord swore on his honor that he genuinely wanted the employee to succeed. This particular Warlord was eager to drop the ultimatum on a frontline agent whose handle times had dipped that month, even though the agent had an active accommodation request still sitting untouched in the queue.

"The metrics are all in there," the Warlord said briskly through Riley's speaker. "Just bless the scroll so I can deliver it before five."

Riley did not cite a single statute or case number. "And how is her pending accommodation request coming along?" Riley asked mildly.

"That is still sitting with Benefits," the Warlord said, shifting in his chair. "I just need to hold the floor accountable."

"So you want to put her on a thirty-day Trial by PIP," Riley said calmly, "for handle times that may be directly tied to an accommodation the castle has not even finished reviewing yet?"

Silence stretched across the Zoom lair. Without another word from Riley, the Warlord heard how reckless his own scroll sounded aloud, muttered a stiff thank-you, and deleted the Trial by PIP on the spot.

Marissa watched them both with a quiet surge of pride. Neither Riley nor Victoria held a formal license, and neither kept towering ironbound Spellbooks of Deep Magic beside their monitors. They did not need them. Both Squires were naturally gifted in Deep Magic, intuitively practicing it with Warlords every day in an unofficial, unglamorous way. Without a shred of castle prestige or a corner office to hide behind, they held the courtyard jousting ring on pure instinct, moral grit, and HR Lore, fighting a relentless two-front skirmish. One hour it was blocking a retaliatory Warlord with one arm while trying to coax a chronically missing employee into actually doing their job with the other; the next it was defusing a reckless Trial by PIP or catching a small fire before it ever grew into a Formal Inquiry or a lawsuit. Nobody on the Watch could force a Warlord to act justly, because Deep Magic never overrode free will, yet watching the Squires strip away a manager's imaginary *HR approved this* armor never stopped feeling glorious.

Marissa turned back to her screen and pulled Sarai into a private Zoom lair from four feet across the alcove.

"If one more Courtier asks me to bless a Friday-afternoon firing with zero documentation," Marissa said dryly, "I'm going to start charging the Tower of Vibes a bridge toll."

Sarai laughed, a warm, grounded sound that immediately loosened the knot behind Marissa's shoulders. "You shouldn't even be in those calls, Marissa. You belong inside the ER Vault, not refereeing Warlords who skipped every single chapter of The Codex."

"I have three crown-level Formal Inquiries stacking up behind Elliot's," Marissa admitted, gesturing toward her stacked monitors. "Every time I get halfway through a witness timeline, another Courtier drops a *quick check-in* on my calendar that turns into an hour of decoding The Grimoire of Optics."

"Hand me the baton," Sarai said without hesitating. "Seriously. Give me the Courtier advising queue. Riley and Victoria are already holding the courtyard line like veterans."

"Are you sure?" Marissa asked quietly. "The Courtiers are relentless right now, especially with Baroness Orla floating around upstairs."

Sarai grinned across the tables and forwarded Orla's latest castle-wide Slack-rune, dotted with silver sparkles and thanking everyone for *co-creating synergistic, human-centric alignment journeys*.

*Did she really use "co-create" three times in one paragraph?* Marissa typed back.

*And apparently her favorite superpower is "elevating executive clarity through radical warmth,"* Sarai replied. *Pray for the Squires if she ever finds the stairwell down here.*

"I've got the queue," Sarai said aloud, her voice steady and firm. "Go do what you were built for. Let me guard the door."

That was what made the two of them work. Sarai was a natural leader, blessed with her own sharp instinct for Deep Magic and an effortless gift for rallying the Watch with wit and warmth even while they worked beside cardboard archive crates under a buzzing fluorescent tube. Marissa loved the team, and she knew she had zero desire to wear the Captain's cloak or lead the charge out front. She was the deep expert they turned to when the ground grew thorny, and Sarai was the Captain who kept the Watch upright and marching forward. There was no rivalry between them, only two friends who knew their own strengths and trusted each other completely.

---

With Sarai shielding the front door, the next week inside the alcove felt less like an exile and more like a sanctuary.

Marissa closed her final debrief with the junior engineer from Elliot's fiefdom, confirming that the Warlord had received a formal Writ of Warning, her transfer request was approved, and strict non-retaliation safeguards were locked in writing. The young woman did not cry or deliver a speech. She simply let out a long, shaking breath she looked as though she had been holding since spring, and said, "Thank you. I honestly did not think anyone could touch them."

When the call ended, Marissa sat alone at her folding table in the quiet hum of the archive alcove.

It felt like oxygen.

This was why she had chosen ER instead of chasing a gilded crest upstairs or staying behind a desk in Liability Tower. Both practices had real weight, and good practitioners like Leo belonged six floors up. Marissa belonged here, in the rare, clean moment when the noise stopped, the scales balanced, and someone doing honest work did not get crushed by rank.

At 4:45 PM, a Royal Summons pinged in the corner of her screen.

It was from Sarai. The subject line read only: *Quick chat?*

Across the alcove, Sarai was already packing her satchel, her face pale and unusually tight beneath the fluorescent lamp. When Marissa looked up, Sarai did not crack a joke about the archive crates or the Courtiers. She offered a tired, apologetic half-smile.

"Hey," Sarai said softly. "Do you have ten minutes? My doctor just put me out on Protected Exile starting Monday. It should only be for three weeks. Would you mind keeping an eye on Riley and Victoria while I am gone?"

Marissa did not hesitate for a second. Sarai was her friend, and she would never leave the Squires stranded in an unfunded corner while the Tower of Vibes circled overhead.

"Of course," Marissa said warmly. "Three weeks is nothing. Go take care of yourself. I've got the Watch."

Above them, the lone fluorescent tube hummed steadily against the stone ceiling. Three weeks seemed small enough to carry.
