# Chapter Twenty: The Transfer

The lift at Blackwater did not move that morning.

Vessa's two colleagues had made different decisions. Tola stayed away from the chain shed. The other tester went to work and examined the upper links, but refused to sign for the lower length without a second person. The foreman sent for Kelm, then remembered that Kelm was no longer there.

By ten, three carts carrying Ministry stores were waiting on the river road outside Blackwater. Officers could still reach the bank house; Harrow's supplies had to wait. A fourth cart turned back. The drivers left enough space between the vehicles for a horse to pass, and stood under the trees to keep out of the rain. One of them had brought a kettle. He refused to say who had lent it to him.

Ragna saw the carts from the carrier's wagon as she travelled to the bank house. Hedra sat opposite her, holding the receipt Pell had signed for Tavin's clothes. She had carried it every day since the visit.

“Don't give that to anyone,” Ragna said.

“I wasn't going to.”

“I know.”

“Then stop making me answer.”

Hedra leaned back against the wagon. Ragna left her to hold the receipt in peace.

At the bank house the boxes stood in the entrance hall. Arven had put a separate list on each lid. Halrik checked the contents with him while Issa watched the windows. The house had two doors, one to the street and a smaller one to the yard. Both were open. Fenn had insisted on seeing them opened before he sat down.

His arm was still supported. He had brought Edda to turn pages for him.

Mere arrived with the counterfoil in an oilcloth wrapper. Kelm came with her. His sleeve had been mended badly where the porter tore it. He stood in the yard until Sella arrived, and only then came into the hall.

Neri had wanted to attend. The healer had refused to let her travel in a cart over the rough road. She had sent her account of the rail, signed and dated, with Sella. There was a second sheet listing the tools left at Thorn and the condition of the cart when they had taken it from the shed.

“Give that one to Mere,” she had said. “Don't let it disappear in the important papers.”

Mere read it in the hall and put it beside her hire bill.

At eleven a Ministry clerk arrived to count the boxes. He had no power to change the order, he said, and was grateful when Arven gave him the lists. They counted together. The clerk found one missing withdrawal slip; Arven located it in the next box, where he had placed it with the household's other papers.

“Write the correction,” Arven said.

The clerk did. He initialled it and asked Arven to do the same.

Ragna watched from the stairs. She wanted to believe a count could keep the records safe. The count would establish what they handed over, not who opened it afterwards.

At half past eleven the clerk went to the window. A cart was approaching with two wizards on the seat. Another followed behind. Harrow stepped down from the first, carrying a case large enough to hold the iron ledger.

Merrok came in through the yard door at the same moment. His coat was dry. He had been waiting in the covered passage.

“The Ministry has agreed to compare the seized ledger with the reserve files here,” he told Issa. “It will make the transfer quicker.”

“Who agreed?” she asked.

“Harrow.”

“And who asked him?”

“I did. He promised the comparison would show the advance was a Ministry undertaking. I wanted you to see that before the boxes left.”

Harrow entered before she could answer. Pell came behind him. The second cart carried two more officers, one of whom remained with the horses. The other stationed himself at the street door.

“There are people on the road,” Pell said.

“They came to see their records counted,” Sella replied.

“They cannot all come in.”

“They haven't.”

Harrow put the case on the table Arven had cleared. He opened it and lifted out the ledger. It had returned to its full size; a dark mark crossed one corner of the cover. Ragna could not tell whether it had been damaged or merely left against something wet.

“I will compare the reserve schedule with the entries in this book,” he said. “The boxes can then be loaded.”

Issa placed Ilva's control book beside it. “Before you do, we require an explanation of the advance recorded here.”

“The inquiry is considering that matter.”

“The advance came from funds entrusted to this bank. We have supplier and receiving records for the purchase. We have an examiner's statement that a household charge was used to offset it.”

Harrow looked at Ilva. She had stood behind the partners, close enough to see the table but not close enough to touch it.

“Your statement is disputed.”

“By whom?” she asked.

“By the person responsible for the account.”

Merrok stepped forward. “The posting was temporary. It was to be reversed.”

“You checked it,” Issa said to Ilva. “Was that the instruction?”

“I was told it would be reversed when the Ministry paid.”

“Did the workshops agree to finance it?”

“No.”

Merrok's jaw moved. “You understood the risk if we refused the advance.”

“I understood the risk to the bank. I let the workshops carry it without telling them.”

The Ministry clerk stopped writing. Harrow told him to continue.

Mere unwrapped the carrier's counterfoil and put it on the table. “Forty bars. Sold by my foundry, paid for through the bank, received at Thorn. Kelm counted with the receiver.”

Harrow read the sheet. “This is a vehicle document.”

“It is also a receipt for the load.”

“It says nothing about the bank's internal posting.”

“No,” Ragna said. “The reconciliation does.”

Harrow's hand rested on the ledger cover. He did not open it.

Outside, someone called that the Blackwater lift was still idle. Another voice answered that the carts had nowhere else to go. The officer at the door went out to speak to them.

Halrik held out his hand. “The page, please.”

“The ledger is under Ministry seal.”

“It was brought here to be compared.”

“It will be compared by me.”

The Ministry clerk looked up from his list. “The transfer instruction requires the bank partners to witness the comparison. I must record whether the reconciliation was shown.”

Harrow held out his hand for the instruction. The clerk found the clause and pointed to it. Harrow read it, then looked at Merrok, who had asked for the comparison and was watching the clerk write. A refusal would go on the transfer record before the ledger reached the archive.

Issa put her own hand on the table. “This is our bank house. You have an order for the reserve records, not for the control book. I will not surrender it without a separate instruction.”

Harrow looked towards Merrok. Merrok was watching the yard door.

“Do you intend to let her obstruct the inquiry?” Harrow asked.

“I intend to find out why the Ministry has not paid the advance,” Issa said.

For the first time Harrow's attention left the ledger. He looked at the open boxes, the people in the hall, the clerks who had signed the count. His gaze moved from the open boxes to the clerk's pen.

“Mr Venn has been questioned,” he said. “His account is incomplete. Mr Tavin remains under investigation for obstruction.”

Hedra rose from her chair. “I have been to Blackwater every day. You can tell me what he's accused of without making his name part of your bargain.”

“He has been told.”

“Then tell me.”

Harrow did not answer her. He opened the ledger.

The pages moved beneath his hand. Ragna saw the familiar ruled columns, then the wages, then the edge of the page Ilva had covered. The two sheets had not been reattached. A narrow tear showed along the margin.

“There,” Ragna said.

Harrow turned the book towards himself. Halrik moved closer, holding out his hand for the reconciliation. The clerk was writing down every exchange, and the partners had already heard Ilva describe what the page contained. Harrow drew his wand and touched the page. A line of fire ran across the paper. For a moment it was so narrow Ragna thought he meant to underline an entry. Then the corner curled and the flame reached the leather.

“The seal has caught,” Harrow said. He made a quick motion as if to shut the cover, but the flame ran beneath it.

“Stop him,” Ilva said.

Pell stepped between her and the table. Halrik seized the control book. The Ministry clerk dropped his pen and backed into a box. A lid fell; withdrawal slips scattered over the floor.

Ragna went towards the ledger. Harrow raised his wand. She stopped, then moved sideways as Sella reached the table from the other end.

“Not the book,” Sella said. “The page.”

The fire had caught the binding. Ragna grabbed the iron cover. Heat struck her palm. She pulled back, and the book fell open against the edge of the table. The torn sheet came partly free.

Ilva pushed past Pell and took the loose margin. He caught her arm. She did not let go. The paper tore across the middle, leaving a strip in her hand. Ragna seized the part still attached and pulled. The binding resisted. Halrik knocked the ledger to the floor with the side of the cash box.

The lamp on the table fell. Oil spread in a bright line. Edda dragged a box away from it and shouted for water. The clerk fetched a jug, but Arven stopped him before he poured it onto the burning oil. He took a wool coat from the stand and dropped it over the flame.

In the confusion Harrow moved towards the yard door. Kelm held the door open and called to the people outside that the ledger was burning.

Two workshop owners came into the passage. Behind them Brann and Vessa appeared with a blanket from the waiting cart. Pell tried to close the door. Vessa caught the edge before it struck Kelm and held it long enough for Brann to pull the burning book clear of the oil.

Ragna had a piece of the page in her hand. Ilva held another. She could see the red figures on both. The book itself was still burning. Brann had thrown the blanket over it, but flames pushed through where the wool did not cover the spine.

“The other end,” he called.

Ragna moved to help him. From the stair came a crash. The Ministry clerk was on the floor beneath a fallen box, his ankle twisted against the bottom step. Papers had spilled around him. Harrow had gone out into the yard; Pell was following.

The clerk tried to lift the box and could not. Ragna looked at the book, at the clerk, and at the open door through which more people were coming. She put the torn page inside her coat and went to the stairs.

“Don't pull,” she told him. “We'll lift it.”

Edda came to the other side. Together they raised the box while the clerk drew his leg free. He was crying with anger and embarrassment. Ragna gave him her shoulder and brought him through the street door.

By the time she returned, the fire in the hall was out. The ledger lay beneath the scorched blanket. Its covers had held their shape. Most of the pages had not.

Brann had burned two fingers. He was trying to hide them from Vessa, who took his hand and dipped it into the water jug before he could object. Ilva sat on the bottom step with a strip of parchment on her knee. The ink had blurred at one edge, but the signature remained visible.

Issa had kept the control book. Halrik had picked up the counterfoil and given it back to Mere. The clerk from the Ministry asked for his count list. Arven found it beneath the fallen box, damp but legible.

Harrow stood in the yard with Pell. Beyond them the workmen who had come from the lift waited in the rain. Wands stayed lowered. The carts waited beyond the yard.

“You have destroyed a record held under seal,” Issa said to Harrow.

“The fire began during an attempt to obstruct an examination.”

“I saw you light the page,” Ilva said.

Merrok came out of the hall. Soot marked one side of his face. He looked at Harrow, then at Issa.

“The page was an internal reconciliation,” he said. “It was not necessary for the Ministry's transfer.”

“You signed it,” Ilva said.

He did not answer her.

The Ministry clerk appeared at the door, leaning on Edda. His ankle was swelling above his shoe. He had his count list in one hand.

“I saw the wand touch the book,” he said. “I didn't see what happened before that.”

“You saw him burn it,” Edda said.

“I saw the wand touch it. Then it caught.”

“Write what you saw,” Ragna said.

Harrow looked towards the street. More people had arrived. Some were there for their own records; others had come from Blackwater when the lift stopped. Among them Ragna saw the charcoal carrier from Thorn. He stood beside his horse with a hand on the reins, looking at the smoke above the yard.

A woman in a travelling cloak pushed through the crowd. She carried a Ministry badge and a folder under her arm. Arven had asked the officer at the street door to send for the appointed supervisor as soon as the fire began. She listened to Harrow, then to Issa. She asked for the order and the list of boxes. She did not ask to see the torn page until Ilva offered it.

“My name is Vale,” she said. “I am responsible for the transfer.”

“Then your officer has burned the book he brought to compare,” Issa replied.

Vale looked at the blackened ledger. “I will require statements.”

“You will also require a new count,” Arven said. “One box fell. Several records are wet.”

She glanced at the people in the street. The horses had been moved aside, but the carts remained empty. From the road came the sound of someone calling that no lift would be signed until the injured man at the bank house had a healer.

Vale gave the clerk permission to leave for treatment. She asked another officer to take over his list. Then she told Harrow to give her his wand.

He stared at her. “On whose authority?”

“Mine, while I establish what has happened to a record in our custody.”

For a moment Ragna thought he would refuse. He handed it over. Vale put it in her folder rather than her pocket.

“This is not a judgment,” she said to Issa. “The reserve records remain subject to the order. They will stay here under seal until the count is complete.”

“And the people held at Blackwater?” Hedra asked.

“I have no instruction regarding them.”

“Then get one.”

Vale looked at the damaged hall, the empty carts and the people waiting. She asked Hedra for their names. Hedra gave them slowly, so the officer writing beside Vale would not have to ask twice.

Ragna unfolded the piece of page inside her coat. The household name was intact. Beside it was the later forty-bar charge, and beneath that a reference to the reserve advance. The figure at the end had burned away. Ilva's strip held Merrok's signature and the start of the matching entry. Neither piece was whole. Together, with the control book and the copies, they might be enough to show what had happened.

She took them to a dry table inside, where Ilva and Arven could compare the edges.
