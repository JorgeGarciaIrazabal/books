# Chapter 6: Pip-pip-PIP!

## Page 26

She tapped the string winder.

Tonk. Tonk. TINK.

Grandma joined in on her saucepan.

Mr. Moss rattled his tin. The twins plonked their pots. The dog squeezed his bone with great care.

The red kite pulled against the sky.

Grumble stood taller on the mitten's thumb.

He puffed his cheeks.

"PIP-pip-PIP!"

From beyond the hill came an answer.

"POM-pom-POM!"

Another voice went, "Brrrumble!"

And a third went, quite distinctly, "HICCUP."

## Page 27

Grumble jumped.

For one breath he hung beneath the kite, a small gray comma in the pink sky.

Then the wind caught him.

He rose past the red tail. He tumbled once, found his balance, and sailed toward the hills.

"Thank you!" he called.

Nell waved with the spoon.

The mitten swung empty.

She kept watching until a bigger cloud curled one fluffy edge around him.

## Page 28

They wound in the kite together.

Mr. Moss opened his biscuit tin. It contained three biscuits and rather a lot of crumbs.

Grandma fetched more from the kitchen.

The twins shared theirs with the dog. The dog ate the crumbs too, just to be helpful.

Nell sat on the overturned wheelbarrow with the mitten in her lap.

Its thumb was still a little damp.

She tucked one finger inside and looked toward the hills.

## Page 29

At bedtime, Nell lined up her boots beneath the window.

She checked them both.

One pebble. No weather.

Grandma came to say good night. She had washed her hair, which turned out to be a little wet after all.

"Should I leave the window open?" Nell asked.

"A crack, if you like."

Nell hung the mitten on the latch.

Then she got into bed and listened.

## Page 30

For a while, nothing happened.

Then three drops tapped the windowsill.

Tonk. Tonk. TINK.

Nell sat up.

Far above the house, something small and fluffy crossed the moon.

"PIP-pip-PIP!"

Nell tapped the bedpost in reply.

From next door came a sleepy squeak.

The dog had joined in.

Nell snuggled under her blanket.

In the morning, her left boot was dry.

Her right boot contained exactly one tiny rainbow.
