# Chapter 10: The Kingdom of Free Mornings

The first miracle in the Kingdom of Valladolid arrived quietly at seven forty-five on a Tuesday morning, and nobody had to run for a train.

Sunlight poured clear and bright across pale oak floorboards. Outside the wide garden glass, sparrows hopped along the stone wall while a warm Castilian breeze stirred two potted rosemary bushes beside the patio bench. Inside, on the sunny kitchen shelf above the range, the heirloom copper pot sat polished and serene beside Poki's silver collar bell, his smooth gray windowsill stone, and the gleaming brass sunburst key.

Marissa stood by the front gate in soft wool, sipping dark coffee while the Compass of Living Streets rested quiet and warm inside her pocket. It did not buzz or tug anymore; it simply stayed comfortably settled, because fifty paces down the avenue three neighbors were already waving from the bakery corner.

"Walk Clara in?" Marissa called back toward the porch. "Elena invited me to test the new café on the plaza before our afternoon errands at RÍO."

"Go enjoy real coffee," Jorge said, buckling Clara's bright purple helmet under her chin while her red bicycle gleamed in the morning sun. "And warn Elena I am terrible at baking, in case anyone gets wild ideas about a neighborhood cake swap."

"Trust me," Marissa laughed, kissing both of them cleanly on the cheek. "I already protected the entire block."

Jorge reached onto the kitchen shelf, slipped the brass sunburst key into his coat pocket, and walked beside Clara down the patio steps. At the wrought-iron garden gate, he fitted the key cleanly into the lock and turned it once. With a crisp, bright click, the Gate of Free Mornings swung smoothly wide onto the shaded bike path.

Clara coasted ahead on two steady wheels, ringing her silver bell at every puddle like a miniature knight on patrol.

"¡Mira, Papa!" Clara shouted happily, pointing one finger up toward the crisp blue sky. "¡Un *dragón* de nubes! No, espera, it turned into a unicorn with purple boots!"

"Un *caballo mágico* impresionante," Jorge tried solemnly, mangling one ending on purpose just to watch her grin.

Clara braked neatly beside the school gate and shook her helmeted head in strict six-year-old judgment. "Un *unicornio*, Papa. And you promised to read the big wizard mystery chapter tonight without skipping the creepy dragon part."

"Deal," Jorge promised, tapping her handlebars once as she raced through the bright courtyard toward two smiling new classmates shouting her name. "I own all of Tuesday now. Even the creepy dragon chapters."

Walking home without checking a pocket watch once felt almost illegal.

By nine thirty, Jorge stood out on his very own back patio, surrounded happily by screwdrivers, copper conduit, a solar panel leaning against the wall, and Garbanzo hovering anxiously behind a terracotta geranium pot.

"Are we completely sure the yellow wire joins the terrace switch?" Garbanzo whispered nervously, all four emerald star-crystals glowing pale cautious green. "Last week's instruction parchment was written in very small letters."

"I took an entire electrical workshop for this," Jorge said loftily, tightening the final brass screw on his brand-new solar garden lantern. "Behold practical craftsmanship!"

He pressed the switch.

*POP! FZZZT!*

A cheerful little puff of bright blue smoke shot straight out of the junction box, leaving one faint smudge across Jorge's left knuckle and making a neighborhood magpie squawk down the fence.

Garbanzo yelped and hid squarely behind the rosemary bush. "Abandon patio! Call the Citadel!"

Jorge stared at the smoking wire for two whole seconds, burst out laughing, wiped his face with his shirt sleeve, and reached calmly back into his Tinker's Satchel for his voltage meter. "Good thing nobody puts me in charge of dinner, huh? Wrong terminal on the ground rail. Give me three minutes."

This time he checked both wires twice, insulated the seam cleanly, and snapped the housing shut.

*Click.*

Inside its clear glass globe, the solar lantern bloomed soft, steady amber light right in the middle of the bright Spanish morning, humming quietly without a single spark out of place.

At ten o'clock sharp, a clear brass bell rang across the garden grass.

Garbanzo rose proudly above the patio table, brushed rosemary dust off his brass shoulder, and let his four emerald crystals spin into neat morning formation. Out of his neat printer slot slid a single crisp cream-colored scroll edged in gold, without a speck of fake celebratory confetti anywhere near the butter dish.

Jorge unrolled it and read aloud:

*Ten O'Clock Report. Salary: Zero. Internal Transfers: Zero. Emergency Buffer: Intact. Family Vaults: Balanced at Fifty-Fifty. Tuesday Morning Freedom: Paid in Full.*

"Not a single double count," Jorge murmured softly, tapping the little brass lid affectionately. "Nice work, buddy."

That evening, after a fast, loud padel match down the avenue and two long, triumphant chapters of Clara's wizard book read aloud on the sofa without once glancing at a work chime, the family lingered out in the garden while twilight painted the limestone golden violet.

Inside, dinner smelled gently of rosemary and slow warmth. Out along the back garden wall, high above the blooming lavender, a sleek shadow cat sat poised and calm in the gathering stars, wearing nothing but dark fur and silver light, green eyes steady on his three favorite people laughing together below.
