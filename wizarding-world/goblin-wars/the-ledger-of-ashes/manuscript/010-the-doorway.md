# Chapter Ten: The Doorway

Sella moved the food list away from Harrow's hand.

“There are names on that which have nothing to do with you.”

“I have not asked for it.”

“Then you won't need to see it.”

She folded it and put it inside her coat. Ragna saw Pell watching the movement. He had his wand out already, held low against his leg.

Venn took the dispatch from its envelope. He laid it on the table with the receiving office's receipt beside it.

“This is the original I have retained. You may examine it. I want its receipt witnessed.”

Harrow opened the black case.

“Come here,” he said.

Venn stayed where he was. “May I have the receipt?”

“You will have an acknowledgement at the office.”

“Which office?” Ragna asked.

“The inquiry office.”

“Here, or at Blackwater?”

“You are not being asked to accompany us.”

“His family will ask where he is.”

Harrow looked at her at last. The room had grown quiet enough that she heard water running from a gutter outside.

“Blackwater,” he said. “For the time being.”

“Write it down.”

Pell stepped forward. The movement brought him close to Fenn, who was still standing beside the table. Fenn backed away, caught a stool with his heel and righted himself with a hand on the weighing beam's upright.

“We are not preventing you taking a record,” he said. “We have asked for a receipt.”

“You have been given an answer,” Pell said.

At the door someone called, “What have they done?”

Tavin turned. “Stand back. There's no room.”

The younger wizard in the entrance put an arm across it. He had freckles and a wet collar. “You heard him. Keep the doorway clear.”

Ragna could see two faces over his shoulder. Edda was one. She had come with her mended lantern, its new pane catching the light from the room.

Venn picked up the dispatch and held it out. Harrow took it with two fingers, glanced at the initials and put it into the case.

“Pell. His wand.”

Venn looked at him. “I've given you the record.”

“And you have been directed to accompany us.”

“Am I being detained?”

“You are required for questioning about the retention and alteration of official material.”

“I told you I altered it. I gave the instruction reference.”

“Your wand.”

Pell held out his hand. Venn slowly unfastened the pocket inside his coat. His fingers caught on the button. He tried twice before he freed the wand and laid it in Pell's palm.

The furnace worker at the door said something Ragna did not hear. Edda replied sharply. The younger wizard raised his voice.

“Move away from the entrance.”

“We are moving,” somebody said.

But they were not. People behind were still arriving, stopping when they saw those in front. The man beside Edda turned sideways and tried to push back. His shoulder struck the door. It swung into the young wizard's arm.

“Reed,” Harrow said.

The wizard turned with his wand raised. A pale force struck the wall beside the opening. Ragna heard a crack of wood and a scream. The people nearest the doorway were thrown backwards into the crowd. The opening cleared.

Fenn let go of the upright and went towards the door.

“Don't,” Ragna said, too late.

The man who had been beside Edda stumbled into the room, one arm across his face. He collided with Fenn. The table lurched. A chair overturned. Fenn put a hand out to break his fall, and Ragna heard the short, dry sound from his wrist.

Tavin dropped beside him.

“Back,” he said to the stumbling man. “You're on his foot.”

Pell caught Venn by the arm. Sella stepped away from the table as the hanging pan swung through the place where her head had been. It struck a lamp bracket. The lamp fell, went out, and hit the floor with a hollow metal sound.

Someone outside shouted that a wand had been drawn. Another voice shouted that they were taking people. Instructions rang from the weighing pans, too loud and tangled to follow.

Ragna went to the side door. She remembered it opening into the carrier's lane, but there was a plank set across the bolts. She pulled at it. One end lifted; the other had swollen in its socket.

“Sella.”

Sella was beside her at once. She put two fingers against the iron catch and pressed. The catch warmed against Ragna's hand, shifted enough for the plank to lift. Together they dragged it away and drew the bolts.

The door opened onto rain and a wall of stacked baskets.

“Put them outside,” Ragna said to a woman who had come around the building. “Clear the door.”

She turned back. Fenn was on his knees, his right arm held against his stomach. Tavin had one hand under his left elbow and the other on the back of his coat. They moved towards the new opening a step at a time.

Behind them the man who had stumbled in grabbed Pell's sleeve.

“Let him stand,” he shouted. “There isn't room.”

Pell twisted free and struck him in the chest with a burst from his wand. The man flew backwards against the upright. This time Ragna saw the wand and the light. She saw him hit the wood. She did not see what his hand had been doing before he grabbed the sleeve.

The beam rocked. Its chain swung down towards Fenn's head.

Tavin shoved Fenn sideways and caught the pan with his shoulder. Metal struck metal. Fenn fell again, this time against the open door. Ragna reached for his coat and pulled him clear.

“Out,” she told him. “Go out.”

He could not straighten. A woman outside took his uninjured arm.

Tavin was still under the pan. Pell had turned towards him, wand level with his face.

“He's hurt,” Tavin said. “We were getting him out.”

“Stay where you are.”

Tavin looked towards Fenn. He lifted one hand, palm open, and moved to follow.

Pell made a short movement with his wand. Cord whipped around Tavin's wrists and pulled them together so sharply that he cried out.

Ragna started towards him. Harrow stepped into her path.

“He has obstructed officers carrying out their duties.”

“He helped a man who fell.”

“Stand aside.”

Pell pulled Tavin upright. One cord caught around the broken clasp of his sleeve, pinching the skin beneath. Tavin twisted his hands to ease it.

“Give me his name,” Harrow said.

“You know his name.”

“Then he may give it.”

Tavin was looking at her. He was trying to keep his face still. His mouth trembled.

“Tavin,” he said, and gave the rest of it quietly enough that Ragna could not hear over the voices outside.

Reed had come into the room. Blood ran from a cut at his hairline. He looked at Fenn through the side door, then at the man slumped beneath the upright.

“That one needs help,” he said.

“Then call it,” Harrow replied.

“Sir.”

“Now.”

The fourth wizard went out. Reed stayed beside the injured man and crouched to speak to him. Ragna saw the careful distance he kept between his hand and the man's chest. She would have to include that, too, when she told what she had seen.

Harrow took Venn by the upper arm. Venn's face was white. He had not resisted, and it seemed not to matter.

“Please tell my aunt,” he said to Ragna.

“I will.”

“Not my mother by herself.”

“I understand.”

Pell brought Tavin after him. Ragna followed to the front door. Outside, the crowd had spread into the road. Edda sat on the low wall with her lantern in pieces beside her. She was holding a cloth against her cheek. A carrier stood beside his cart, staring at the shaft of one wheel as if it had betrayed him.

Harrow ordered him to bring the cart forward.

“It's hired,” the man said.

“It will be paid for.”

Ragna almost laughed. The sound caught in her throat and hurt.

Venn climbed onto the cart without being pushed. Tavin had difficulty with the step because his hands were bound. Pell put a hand under his elbow; Tavin jerked away from him, nearly fell, and had to accept the hand on the second attempt.

Ragna went to the side of the cart.

“Look at me,” she said.

Tavin did.

“I am telling your mother where you are. We will ask to see you.”

He swallowed. “Fenn?”

“Outside. He's with Sella.”

“His hand.”

“I know.”

Harrow closed the cart's little rear gate. Pell sat beside the driver. The black case rested on Harrow's knees. Ragna looked from it to Venn and then to Tavin, trying to hold all three places in her mind.

As the cart moved, Tavin twisted to see past Harrow. Ragna walked beside it until the wheel struck a rut and splashed mud over her coat. Then the horse found its pace, and she had to stop.

The fourth wizard came back with a witch carrying a leather medical bag. They passed Ragna without speaking. She followed them into the weigh house.

A splinter from the doorframe had cut her jaw. She found it when she touched the wet place beneath her ear. The witch told her to sit down. Ragna shook her head and went through the side door to Fenn.

He was on an upturned basket with Sella beside him. His wrist had begun to swell above his cuff. He held it very carefully, his mouth open a little as if he were listening to a distant sound.

“He thinks he can mend it himself,” Sella said.

“I said the glass in the lantern,” Fenn replied. “Not my wrist.”

Edda had come around the building. She held the lantern frame, bent now at one corner, with a useless stubbornness Ragna recognised.

“Leave it,” Fenn said.

She put it on the ground at last.

The witch came to examine his hand. Ragna gave her room. Inside the building Reed was talking to the injured man, who had begun to answer. The witch asked for a clear table. Sella went to find one.

Ragna stood in the carrier's lane with the remaining papers under one arm. She could not remember picking them up. The receiving receipt was crumpled against the other papers. One corner was wet.

A woman asked her what had happened.

Ragna started to say that they had come for a record. She stopped. The woman was looking at the side door, the injured people and the broken lamp somebody had brought out into the lane.

“I saw them take Daniel Venn and Tavin,” she said. “I saw Pell bind Tavin after he helped Fenn. I didn't see the first movement at the front door clearly.”

“Somebody said a knife.”

“I saw no knife.”

“Somebody said a man hit an officer.”

“I saw a man grab Pell's sleeve.”

The woman nodded, unsatisfied. Ragna wanted a whole account to give her. She had only pieces, some of them very clear.

Reed came through the side door holding a slate. The cut at his hairline had stopped bleeding. He asked the witch for the injured man's name.

“Ask him,” she said.

“He isn't answering me.”

“Then wait.”

Reed looked at the people in the lane. Nobody offered to help him fill the silence. At last he put the slate under his arm and went back inside.

The witch told Fenn that his wrist needed further attention in her rooms. He asked how long before he could use the hand.

“I haven't finished looking at it.”

“I work glass.”

“I know what you do. At present you are making it harder for me to do what I do.”

He shut his mouth. Sella gave the witch his workshop address for the bill. When Ragna began to say the fund had no money left, Sella stopped her with a look.

“We will deal with that later,” she said.

The witch pressed a small warmed pad into Fenn's uninjured hand and told him to hold it against the bandage. He did as he was asked, staring at his own thick fingers. Ragna had seen those fingers sort coins, check dispatch copies and carry flour. She could not remember seeing them idle until now.

Neri arrived while the witch was binding Fenn's wrist. She had run from the workshop and was carrying her apron in one hand. Sella told her Orren was still with the neighbour. Neri nodded, bent to pick up Edda's lantern and put it out of the way so nobody would step on it.

“Who was taken?” she asked.

Ragna told her.

“Where?”

“Blackwater. Harrow said Blackwater.”

Neri looked at the road. The cart was long gone.

“Tell his mother,” she said.

“I will.”

“Now. Before somebody else does.”

Ragna gave her the papers. She checked that Sella had seen the handover, then went to do the thing she had promised Tavin she would do.
