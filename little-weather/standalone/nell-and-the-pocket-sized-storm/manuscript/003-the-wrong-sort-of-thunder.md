# Chapter 3: The Wrong Sort of Thunder

## Page 11

"I would rather not."

Nell waited.

A bee visited a bean flower. Grandma untangled the kite string.

"My thunder is wrong," Grumble said at last.

"Wrong how?"

"Other storms go BOOM. Or CRASH. My cousin can rattle every saucepan in a kitchen."

He pulled his eyebrows over his eyes.

"Once, I thundered above a pond. Six ducks answered."

Nell pressed her lips together very hard.

## Page 12

"You can try with just me here," she said.

Grumble glanced at Grandma.

Grandma turned toward the beans and began humming to them.

Grumble took a breath. He puffed his cheeks. His belly wobbled.

"PIP!"

It sounded like a rubber duck being sat on.

A laugh popped out of Nell before she could catch it.

Grumble turned away.

One drop slid off his chin.

## Page 13

Nell's laugh stopped.

"I'm sorry. I didn't mean to laugh."

Grumble said nothing.

Nell picked a blade of grass and twisted it around her finger.

"It surprised me," she said. "But you asked for help, and I laughed. I'm sorry."

From behind the beans came a gentle clatter.

Grandma had knocked her spoon against the watering can.

Tink. Tonk.

Nell looked up.

Then she looked at Grumble.

## Page 14

"Would you like some other noises to go with yours?"

"What sort of noises?"

Nell borrowed the spoon and tapped the watering can.

Tink. Tonk. Tink-tink. Tonk.

Grumble lifted one eyebrow.

"That's a peculiar noise."

"It's a watering can. It hasn't practiced."

She held out the spoon.

"You can choose the beat."

Grumble listened to the can, then tapped its side with one fluffy point.

Tonk. Tonk. TINK.

## Page 15

Grandma fetched a saucepan and two wooden spoons.

"I was going to cook peas," she said. "They can wait."

She played Grumble's beat.

Tonk. Tonk. TINK.

Nell joined in on the watering can.

Over the fence, Mr. Moss's head appeared.

"Is that a band?"

"Possibly," said Nell.

"Needs a biscuit tin."

He disappeared.

A moment later, a biscuit tin rose above the fence. Then Mr. Moss rose behind it.
