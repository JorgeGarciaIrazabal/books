# Causal outline

- SC-0001, pages 1–4: Nell discovers Grumble in her boot; his family is departing over the hills at sunset; she offers help.
- SC-0002, pages 5–7: Lack of wind prevents his ascent. Nell chooses a kite and mitten carriage; the first launch fails because the evening breeze has not arrived.
- SC-0003, pages 8–10: While Nell fetches the winder, Grumble drinks from the tap and swells too large and heavy to fly. He reveals that thunder releases his water as rain.
- SC-0004, pages 11–13: Fear of his squeaky voice prevents rain. Nell's surprise laugh hurts him; she apologizes and listens.
- SC-0005, pages 14–18: Nell offers accompanying sounds; Grumble chooses the beat and consents to the growing band. His thunder makes rain; shared comic play restores trust.
- SC-0006, pages 19–20: Nell redirects the shower to the dry vegetables. Releasing water makes Grumble small and buoyant again.
- SC-0007, pages 21–24: The breeze arrives as his family reaches the hills. Nell and Grandma untangle the string and fly the prepared kite with its mitten passenger.
- SC-0008, pages 25–27: At height, Grumble hesitates. Nell uses their rhythm to support him. His family answers his call; he chooses to leap into the wind and joins them.
- SC-0009, pages 28–30: The garden company shares biscuits; Nell feels the absence. A nighttime musical greeting and a rainbow in her boot give her a lasting connection.

## Local drafting contract
POV: Nell, except no access to other characters' private thoughts. One afternoon through the following morning. Stage the action in house, garden, and adjoining open grass with Grandma available. Track cloud size, water, saucer, wheelbarrow, mitten, kite and string; separate the wind problem from the water-weight problem. Readers learn the rain rule when Nell does. Do not reveal the full family chorus or final rainbow until their pages. No sequel setup is required.
