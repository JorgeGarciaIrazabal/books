# Chapter 4: The Garden Band

## Page 16

Next door, the twins heard the tin and hurried over with two empty flowerpots.

The dog followed, carrying a squeaky bone.

Nell checked with Grumble.

"Is this too many people?"

He peered over the wheelbarrow.

Grandma held her spoons ready. The twins waited with their hands above the pots.

Nobody played.

Grumble straightened one fluffy point.

"The dog can stay," he said.

Then he lifted the other point.

"And everyone else."

## Page 17

"From the beginning," said Grumble.

Tonk. Tonk. TINK.

The saucepan clonked. The flowerpots plonked. The biscuit tin went RATTLE-ATTLE-ATTLE.

The dog bit his bone.

SQUEAK.

Grumble blinked at him.

The dog wagged.

Grumble took a breath.

"Pip."

A few drops spotted the grass.

Nell kept the beat steady. She watched Grumble's little points rise and fall.

He took another breath.

"PIP!"

## Page 18

Rain pattered out of the wheelbarrow.

Grumble's eyebrows bobbed.

"PIP-pip-PIP!"

The twins added a plonk. Mr. Moss shook his tin. Grandma put the saucepan on her head and tapped the bottom.

Nell missed a beat because she was staring at Grandma.

"Keeps my hair dry," Grandma said.

Grumble gave such a loud PIP that the dog answered.

SQUEAK!

This time Grumble laughed first.

## Page 19

The rain made a widening puddle under the wheelbarrow.

Beside it, Grandma's bean plants stood in dry, cracked soil.

"Can we take the music to the beans?" Nell asked.

Grumble peered down.

"I believe this shower is available for bookings."

Grandma lifted the handles.

Nell walked beside Grumble, playing the watering can. The twins followed. Mr. Moss brought up the rear.

The dog marched in the middle as if the whole thing had been his idea.

## Page 20

They watered the beans.

PIP-pip-PIP.

They watered the carrots.

PIP-pip-PIP.

They watered a very dusty garden gnome.

"He's been needing that," said Grandma.

Grumble grew smaller as they went. First armchair-sized. Then cushion-sized. Then teapot-sized.

At bread-roll-sized, he rose out of the wheelbarrow and settled on Nell's shoulder.

His rain stopped.

"Oh," he said. "I can see the gnome's ears from here. They are dreadful."
