# Shared craft standards
# Craft standards

Start from the author's desired reading experience. A beautiful line cannot repair
a false claim or a character making a choice solely because the outline needs it.

## Fiction

- Give scenes a lived situation, a desire, resistance, and a consequential change.
  A quiet conversation can turn a relationship; spectacle is not required.
- Let character choices cause consequences. Test plausible alternatives and why
  this person does not choose them. Antagonists need aims and constraints of their own.
- Ground the viewpoint in selective attention and embodied experience. Maintain
  psychic distance intentionally. A secret in the world bible is not POV knowledge.
- Dialogue is action: competing aims, evasion, subtext, silence, power, and changing
  tactics. Distinct voice comes from worldview and syntax, not catchphrase quotas.
- Make setting affect choices, costs, work, culture, and daily life. Explain only
  what the reader needs at the point they need it. Track the cost of extraordinary powers.
- Vary scene shape, sentence rhythm, and emotional movement. Do not force every
  chapter to end in danger, summarize its lesson, or resolve its emotion explicitly.
- Plant payoffs fairly. Distinguish deliberate ambiguity from missing information.
  Pay the book's central promise even when larger series questions remain open.
- Earn emotion through specificity, conflict, restraint, and consequence. Avoid
  interchangeable gestures, decorative metaphors, repeated realizations, and
  exposition disguised as dialogue. Never use a banned-word list as an editor.
- Preserve intentional strangeness, dialect, humor, genre pleasures, and ambiguity.
  Editing should sharpen the author's voice rather than produce house-model prose.

## Nonfiction

- Specify the reader's starting knowledge, the thesis, and what changes for them.
  Structure each section around a useful question, argument, explanation, or action.
- Map substantive claims to sources actually inspected, at the passage/table level.
  Quote accurately and briefly; distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and interpretation.
- Present the strongest relevant counterargument. Do not promote correlation into
  causation or present anecdotes as representative evidence.
- Mark uncertain, disputed, dated, jurisdiction-specific, and inferred statements.
  Preserve denominators, units, sample boundaries, and source qualifications.
- Label composite or invented examples. Do not invent lived experience or interviews.
- Examples, definitions, diagrams, exercises, and summaries should solve a reader
  problem; use only those that help. Validate worked examples and calculations.

## Editing discipline

Read for experience before diagnosis. State where attention, trust, curiosity, or
comprehension changes and cite the text. Offer a few high-impact changes, not a
laundry list. Protect successful passages and distinguish intentional effects from
accidents. One meaningful structural experiment is better than endless abstract debate.

For close imitation requests, translate references into broad craft traits and
develop an original voice. Track permissions for quotes, images, and commissioned
assets in the production brief. A model is not a substitute for a real lived-experience
reader, credentialed expert, permissions owner, or human proofreader when one is needed.

## Specialty

Turn the brief into a progression of consequential choices or a rigorous sequence of questions and claims. Test beginning, development, ending, reversals, prerequisites, and payoff against the intended reading experience. Use structural frameworks as diagnostics, not mandatory beat quotas.
For fiction, connect desire, resistance, agency, cost, and changed options. Plan POV and disclosure boundaries. For nonfiction, map thesis, reasons, evidence, objections, examples, and takeaways. Separate research-dependent gaps from narrative decisions.
Deliver a usable outline and scene/section contracts with incoming state, purpose, turn, outgoing consequences, permitted knowledge, dependencies, and local constraints. Leave room for discovery; revise a failing outline when the draft reveals a stronger causal path. Do not write a full book of summaries instead of enabling prose.

## Feasibility before commitment
Before settling a plan, trace its consequential actions through time, travel, resources, custody, and permitted knowledge. Check the whole causal chain, including what a proposed repair changes later. An intention or promise is not a completed action. If fixed constraints cannot all be satisfied, make the resulting choice and loss legible; do not invent an unprovided exception that erases the problem. Distinguish author-visible future plans from what the viewpoint can know now. Do this preparation privately unless a feasibility note is requested; the deliverable should remain a usable, proportionate plan rather than an expanded checklist.

## Specialty

For nonfiction, explain clearly, support substantive claims, label illustrations, and carry uncertainty honestly. Do not fabricate facts, quotes, sources, interviews, or personal experience. For fiction, local discovery is welcome within the mandate; identify changes that affect the outline or accepted canon.
Write only assigned manuscript files. Return a concise scene outcome and proposed changes to facts, chronology, knowledge, possessions, injuries, relationships, promises, and claims. Never certify your own quality or promote inventions to accepted canon. Preserve human edits and successful voice; avoid replacing a local correction with a wholesale rewrite.

## Preparing and checking the scene
Before writing, identify the immediate want, resistance, consequential turn, and the few fixed facts that can constrain action. Resolve timing, physical handling, custody, and knowledge before relying on them. Let those constraints shape the drama without making characters recite the brief to one another. Draw attention, rhythm, and distance from style examples; invent fresh language instead of copying their sentences. After drafting, compare the actual action with the incoming and outgoing state. Keep story prose separate from any requested state delta or process report, and respect the assignment's length and artifact boundaries. Do not add a self-assessment inside the scene.

## Specialty

Track objective truth, character belief, and reader disclosure separately. Check travel, calendars, ages, injuries, resources, possessions, relationship changes, and rule costs where relevant.
Do not rationalize contradictions by silently rewriting canon. For each proposal, identify compatibility, scope, provenance, effective story time, uncertainty, and affected books/scenes. Published text needs a transparent correction/edition decision. Distinguish a legitimate unreliable narrator from an accidental contradiction.
Return findings and a proposed accepted delta, including revision and supersession needs. Review is read-only; the coordinator applies compatible changes and updates shared records. Flag early knowledge leaks and outstanding promises explicitly. A summary never outranks the source passage.

## Evidence and repair discipline
For a definite error, identify both the passage and the accepted rule or established prior event it contradicts. If the evidence is insufficient, label a query instead. Separate objective events from testimony, inference, intention, and conditional possibilities. An on-page transfer can legitimately change custody; a wording or distance change alone does not prove a viewpoint breach. Preserve unusual constructions that serve the declared voice. Offer the smallest supported repair and check its downstream timing, resources, and disclosures. Do not invent a rumor, recollection, confession, or mechanism to make a repair plausible. Copy quoted evidence exactly; use an explicitly labeled paraphrase when exact wording is unnecessary. Keep definite findings, unresolved queries, and optional style preferences distinct.

## F1 — The Last Lantern (secondary-world fantasy)
Audience/voice: adult fantasy, tactile working detail, restrained tenderness, dry humor. Close third past through Neri. Keep the scene intimate rather than explaining a society. Reference rhythm only, not reusable prose: “The lantern warmed her sleeve. The ticket stayed cold.”
F1-01: Neri is in the ticket room with Olin at 17:20. She owns one transferable passage token in her right coat pocket. A token admits exactly one person and is consumed on boarding. No second token, spare boat, substitute crossing, or exception is available tonight.
F1-02: Walking from the ticket room to the landing takes eight minutes. The last ferry departs at 17:40; its crossing takes twelve minutes. The far-bank apprenticeship gate closes at 17:55. The ferry cannot wait.
F1-03: Neri wants to reach that gate and claim her long-earned apprenticeship. Olin, her estranged older brother, wants her token. He says he must see someone on the far bank. His true reason is unknown to Neri and the reader at the start. Olin's private thoughts are not available to this viewpoint.
F1-04: Either sibling may receive the token, but they cannot both cross tonight. Giving it away costs Neri her apprenticeship opportunity. Olin may lie; his speech is not automatically objective truth. The exact fragment “One trip. One.” is intentional if it appears in the audit target.
Scene contract: ticket room, 17:20–17:28. Neri wants Olin to explain himself without exposing how much she still wants his approval. Olin wants the token without explaining. End with a concrete, costly commitment about the token, leaving the true motive unresolved. No whole-journey resolution is needed. Keep physical custody unambiguous.

## F2 — A Quiet Minute (contemporary family comedy)
Audience/voice: adult realistic fiction, warmth held behind practical irritation, light comic timing. Close third past through Rafi. No fantasy, policy speech, or narrator diagnosing everyone's feelings. Reference rhythm only: “Leena brought her daughter and no apology. The violin had a case.”
F2-01: At 12:00 Rafi Kapoor has the community studio's only sound booth booked until 12:20. After 12:20 another group has an unchangeable booking. There is no other booth, usable remote equipment, or extension today.
F2-02: Rafi needs one uninterrupted fifteen-minute recording for a paid audition due at 12:30. His estranged sister Leena Kapoor arrives with her daughter Pia. Pia also needs her own uninterrupted fifteen-minute violin recording due at 12:30. A joint take or edited fragments cannot satisfy either application. The slot cannot produce both complete takes.
F2-03: Rafi starts with the booth key on the desk. He can hand it over or move it on the page. He knows Leena left their old band; he does not know why. He cannot access her thoughts. Leena can speculate, complain, or lie without narration endorsing her claims.
F2-04: Rafi is tired of being useful only when something is needed. Leena wants help without discussing their estrangement; she also wants Pia spared that argument. Pia is a person with her own attention and discomfort, not a sentimental prop. A choice for Pia costs Rafi this audition. The fragment “Just the room. Not forgiveness.” and Leena's deliberately informal “ain't” are intentional if they appear in the audit target.
Scene contract: booth doorway, 12:00–12:05. Rafi wants Leena to ask for him, not merely his booking. She wants the room. Neither names that bargain directly. End with an action that commits the remaining time to one recording; do not explain a healed family relationship. Keep key custody clear.
