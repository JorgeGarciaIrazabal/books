# 6. The Map That Wasn't

Noah had drawn a large rectangle beside the mint bed. Inside it was a wobbly circle.

“What's that?” Mila asked.

“The lantern table. With the paint bowl.”

She looked up. There was no table there now.

“They carried it inside when it rained,” Noah said. “You can see where its legs were.”

On the paving stones were four small dry squares, slowly darkening.

Mila remembered the bowl. Someone had been washing a brush in it when she and Noah first came out.

Noah put his finger on two lines in his drawing. They curved past the library door, then bent toward the left.

“I drew the tracks I could see.”

“You drew those?”

“That's why I wanted the ruler.”

Mila looked harder. She had thought he was drawing tables because he liked drawing tables. She hadn't asked.

The little lines went around the place where the lantern table had stood.

Mila carried the notebook back across the yard. Dad and Noah came beside her.

Under the library's overhanging roof, a pale bit of track had survived. Farther out, everything was dark and wet.

She turned the notebook so its doorway faced the real doorway.

“Here?” she asked.

Noah nodded.

She followed the drawn lines with one finger. They headed toward the gate. The other path, past the bakery, lay beyond the table's old position.

“If the trolley went right, wouldn't it have hit the table?”

“Or it could have turned again,” Noah said.

Mila pictured someone steering a big box between table legs and chairs. She couldn't quite make the turn fit in her head.

“Can you show me?”

Noah looked up.

“With the trolley,” she said. “You work out where the table was. I'll help.”

“We'll need something to mark the legs.”

Dad offered four fallen twigs.

Mila took them from him.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

They put one twig beside each dry square.

Dad brought out the empty trolley. Noah lined its back wheels up with the marks beneath the roof. Then he pushed slowly along the curve in his drawing.

Mila walked beside the corner, holding the notebook where he could see it.

The trolley rolled past the twig table and pointed toward the garden gate.

“Now try going to the bakery,” she said.

They returned to the beginning. Noah tried turning right where the drawn tracks turned left. The trolley's corner swept over one of the twigs.

“Table leg,” Mila said.

He tried a wider turn. This time the trolley missed the twig, but its wheels left the route he'd drawn.

“It could get there,” Noah said. “Just not along those marks.”

Mila looked down the garden path. “Let's try the shed first.”

She picked up the twigs while Noah backed the trolley away.

There was room for his drawing in the notebook after all.

Dad put the trolley back. Mila waited for Noah at the gate.

“I shouldn't have called it just a picture,” she said.

“It is a picture.”

“You know what I mean.”

He did. She could tell by the way he took the notebook without shutting it.

“You draw better maps than me,” she said.

“You ask more questions.”

“Some bad ones.”

Noah bent to move a snail out of the path. He set it beneath the mint, facing away from everyone's shoes.

“Ask June if she's got the key yet.”

Mila laughed. That was a question she could manage.

Aunt June came back with the basket on one arm and a key held high in the other hand.

They went through the gate together. The garden smelled of damp soil. Beyond the beds, a small shed stood under a plum tree.

Its roof was still ticking with drops.

Mila hurried to keep up with Noah.
