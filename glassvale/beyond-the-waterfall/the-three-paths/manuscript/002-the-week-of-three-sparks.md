# Chapter 2: The Week of Three Sparks

Every eleven-year-old at Glassvale spent Common Term testing all three Paths before choosing one beneath the central bells.

On Tuesday morning, Clara climbed into the Brass Spire, where giant wooden gears clicked high overhead and warm golden steam puffed harmlessly from copper pipes. Each workbench held brass pliers, tiny screws, and glass jars glowing with steady amber spark.

"Today you build a simple latch-spring," Jorge announced, clapping his hands. "Measure twice, wind three turns, and test until it holds."

Across the bench, a neat girl with short copper hair and safety spectacles named Nora was already counting gear teeth aloud. "Twenty-four teeth on the wheel, Jorge! Ready for test one!"

Clara tried winding her tiny brass spring, missed a notch, and watched the spring ping straight across the bench into Sam's hair. Frustrated, she cupped her hand and coaxed a quick violet-gold spark from her fingertips to fuse the latch shut in one instant.

A sharp crack echoed off the ceiling. Black soot puffed over her workbench, and the brass spring slumped crooked and useless.

Jorge walked over, picked up the warped spring with copper tongs, and shook his head. "Pure Magic guesses fast and leaves a mess," he said crisply. "In the Path of Makers, you earn the click by testing step by step. Reset your board and measure again."

Clara's cheeks burned hot. Behind her, Nora muffled a snicker. Clara wanted to snap back that raw magic was faster than tiny brass screws, yet she bit her lip and reached for the calipers.

Wednesday brought the Path of Nature inside the Living Dome, where warm indoor rainclouds floated high under the glass roof and clear streams wound around giant oak roots.

"A rope-bridge across the creek has come loose," Marissa told the class as Pip the Bramble-Owl blinked sleepily on a branch above her. "Do not yank the vines. Listen to the root-pulse, ask politely, and weave together."

Maya knelt right into the damp moss beside the stream, grinning as mud splashed her knees. Within two minutes, green tendrils curled gently around Maya's fingers and knotted firmly onto the bridge post.

Clara wanted to catch up fast. She leaned over the next post, pointed both hands at a stubborn vine, and pushed a bright burst of spark toward the leaves, shouting, "Up! Tie onto the wood!"

Instantly the vine whipped backward like an angry spring and dropped Clara flat onto the mossy bank, soaked to her elbows.

Marissa did not laugh, though her calm eyes stayed guarded. "Stop shouting at living things, Clara," she said quietly. "Nature is not a servant. If you bring impatience into the dome, the forest closes its doors."

Clara trudged out of the conservatory with mud on her cloak and a tight knot in her throat. Makers thought she was sloppy. Nature thought she was loud.

By Friday afternoon, when she finally climbed into the midnight-blue observatory of the Starlight Tower, she felt ready to prove she belonged somewhere.

Tall brass constellation rings turned slowly beneath painted stars. At the front of the room stood a neat blond boy wearing a crisp navy-and-silver jacket and dark leather gloves. He looked twelve and carried himself like he already owned the tower.

"Watch closely, first-years," Victor said, lifting his gloved fingers with a smooth smirk. He snapped once. A razor-sharp star of silver-violet light zipped between two hanging mirrors and snuffed three candles without spilling a drop of wax.

"Neat control, Victor," Peter said warmly from the center circle. "Now let us see how the newest students call their spark. Remember: imagine a simple lantern, not a storm."

Clara stepped into the star-circle. She pictured a bright lantern and opened her palm.

Violet and gold light poured out far faster than she expected. It swirled up past her elbows like a rising wind, lifting stray pages off Sam's notebook until Sam ducked behind a bookshelf.

"Whoa, Clara!" Sam yelped, peeking around a stack of charts.

Victor chuckled softly. "Careful, first-year. Try not to set the roof on fire."

Clara panicked, and the swirling light crackled hotter, rattling the glass mirrors along the wall.

Before the surge could snap, a large, steady hand closed gently around Clara's wrist. Peter stood beside her, completely unhurried, his kind eyes crinkling. "Easy now, Clara," he murmured in his deep, reassuring voice. "Fear makes starlight scatter, and pride makes it burn. Breathe out, picture one quiet star, and hold the shape."

Clara breathed out slowly. The wild storm folded inward until a single bright violet-gold star hovered calm and warm above her palm.

As Peter leaned down beside her, the small antique brass sand-timer clipped to his belt gave one tiny, silent shiver against his robes, as if a gust of wind had brushed only the glass. Neither Clara nor Sam noticed it in the glow.

"There she is," Peter said proudly, clapping a gentle hand on her shoulder. "Strong magic takes courage to steer, Clara. You have plenty of both."

For the first time all week, Clara stood tall. She knew right then which bell she was going to ring.
