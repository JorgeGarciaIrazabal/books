# Chapter 9: The Flying Furniture and the Great Crossing

Packing a whole life into three enchanted shrinking trunks turned out to be much funnier on paper than on a Thursday afternoon in New Jersey.

"If anybody stuffs one more bicycle helmet into Trunk Two," Marissa warned, bracing both palms against a trembling oak chest in the hallway, "it is going to spit the toaster right back at us."

*POP!*

The brass clasp flew open like a champagne cork, launching a velvet pillow, three pairs of yellow rain boots, and a startled stuffed dragon straight across the foyer carpet.

"Dragon down!" Clara yelled, diving smoothly to rescue her scarlet plush flyer before her three-year-old cousin Denny could kidnap it as a puppy steed. "Mama, trunks cannot eat dragons! Dragons must ride in my carry-on!"

Around them, the New Jersey house echoed with joyful commotion. Boxes sat stacked cleanly in the living room while new buyers stood admiring the spotless walls, pausing only near the garage door to ask why the concrete floor still hummed faintly with emerald star-crystal dust.

"Just residual computing magic," Jorge explained cheerfully, patting his copper-filled Tinker's Satchel. "Do not worry. All the noisy dragons are coming with us."

Before any trunk could finish sulking, the house filled with the glorious, unmistakable perfume of simmering marrow, golden broth, tender greens, and sweet chickpeas: a feast-sized cauldron of *cocido madrileño*, Marissa's favorite dish on earth, bubbling merrily to wish the Garbanzo family safe crossing.

"Stay back three paces from the ladle," Marissa teased as Jorge wandered within sniffing range. "This is a farewell dinner, not an electrical experiment."

"I am offended," Jorge said with mock dignity, raising both hands in surrender. "I only wanted to admire the thermodynamics."

"Admire them from the salad bowl," Marissa shot back, while Aunt Lidia laughed across the kitchen island, trading lively travel tales from her coastal inn, Ballena Rey, as grandparents passed warm bread around the long dining table.

Under the tablecloth, Clara's cousin Ismael, who strictly preferred being called "Isma" now that he felt grown up, crawled past everyone's sneakers while holding one miniature patrol pup in each hand, trailing toddler Denny in hot pursuit.

"Wait for the Silver Knight!" Clara whispered, slipping down between chairs to crown both cousins temporary deputies of the New Jersey Guard while Marissa refilled mugs and Jorge explained to skeptical uncles why a four-crystal brass chickpea belonged inside his carry-on bag.

"So you are really leaving the black towers behind?" Aunt Lidia asked softly once the broth bowls settled into happy clatter.

Marissa looked toward the kitchen counter, where the Golden Garbanzo's bright brass sunburst key now rested gently inside a velvet travel pouch beside Poki's small silver collar bell and his smooth gray windowsill stone.

"We did not outrun the towers overnight," Marissa answered warmly. "We simmered this pot one ordinary Tuesday at a time. Now it is cooked."

By Saturday dusk, every shrinking trunk finally locked tight with only two spare socks left behind for comedic mystery. Garbanzo hovered solemnly beside Clara's backpack, having successfully compressed his four emerald crystals into travel mode without bursting into a single confetti cannon. On his brass screen glowed one neat line: *Carry-on weight: legal. Spirit weight: feather-light.*

At Newark Gate Fourteen, not a single Cloud-Troll dared whisper a delay. Their silver Atlantic Sky-Vessel lifted right on the minute, climbing steadily above the glowing grids of New Jersey and the distant amber gleam of Manhattan across the river.

Pressed close beside the oval window, Clara held her smooth white backyard pebble in her left palm and Starlight the unicorn tucked under her right arm. Down below, city lamps shrank to spilled glitter before vanishing beneath calm ocean dark.

"Look, Papa," Clara murmured sleepily as the silver wing tilted gently east toward dawn. "No glowing tower screens."

Jorge leaned across the armrest, brushed a stray curl off her cheek, and smiled at Marissa across the aisle.

"Not a single tower screen," Jorge whispered, pulling the thin blanket neatly up over her shoulder.

Behind the wingtip, high above the dark Atlantic clouds, a sliver of moonlit silver trail curved lightly into the night sky and lingered for half a breath before melting cleanly toward Spain.
