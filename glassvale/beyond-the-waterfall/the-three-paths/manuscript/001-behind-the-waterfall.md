# Chapter 1: Behind the Waterfall

The waterfall roared like a hungry giant, and Clara stood three steps from getting soaked.

She pressed her thumb against the smooth river-glass coin in her pocket. Her letter from Glassvale Academy had promised one simple rule: hold the Pass-Token, keep your eyes open, and walk straight through the water.

"Easy for a piece of paper to say," Clara muttered, pushing her straight light-brown bangs out of her eyes.

She tightened the strap of her leather satchel, adjusted her tiny silver-star hair clip, and stepped into the cold white spray.

No freezing water hit her coat. Instead, a warm hum buzzed through her boots, like standing inside a giant ringing bell. Silver mist parted like curtains, and Clara walked out into bright golden morning light, completely dry.

In front of her spread a hidden mountain valley that made her breath catch. Tall terraced waterfalls tumbled past mossy cliffs toward a cluster of soaring towers. Right in the middle stood a grand stone Bell-Tower. Around it rose three very different wings: a copper spire covered in spinning wind-sails, a giant glass dome wrapped around a living oak tree, and a tall midnight-stone tower whose pointed stone crown floated six inches above the roof without touching it.

A sleek carriage painted teal and silver rolled up beside the cobblestone path. The door swung open, and out stepped a tall woman with silver-streaked dark hair in a loose bun and thin silver glasses dangling on a chain.

"Welcome to Glassvale Academy," said Headmistress Monica, smoothing her teal robes with a knowing smile. "I would have sent the gold-plated carriage to meet you, Clara, but only show-offs buy the most expensive things. The second most expensive carriage has much softer seats."

Clara laughed, surprised by how quickly her nervousness melted away. "It looks pretty grand to me."

"Wait until you taste the tea," Monica said, guiding her up the wide stone steps into the Great Hall.

Long oak tables gleamed under tall arched windows. At the nearest table, a boy with wild curly ginger hair and crooked round glasses was staring intently at a blue-and-white teacup. With a quick flick of his fingers, the cup hopped off its saucer, rose two feet into the air, and began wobbling like a spinning top.

"Hold still, hold still," the boy whispered through clenched teeth. A tiny gold spark popped near his nose with a puff of silver smoke, and the teacup tilted sideways.

Clara darted forward and caught the cup with both hands just before the tea could splash onto his burgundy knit sweater.

The boy let out a huge breath. "Technically, floating the teacup worked," he said, adjusting his glasses. "Nobody said anything about catching the tea. I am Sam."

"Clara," she said, setting the cup gently down. "Nice wobble."

"She has fast hands, too!" called a bright voice across the bench. A sporty girl with bouncy dark curls tied back in a mustard-yellow bandana leaned over in an olive utility vest and muddy hiking boots. Perched calmly on her raised wrist sat a tiny, bright turquoise frog. "I am Maya. That little show-off on my arm is Pebble. Watch out if his throat turns gold, because he spots trouble before anyone else."

Pebble blinked big dark eyes and let out one polite squeak.

"Clara, come meet your Path leaders," Monica said, pouring steaming tea from a handsome silver teapot. "My second most expensive teapot, naturally. Keeps the leaves twice as warm."

She led Clara toward three teachers waiting near the high stone hearth.

The first man wore brass goggles pushed up into tousled dark hair and a leather vest stuffed with shiny calipers, screwdrivers, and glowing vials. A small brass clockwork sphere spun slowly above his open palm. "Jorge," he said briskly, squinting at Clara's satchel as if checking how well the buckles were stitched. "Path of Makers. If you break something in my workshop next week, bring me the pieces and a notebook, not excuses."

Beside him knelt a calm woman in an emerald-and-moss cloak clasped with a silver leaf pin. A fluffy, moss-feathered creature that looked half owl and half fox sat on her forearm with bright amber eyes. "I am Marissa, Path of Nature," she said softly, though her gaze measured Clara cleanly from boots to bangs. "Do not rush my greenhouse, and Pip here will let you pass."

Clara stiffened a tiny bit. Both teachers sounded sharp and hard to impress.

Then the third teacher stepped forward. He was a tall, fit, broad-shouldered Black man in tailored deep-plum and gold-trimmed robes, and his smile was so warm and steady that the whole stone hearth seemed brighter.

"Welcome home, Clara," said Peter, Leader of the Path of Pure Magic. "I know the first day feels huge. Take your time. Every great spark begins with one honest step."

A small antique brass sand-timer hung cleanly at his belt, its pale golden sand resting quietly inside the glass. Clara smiled back right away. "Thank you, sir."

Later at dusk, after unpacking in her sunlit tower room, Clara walked back across the courtyard with Maya and Sam to find the dining hall. The evening wind turned sharp near the central Bell-Tower. Just above an iron floor grate, the lantern light flickered blue.

"Did you hear that?" Clara asked, stopping short.

From beneath the iron bars came a thin, papery whisper that sounded eerily like Headmistress Monica's voice: "Come down, Clara. Down here."

Maya froze. On Maya's wrist, Pebble's turquoise throat flashed a sudden, bright warning gold.

Before Clara could step closer, a pale violet moth no bigger than a plum fluttered straight up through the grate, drank the nearest candle flame into darkness, and vanished into the twilight mist.
