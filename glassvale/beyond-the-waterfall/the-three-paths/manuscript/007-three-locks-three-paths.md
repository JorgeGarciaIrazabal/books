# Chapter 7: Three Locks, Three Paths

Cold emerald eyes gleamed up through the stone cracks beneath the belfry floor, and thick black smoke spilled through the air vents like rising water.

Clara threw a quick spark bolt down past the jammed wooden hatch, yet the smoky Ink-Hounds prowling the stairs below merely split around the wild burst and kept climbing.

"Pure Magic alone burns too fast in the dark!" Sam yelled over the thunder banging against the belfry shutters. "Every time you throw a loose spark, it fades before it pushes them back!"

"And the belfry hatch latch is frozen solid from underneath!" Maya added, tugging hard at the bronze ring while wind whistled through the high arched windows above them.

Clara looked up toward the rain-lashed arch. Through the storm, two warm amber pinpricks moved across the courtyard below: Jorge's storm-vane crew checking the Brass Spire cables.

"Sam! Help me send a steady star-signal out the arch!" Clara shouted, bracing her back against the Bell-Tower pillar. "Maya, hold that shutter wide!"

Sam tore a blank diagram page from his notebook, folded it fast into a funnel, and aimed alongside Clara's hands. Together they sent three steady violet-gold rings pulsing straight down into the rainy courtyard.

Down below, Nora spotted the triple ring immediately. Beside her, Ben from Jorge's workshop swung up a heavy brass launch-cradle. Within ten seconds, something small, golden, and furious buzzed straight upward through the rain like an angry hornet.

A palm-sized brass **Clock-Beetle** flew neatly through the open belfry arch, shook raindrops off its copper wings, and clicked twice onto Clara's palm.

Clamped beneath its brass belly sat a coiled copper spark-lens and a waterproof wax note in Jorge's neat handwriting: *Three turns of the brass key, not four! Connect Pure Magic behind the glass, and aim straight!*

"Three turns of the brass key, Clara, not four!" Sam read aloud from Nora's scribbled reminder on the back of the slip. "Four melts the spring!"

Clara knelt beside the jammed floor hatch while the first Ink-Hound's smoking claws scraped the wood right below. Hands steady, she counted out loud over the thunder: "One, two, three!"

She clicked the tiny copper coil onto the back of the beetle's crystal lens and fed a calm, steady thread of violet-gold spark into the copper ring. Instead of scattering wildly, the Makers coil focused her magic into one razor-bright beam of warm starlight that held solid without draining her breath.

The beam sliced straight through the vents and down the dark staircase. Both Ink-Hounds recoiled down the steps, hissing like hot iron in snow, yet smoke still clogged the narrow stairwell and kept the frozen hatch jammed from below.

"I cannot burn through the smoke fast enough!" Clara called out.

"You do not burn wind, Clara!" Maya grinned fiercely, leaning out into the open rain-soaked arch with Pebble glowing like a small turquoise star on her bandana. "You ask it to sweep the room!"

Maya closed her eyes, ignored the terrifying howl of the storm, and rested one palm gently against the wet stone sill. She did not yell at the thunder. She breathed in the smell of wet cedar and ozone, whistled three clear notes like a mountain hawk, and asked the gale politely for one clean breath down the tower.

The storm answered.

A rush of cool, crisp mountain wind plunged straight down through the belfry arches, roared past the Brass, Leaf, and Star bells overhead, and slammed against the belfry hatch behind Clara's blazing starlight beam. The frozen catch snapped clean off, the hatch flew open, and thick shadow-smoke shredded to rags as the trio spilled down into the ground hall just as two familiar voices boomed past the splintering ice of the outer doorway.
