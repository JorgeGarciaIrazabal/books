# Chapter 8: The Last Stand of the Someday Siren

Nothing on the Storm-Bridge matched the strange quiet inside the Amber Terminal Citadel on a Friday afternoon in early spring.

All winter long, Jorge and Marissa had worked straight down that brown binder with real human advisors across both shores, verifying cross-border residency rules, health coverage, and Spanish tax filings line by line until nothing rested on guesswork. Now the tidy binder sat inside Jorge's desk drawer. Every vault had been checked twice without duplicate transfers. The family had a clear plan, a real neighborhood waiting in Valladolid, and an honest margin on the Brass Scale. Only one signature remained: his own.

That was when the glass along the twentieth-floor skyway misted pale gold, and the Someday Siren stepped out of his own reflection.

She did not wear claws or roar fire. She looked terrifyingly reasonable, draped in tailored slate wool, holding a crystal goblet brimming with shimmering spring sunlight.

"Just look around you, Jorge," the Siren murmured softly, gesturing toward the glowing amber pipelines humming smoothly beneath his boots. "Not one Event-Stream is crooked. The Automaton Loom still respects gray-haired architects who keep the dragon cages locked. Stay for one more March bonus. Wait just twelve polite months more. Think how thick the mattress will look with another fifty thousand gold stacked underneath."

Jorge paused with his hand resting on his satchel strap. Fifty thousand gold was not nothing. One more year meant extra buffer against stubborn inflation, extra cushion for flights home, extra comfort without touching a sliver of safety margin. Every cautious habit inside him leaned toward the goblet.

Then the Siren smiled gently and slid home her real dagger.

"Besides," she whispered, leaning near his shoulder until the amber light caught her eyes, "who are you once you turn in your tower badge? On Tuesday morning at nine fifteen, when nobody rings your chime for an emergency schema patch, what will you tell people you do? What if freedom feels... small?"

Silence stretched down the corridor. Down in the bullpen, keyboards ticked steadily. Outside, barges pushed coal and sand down the gray Hudson.

For five long seconds, Jorge looked straight into the Siren's tall glass mirror.

At first, he saw himself wearing the same dark fleece, holding the same travel mug, standing proud and safe inside the tower. Then the silver glass rippled forward by ten winters.

In the future mirror, his hair wore thick streaks of silver. He was still standing inside the exact same hallway, squinting at an amber screen while his pocket voice-crystal buzzed faintly. Across the line came a taller, older teenage voice he barely recognized as Clara's:

*"No worries, Papa. Maybe next summer after your spring release wraps up."*

The cold inside Jorge's chest shattered all at once, and he laughed out loud right in the Siren's face. It was a booming, relieved, slightly ridiculous bark of pure clarity that made two junior engineers across the row jump in their chairs.

"Nice try," Jorge told the mirror, shaking his head. "You nearly sold me a trophy made of other people's calendars."

He reached deep into his coat pocket and laid two objects squarely on the glass sill between them: one stiff loop of **twelve-gauge copper electrical wire** from his evening hands-on workshop, and one scuffed **yellow padel ball** still dusted with blue club turf.

"On Tuesday morning at nine fifteen," Jorge said plainly, "I will be wiring a solar lantern without blowing up my own patio, playing a wicked second set off the back glass, tinkering with four emerald star-crystals on my own schedule, and walking a six-year-old to school before she turns sixteen behind my back. If that sounds small to you, you have never tried hitting a decent lob."

He pulled the **Crimson Resignation Seal** from his wallet and stamped it straight onto the corner of his Citadel pass.

*Thunk.*

The skyscraper stayed completely still. The amber pipes hummed on without weeping. The Someday Siren simply blinked once, stepped back into plain empty window glass, and dissolved into ordinary New York afternoon glare.

Three rivers and twenty miles west, in a warm New Jersey kitchen, Marissa had just set a bowl of strawberries down beside Clara's crayons when the heirloom copper pot at the back of the range began to sing.

A deep, joyful musical ring replaced the old clank, rising like a brass church bell wrapped in cinnamon steam.

Clara leaped straight onto a kitchen chair. "Mama! Look at the lid!"

The heavy copper lid floated two inches upward on a pillar of golden broth-light. Rising straight from the center, the Golden Garbanzo cracked cleanly down its seam and unfurled into a dazzling brass key shaped like a sunburst, sending warm scent of roasted chickpeas, saffron, and morning sunlight rolling through every corner of the house.

Garbanzo hovered beside the silverware drawer, weeping tiny drops of hot oil out of sheer pride. "Kettle readiness: one hundred percent! No duplicate entries detected!"

Down in Manhattan, Jorge stepped out through the revolving glass doors onto Fifty-Ninth Street into cold spring air, zipped his coat up to his chin, and did not once look back up at the amber windows.
