# Chapter 3: The Whisper Beneath the Stairs

On Saturday evening, every lantern in Glassvale burned gold for the Bell-Choosing Ceremony.

First-years gathered inside the circular hall of the central Bell-Tower. High in the rafters hung three ancient bells: a hammered Brass Bell etched with gears, a wide Leaf Bell carved from living green bronze, and a shimmering Star Bell cut from pale sky-stone.

When Nora stepped forward, she gave the Brass Bell one brisk pull. A clean, bright ring filled the hall, and Jorge pinned a tiny brass wheel onto her collar.

Maya marched up next in clean boots, pulled the Leaf Bell's woven vine rope, and smiled wide as a warm, deep forest chime rolled overhead like distant thunder. Even Pebble hopped proudly on her shoulder while Marissa fastened a silver leaf pin to Maya's olive vest.

Then Sam tugged the Star Bell's silver cord with both hands, squinting nervously behind his crooked glasses. A high, singing starlight note rippled through the air, lifting his ginger curls straight up for one funny second before settling down.

Clara stepped forward last. Her hands felt damp against her slate-blue cloak, yet she wrapped her fingers around the silver cord and pulled.

The Star Bell sang clear and strong, sending bright violet sparks dancing around the rafters like fireflies. Monica clapped warmly from the high balcony, and Peter pinned the shining star emblem onto Clara's cloak with a proud nod.

"Path of Pure Magic," Peter said warmly. "Use it wisely."

Later that night, when the dormitories went quiet, Clara slipped out to the balcony to cool her excited cheeks. Down in the courtyard, she spotted Maya crouched near the shrubbery with Sam tiptoeing right behind her, clutching his Star-Notebook.

Clara hurried down the side stairs. "What are you two doing out after lights?"

"Pebble went bright gold again," Maya whispered, pointing toward the Starlight Tower's lower staircase. "Look. Every candle on those steps just went out by itself."

Sam pushed his glasses up his nose and checked a sketched map in his notebook. "Wait. That staircase leads down to the Deep Gate. Peter told us nobody goes down there. It seals off old Shadow Magic vaults from two hundred years ago."

"Then why are Whisper-Moths flying up from it?" Clara asked softly.

Three pale-violet moths drifted past the stone archways. Where their powdery wings brushed the air, frost formed on the moss. Worst of all, soft voices curled around the pillars, whispering Clara's name in Sam's voice, then Maya's name in Monica's voice, trying to pull them apart toward separate dark corners.

"Stick together!" Maya hissed, grabbing Clara's sleeve with one hand and Sam's sleeve with the other. "Ignore the voices. Watch Pebble's light!"

They crept down the narrow spiral stairs until wrought-iron bars blocked the corridor ahead. Beyond the gate lay a drowned shadow hall where eerie emerald-violet mist swirled ankle-deep across cracked flagstones.

A tall figure stood just inside the iron gate.

He wore a rain-dark slate-gray coat with a high storm collar and a smooth silver-etched mask that hid everything except two cold, narrow eyes. When he raised one gloved hand, the Whisper-Moths settled quietly onto his sleeves like sleeping leaves.

Clara's heart hammered against her ribs. She wanted to raise a spark, yet every warm thought felt sluggish near that green-purple fog.

The masked man tilted his head toward the iron bars, studying the fresh silver star pin on Clara's cloak. He did not shout or rush. Slowly, he pressed two gloved fingers against the lock. Sparks of sickly dark frost crackled over the iron, testing its strength, before a distant bell chimed midnight far above.

With one sharp gesture, the intruder stepped backward into the swirling green-violet mist and vanished completely, as if the stone floor had swallowed him whole.

"Wait!" Sam squeaked, holding up a trembling ball of starlight. "Look by the gate!"

Lying right beside the wrought-iron threshold was a heavy black-iron coin stamped with an empty ring, cold enough to numb Clara's fingers even through her sleeve.

"Cold-Iron," Maya breathed, her eyes wide. "Someone forged that in the dark side of Makers. Why is a shadow wizard carrying dark Makers tools?"

Clara stared down into the empty mist, shivering from head to toe. For the first time since walking through the waterfall, Glassvale did not feel completely safe.
