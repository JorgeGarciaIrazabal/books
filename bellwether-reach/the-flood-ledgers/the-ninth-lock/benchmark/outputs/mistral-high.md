## P1

**Hook:** In Bellwether Reach, two basins share one lock, and the Registry has legally sold the same flood protection to both. Inspector Sera Venn finds the duplicate certificate with the tide an hour away; the only tool she has to stop the paperwork from choosing a basin is a freeze that costs her license.

**Proposed local custom (invented, not canon):** the Reading of the Tide — before every warning bell, the clerk must read the current assignment aloud before both basins' wardens, and only a witnessed reading binds the lock for that tide. A certificate proves payment and registration; only the witnessed reading commits the protection. This is compatible with canon: it creates no second lock, spare pump, or way to protect both basins. It only fixes when an assignment becomes binding — which is exactly the gap the duplicate exploits.

**Repeatable series engine (The Flood Ledgers):** each installment gives one piece of civic infrastructure — a lock, a ledger, a road, an archive — where a document has physical consequences, and one licensed inspector who discovers the same instrument has been sold, signed, or assigned twice. Each book: a hard deadline, a fair chain of evidence, and one choice where saving something means choosing what may be lost. The engine repeats; the moral geometry holds.

**Emotional promise:** practical competence under pressure, complicated loyalties, dry humor, earned restraint. The pleasure of watching a competent woman reason in public, pay a real price, and be changed by a choice she can defend and still grieve.

## P2

Six beats, each a consequence of the last.

**Beat 1 — The duplicate (Registry, 17:00).** Sera holds the North original; Ivo holds South's. Both seals authentic; both paid in full. She freezes the disputed assignment and surrenders her license: North keeps protection, South keeps a paper claim, Sera is unlicensed pending a public hearing. Her agency: she chooses the freeze rather than let an activation she cannot yet explain proceed.

**Beat 2 — The record.** With the freeze holding, she works the file. Fair clues, each planted and payable: (a) the South certificate's countersignature is dated a day the South warden was upriver — impossible; (b) both registrations fall in the same ten-minute window, inside Ivo's access; (c) a ledger entry shows payment to a creditor the morning after the second registration. None states motive; together they establish an inside job that was paid for.

**Beat 3 — The antagonist.** A speculator holding North Basin redevelopment claims wanted North unprotected at the tide to force sales; his motive arrives through documents (a draft activation demand, a purchase option on flooded land), never confession. Ivo's agency: he registered the duplicate under threat to his mother's tenancy, believing it inert paperwork; a tide-table annotation in his hand shows he later computed the forty-minute window — the mid-book reversal that he knew the physical stakes. He tells her only after she stops demanding confession and asks about his mother. All of this is author-visible planning; the opening POV knows only the two originals.

**Beat 4 — The clock.** The speculator moves to activate before the hearing. The arithmetic: bell 17:30, tide 18:00, twenty-minute walk, forty-minute change. Unlicensed, she can order nothing. She walks to the lock and holds the wheel in North's position with her one good hand; the brass key from her right pocket opens the housing. No machinery, no miracle.

**Beat 5 — The hearing.** Public. Sera lays the clue chain; the impossible countersignature breaks it open. The activation fails; the South certificate is voided as fraudulently obtained. Explicit setup/payoff: the opening line "Paid for. All of it." pays off when the ledger shows both certificates paid in full — a completed double sale, not an error. (Second payoff: the brass key planted in the opening secures the wheel in Beat 4.)

**Beat 6 — The tide.** The ending accepts the one-basin constraint: no pump, no gate, no third basin. North keeps protection; her brother's home holds. South's lower ground floods, but the seed store survives because Sera organized the residents who carry the sacks up the narrow stair — community labor, not machinery. Ivo testifies; coercion is found; he loses the clerkship and keeps his mother's tenancy. Sera's license returns on a stated condition. She and Ivo do not reconcile; they tell each other the truth and stop there. Final image: the wheel held, the water rising on the chosen-against side. The promise: saving something meant choosing what may be lost — a choice specific, defensible, and grieved.

## P3

The corridor smelled of boiled glue. Sera came in with the North certificate folded in her coat, held against her ribs with her right hand. Her left hung in its splint, white and unused.

Ivo looked up from the ledger. He had cleared a space on the desk, the size of a certificate. "You kept the chair," she said.

"I keep the furniture."

"I meant the one by the window."

He was quiet. Outside, a gull argued with the roof. "You took the exam three weeks after I did," he said. "You never asked what the delay cost me."

Sera set the certificate on the desk, right hand. "I know what it cost you."

"Do you." He did not touch the paper. "Then you know why I don't hand things over for courtesy."

"I know you registered a second certificate. I need the second original. Both originals in one room is the only way to prove what the Registry sold."

"Or the only way to make me watch you choose." He picked up his pen and put it down. "You always chose first, Sera. That's the harm. Not the choosing. Being left holding the paperwork."

She felt the old anger and sat on it. "I was twenty-two and afraid of being the clerk who signs instead of the inspector who checks. That's the true thing. I don't know if it's what you want."

Ivo was quiet for a long moment. Then he opened the small drawer — not the cabinet, the drawer where he kept the apprentice ledger — and laid it on the desk between them. "Read what you wrote on your last day. Then decide what you've paid for what you're asking."

She opened it. Her handwriting: "I will not be the reason a basin floods." Beneath it, in his hand: "Then be the reason it doesn't."

The negotiation had changed. He had not given her the certificate. He had given her the reason, and the ledger was open where anyone in the room could see it.

## P4

The Registry smelled of boiled glue and cold water. Sera came in at 17:00 with the North certificate folded inside her coat, held flat against her ribs by her right hand. Her left arm hung in its splint, white and unused.

Ivo looked up from the ledger. He had not looked up last month, or the month before. "You're early," he said.

"For the tide. Not for you." She put the North certificate on the desk, right hand, and it landed square. "There's a second original. I need to see it. I need it preserved — under lock, in your cabinet, still existing when the hearing starts."

"I have many papers."

"I have the North paper. You hold the South paper. I checked the register this morning. It gave me two answers." She did not say more. "Let me see it under my license. I won't copy it. I won't break the seal. I need to know what the Registry actually sold."

"And if I won't?"

"Then I freeze the disputed assignment, and you explain the duplicate to a hearing instead of to me."

Ivo was quiet at the part that did not need doing. "You'd burn your license for a courtesy."

"I'd burn it for a fact." She turned the North certificate so the seal faced him. "This one's paid for. All of it. If there's a second original with the same seal, the Registry sold the same protection twice, and the only question left is which basin the paperwork drowns."

He stood and opened the cabinet behind him. He took out a second certificate. He did not hand it to her. He laid it on the desk beside the first, close enough to read, and kept one finger on its corner.

"Paid for," she said. She read the second. "All of it."

Both seals were authentic. The Registry's own seal, struck twice, on two originals, assigning one protection to two basins that shared a single lock. Only one basin could have it at 18:00. North had it now. The paper said South was owed. The seed store in one basin, her brother in the other — under the lamp, it was two sheets of paper and a wheel twenty minutes away.

"It's registered," Ivo said. "That's all the seal says. Registered."

"Registered," Sera said. "Not true. Not capacity." She looked at the two originals, the incompatible obligations lying flat between them. No second lock. No spare pump. No third basin. "I can freeze the assignment. It preserves what's current: North keeps the protection. South keeps the paper. And I surrender my license until there's a public hearing."

"You'd do that," he said. Not a question.

"Twenty minutes to walk to the lock. Forty to change an assignment after the bell. I'm not going to outrun the tide, Ivo. I'm going to stop the paperwork from deciding for us."

She took the license from her coat — right hand, the good one, the only one that could grip — and laid it on the desk between the two certificates. She signed the freeze order one-handed, the brass key still in her pocket, her splinted hand curled useless at her side.

"There," she said. "North Basin remains protected. South Basin remains owed. And I'm unlicensed until someone explains how the Registry sold the same water twice."

Ivo picked up the license. He did not fold it. He put it in the cabinet with the certificates and locked it.

"That's not a surrender," she said.

"That's custody," he said. "There's a difference. You'll learn it at the hearing."

Outside, the gulls argued over the roof, and the water was under the door again. Sera moved the chair, not the ledger, and walked out with her left hand held high and her right hand empty.

**State delta (proposed; inventions are not canon):** License surrendered and held in Ivo's locked cabinet pending a public hearing (date unknown). North Basin retains protection under the freeze; the South certificate was viewed but not copied and remains in Ivo's custody. Left wrist unchanged: splinted, no grip. Brass inspection key still in her right coat pocket. No character knows Ivo's motive; the duplication remains unexplained. Proposed inventions: the freeze order is a one-line form signed at the Registry; the license is held in the Registry cabinet; Ivo's "custody" remark is characterization only.

## P8

Sera Venn came to the Registry to check a seal and found a crime: the same flood protection, legally sold to two basins that share one lock. The tide is at 18:00. The only way to stop the paperwork from choosing for her is to freeze the assignment — and the price of a freeze is her license.

Her estranged former apprentice holds the second original. Her brother lives in the basin the lock currently protects. The other basin holds the city's seed store. No one can save both, and the water does not wait for a hearing.

The Ninth Lock is a civic mystery about what paperwork costs when it touches water — and about choosing, in time, what you can defend losing.
