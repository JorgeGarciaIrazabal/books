# 9. The Other Side

Two flat boxes lay open outside the shed. One held folded bunting. The other was full of empty seed trays.

Neither held the moon.

“Mrs. Bell wrote MOON on an edge,” Mila told Aunt June. “There could be two messages on the same box.”

Noah showed her the drawing.

“Then let's turn the next ones so you can read them,” June said.

She and Mr. Moss brought out a broad carton. Its front edge said PAPER. Its back edge said BLUE POTS.

Inside were paintbrushes.

“That box needs a new name,” Noah said.

“Several fewer names,” Mr. Moss agreed.

Mila waited beside the wall as Dad helped June lift another carton down. It was shallow and square, with yellow wool crossing its middle.

On the edge facing Mila were three words.

TO THE GARDEN.

She didn't reach for the knot.

“Can you turn it?” she asked.

Dad and June turned the box slowly between them.

On the opposite edge, four black letters came into view.

Mila read them before the box had stopped moving.

“MOON.”

Noah let out one long breath. Mila hadn't known he was holding it.

They laid the carton flat on the cleared bench. June loosened the wool. The knot had pulled tight, so Mr. Moss fetched a small pair of scissors and snipped it.

Dad lifted the lid.

White tissue paper curved around the round frame. Mila knew the place where two sheets met in a slightly crooked line. She had pasted that part herself.

“There you are,” she said.

She hadn't meant to speak to it as if it were a lost cat.

Noah touched the wooden frame with one finger. Then he grinned at her across the box.

Mila grinned back so hard that her cheeks hurt.

The moon had been in the garden all this time.

Mr. Moss looked from one label to the other.

“I never turned it around,” he said. “I saw where it was meant to go and took it there.”

He pointed to the old message. Mila looked at the new one, hidden against the shed wall until now.

“It had two different jobs,” she said.

“And I gave it the wrong one.”

“We can fix the writing,” Noah said.

Mila looked along the yellow wool. One loose end was ragged. A little part could easily have caught on the table.

The wool hadn't told her whose box it was. The wheel hadn't told her what was inside. But they had brought her here.

“Shall I take it back on the trolley?” Mr. Moss asked.

Mila started to say no. Then she looked at the wide box and the muddy path.

“If we put the lid on carefully,” she said.

Dad was still looking inside.

“There's a small tear here.”

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

Near the lower edge, the tissue had split in a short, crooked line. A cardboard tab inside the lid pressed against the same spot.

Mila's smile slipped.

Noah bent to look. “It's not the frame.”

“No,” Dad said. “Just the paper. We can patch it.”

June folded the tab away and taped it flat so it couldn't catch again. Mila watched her hands. She wanted to get the moon home, but she didn't want to rush it into another problem.

“I'll tell Mrs. Bell,” Dad said, taking out his phone.

Mila picked up the notebook. Under the drawing of the two-sided box, she wrote *Found*.

It was a lovely short word. She pressed so hard the pencil nearly went through the page.

Noah put his ruler under the corner before she could make a hole.

Then Mr. Moss went to fetch the trolley, and Mila stayed beside the open box, keeping the lid from blowing shut.
