# Style
Close third person, past tense, Mila's attention and knowledge only. Warm, lightly funny, concrete, read-aloud prose. Most sentences use familiar words; vary length and let dialogue carry feeling. Explain rare terms naturally. American English; curly quotes acceptable. Dialogue speakers need clear anchors. No repetitive chapter-ending threats, moral summaries, or robotic clue lists. Noah is a full partner with different strengths, not merely an admiring helper. Adults can be busy without being foolish. Prose is for age eight with parent support, not an early phonics reader.

Author update, 2026-10-25: avoid prose habits that feel machine-written. Use no em dashes in narration or dialogue. Use double quotation marks for English speech and guillemets («…») for Spanish speech, with clear speaker paragraphs and correct attribution punctuation. This overrides the earlier Spanish dialogue-dash direction. Follow `.studio/craft.md` on concrete language, varied rhythm, redundant explanation, stock phrasing, and keeping assistant/process material out of the story. Preserve the mystery, humor, and natural voice; do not add deliberate mistakes or generic “humanizing” flourishes.

Use a level-one chapter heading. Four page units per chapter separated by the exact standalone marker <!-- PAGEBREAK -->. The marker is layout metadata, never printed. Each unit should contain about 145–180 words (allow 125–195 when scene rhythm requires). Keep paragraphs short enough to fit an illustrated 6 x 9 inch page at readable type size. End pages at paragraph boundaries. No artificial recap at page turns.
