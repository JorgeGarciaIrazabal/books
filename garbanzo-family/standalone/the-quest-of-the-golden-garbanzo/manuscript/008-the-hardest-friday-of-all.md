# Chapter 8: The Hardest Friday of All

Over the cold weeks of February and early March, real accountants and lawyers checked every sheet inside Jorge's brown folder. They sorted out tax treaty forms, reviewed health coverage, and checked visa rules. Finally, they gave Jorge and Marissa the green light:

"Your numbers are solid. You are good to go."

Now there was only one door left to walk out of. And on a sunny early-spring Friday morning in Manhattan, that heavy glass office door felt a thousand pounds heavier than it looked.

At ten-thirty, Jorge's manager Dan asked him into a glass conference room high above the river. Beyond the tall windows, silver ferry boats sliced calmly across bright blue water.

"Close the door, Jorge," Dan smiled warmly, sliding a folded white sheet across the oak table. "We heard you might make a big move. Wait just one more year to guide the team through these new AI tools. We will raise your pay and hand you a huge special bonus next March."

Jorge stared at the paper. His heart thumped hard against his ribs.

It was honest, generous money. Staying meant keeping his close work friends, his big job title, and a steady paycheck every second Friday like clockwork.

Wait just twelve tiny months? What could possibly go wrong with taking one more March bonus for extra safety?

For ten long quiet seconds, fear wrapped warmly around his chest like an old heavy coat. Without this job badge clipped to his belt, who would he even be on Monday morning? Just a guy in sneakers carrying a toolbox?

Slowly, Jorge slipped his right hand inside his jacket pocket and rested his left hand on the gym duffel beside his chair.

His fingertips brushed three familiar things.

First came the folded drawing he had carried since November, showing Dad grinning in sunlight with a wrench and yellow ball above six crooked crayon words: `STOP LETTING WORK EAT TUESDAYS.`

Under his left palm, through the side mesh pocket of his duffel, pressed a real fuzzy yellow padel ball.

And tucked inside his jacket pocket beside Clara's paper sat a short bend of bright copper wire left over from fixing a basement light switch on Sunday.

Suddenly Jorge pictured himself sitting in this exact same glass conference room ten years from now. Gray hair at his temples. Reading a polite text from sixteen-year-old Clara saying, *"It's okay, Dad, maybe we can hang out next summer when work slows down."*

The fear melted straight off his shoulders like morning fog.

Jorge exhaled, looked up with clear bright eyes, and smiled gently across the table at his boss.

"Dan, thank you so much," Jorge said honestly. "You have been awesome to work for, and this place taught me everything. That bonus is super generous. Still, someone eager and rising on our team deserves that seat way more than I do now. I'm going home to spend my Tuesdays with my family."

He reached into his leather bag, pulled out a clean crisp envelope, and laid his polite two-week resignation letter smoothly beside Dan's bonus sheet.

Dan stared down at the paper for two seconds, then let out a low admiring whistle and stood up to shake Jorge's hand hard. "You know what? Half of us upstairs talk about having the guts to do this for twenty years and never push the button. Give Marissa and Clara my best. Have an amazing life in Spain, friend."

Riding the evening train home under pink cherry blossoms along the tracks, Jorge felt strangely light. It felt as if someone had lifted fifty pounds of wet bricks out of his backpack.

When he pushed open his front door at six-thirty, Marissa didn't even ask how the meeting went. She just stood smiling barefoot beside the kitchen island, holding a mug of tea, pointing silently toward the toaster.

*DING!*

Clean and clear as a tiny brass chime, the little copper lid popped half an inch into the air and settled softly to one side.

Jorge and Marissa stepped closer together, peering down inside. Garbanzo tiptoed quietly over on his four stubby green metal feet, bowing his round head cleanly without saying a single noisy word.

Where the golden garbanzo had rested for years, two golden shells now lay open like a tiny walnut cradle. Nestled inside gleamed a small, warm gold charm shaped like a sunburst on a brass key ring.

Jorge picked it up between his fingers. It felt warm as a coin sitting in Spanish noon sun.

"Guess our timing wasn't bad," Marissa grinned softly, slipping her arm around Jorge's waist.

Just then, Clara burst through the front door, dropped her backpack, and spotted the glowing charm. She let out a whoop loud enough to rattle every glass on the drying rack.
