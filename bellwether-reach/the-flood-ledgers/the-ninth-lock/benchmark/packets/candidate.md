# Applicable craft contract
# Craft standards

Start from the author's desired reading experience. A beautiful line cannot repair
a false claim or a character making a choice solely because the outline needs it.

## Fiction

- Give scenes a lived situation, a desire, resistance, and a consequential change.
  A quiet conversation can turn a relationship; spectacle is not required.
- Let character choices cause consequences. Test plausible alternatives and why
  this person does not choose them. Antagonists need aims and constraints of their own.
- Ground the viewpoint in selective attention and embodied experience. Maintain
  psychic distance intentionally. A secret in the world bible is not POV knowledge.
- Dialogue is action: competing aims, evasion, subtext, silence, power, and changing
  tactics. Distinct voice comes from worldview and syntax, not catchphrase quotas.
- Make setting affect choices, costs, work, culture, and daily life. Explain only
  what the reader needs at the point they need it. Track the cost of extraordinary powers.
- Vary scene shape, sentence rhythm, and emotional movement. Do not force every
  chapter to end in danger, summarize its lesson, or resolve its emotion explicitly.
- Plant payoffs fairly. Distinguish deliberate ambiguity from missing information.
  Pay the book's central promise even when larger series questions remain open.
- Earn emotion through specificity, conflict, restraint, and consequence. Avoid
  interchangeable gestures, decorative metaphors, repeated realizations, and
  exposition disguised as dialogue. Never use a banned-word list as an editor.
- Preserve intentional strangeness, dialect, humor, genre pleasures, and ambiguity.
  Editing should sharpen the author's voice rather than produce house-model prose.

## Nonfiction

- Specify the reader's starting knowledge, the thesis, and what changes for them.
  Structure each section around a useful question, argument, explanation, or action.
- Map substantive claims to sources actually inspected, at the passage/table level.
  Quote accurately and briefly; distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and interpretation.
- Present the strongest relevant counterargument. Do not promote correlation into
  causation or present anecdotes as representative evidence.
- Mark uncertain, disputed, dated, jurisdiction-specific, and inferred statements.
  Preserve denominators, units, sample boundaries, and source qualifications.
- Label composite or invented examples. Do not invent lived experience or interviews.
- Examples, definitions, diagrams, exercises, and summaries should solve a reader
  problem; use only those that help. Validate worked examples and calculations.

## Editing discipline

Read for experience before diagnosis. State where attention, trust, curiosity, or
comprehension changes and cite the text. Offer a few high-impact changes, not a
laundry list. Protect successful passages and distinguish intentional effects from
accidents. One meaningful structural experiment is better than endless abstract debate.

For close imitation requests, translate references into broad craft traits and
develop an original voice. Track permissions for quotes, images, and commissioned
assets in the production brief. A model is not a substitute for a real lived-experience
reader, credentialed expert, permissions owner, or human proofreader when one is needed.

# Shared inputs
# The Ninth Lock — benchmark brief
Universe: Bellwether Reach. Series: The Flood Ledgers. Book 1, original adult speculative mystery, planned novel of 70,000–85,000 words; this assignment is a bounded sample benchmark, not a whole novel.
Reader promise: a fair civic mystery in which paperwork has physical consequences and saving something means choosing what may be lost. Sera Venn discovers that the same lock protection was legally sold to both basins. She has until the tide to expose how and choose a defensible action.
Audience: adult readers who enjoy practical competence, complicated loyalties, dry humor, and earned emotional restraint. Avoid modern policy jargon, cartoon villains, convenient new machinery, magical truth tests, and treating South Basin residents as faceless sacrifices.
The opening tests whether Sera can make estranged clerk Ivo release the duplicate certificate while risking her license. The crime, motive, and eventual ending are for structure candidates to propose within the fixed facts. No commercial or market claims are needed.
Success: concrete causality, distinguishable people, legible evidence, material consequences, an actual choice, and language worth reading. Rankings remain comparative preferences on these samples, not numerical literary quality scores.

# Style
Close third person, past tense, Sera’s viewpoint. Concrete selective observation through a working inspector’s attention; restrained feeling; occasional dry humor. Dialogue pursues conflicting goals, with room for silence. Vary rhythm. Avoid explaining a successful image immediately afterward.
US spelling and double quotation marks; Registry and basin names capitalized. Preserve intentional fragments, especially “Paid for. All of it.” Do not normalize voice into generic polished prose.
Reference rhythm (director-written): “The water was under the door again. Sera moved the chair, not the ledger.”
A second reference: “Ivo had ruled a line through her name. Neatly. He had always been good at the part that did not need doing.”

# Fixed canon
- FACT-0001: North Basin and South Basin share the Ninth Lock. Only one basin can receive its protection during a given high tide; changing the assignment after the warning bell takes forty minutes.
- FACT-0002: The next high tide is at 18:00. The warning bell is at 17:30. Walking from the Registry to the Ninth Lock takes twenty minutes; no faster transport is available in the test scene.
- FACT-0003: Sera Venn is a licensed lock inspector. Her left wrist is broken and splinted; she cannot grip with that hand. She carries one brass inspection key in her right coat pocket.
- FACT-0004: Ivo Renn is the Registry clerk and Sera’s estranged former apprentice. He legally registered two incompatible protection certificates. His motives and any coercion remain unknown to Sera and the reader at the opening.
- FACT-0005: The two certificates have the same authentic Registry seal. The seal proves registration, not truth or available capacity.
- FACT-0006: Sera may freeze a disputed assignment only by surrendering her inspector’s license pending a public hearing. A freeze preserves the current assignment; it does not protect both basins. North Basin currently has protection.
- FACT-0007: Sera’s brother lives in North Basin. South Basin contains the city’s communal seed store. Either loss matters; no undisclosed gate, spare pump, miracle, or secret third basin can protect both before this tide.

Desire: Sera needs to see and preserve the second original. Resistance: Ivo risks blame and will not hand it over for a professional courtesy. [P1] At five o’clock Sera reached the Registry with the North certificate folded inside her coat. The corridor smelled of boiled glue. Ivo had set a second chair beside his desk, although their last conversation had ended with her telling him never to waste her time again. She remained standing.

[P2] “You have the South paper,” she said. Ivo spread his hands over the desk. “I have many papers.” He asked whether she had eaten. She told him that her brother had planted beans in North Basin, as though the beans were a legal argument. Ivo moved the spare chair back under the desk.

[P3] A girl came in carrying two mugs. Sera had never seen her before. The girl set a mug beside Sera and said, “There’s another certificate. It’s under the blue blotter. The clerk signed it to pay his mother’s debt.” Then she left. Ivo said nothing. Sera lifted the blotter. Both seals were authentic. She immediately understood the entire fraud.

[P4] “I will freeze this,” Sera said. “My license is the price.” She put her license on the desk, where the wet ring from her mug spread into the photograph. Ivo covered it with a square of clean paper. He told her North would remain protected. For a moment she wished he had shouted at her.

[P5] At the Ninth Lock she found the North wheel locked in place. South’s residents were already carrying seed sacks up the narrow stair, two to a sack. Sera saw one split and lent her good hand to hold its mouth shut. The woman carrying it thanked her without looking up.

[P6] By the next morning everyone had forgiven Ivo. The girl had explained the fraud at a hearing held offstage, and the council had quietly bought a spare pump, which saved both basins. Sera got her license back. She and Ivo agreed that honesty mattered more than rules. She felt, at last, that she was home.
