## P1

The Ninth Lock begins with an inspector asking her former apprentice for a piece of paper he cannot afford to give her. Two basins have bought the same protection. Both certificates are authentic. Sera Venn must establish who sold an impossible promise while deciding which legitimate claim the city will honor—and putting her own standing behind that decision.

For this adult speculative mystery, the distinctive pleasure is practical work under moral pressure: following custody, checking an entry, noticing what a clerk has carefully left intact. The city’s paperwork changes where water goes; discovering the truth does not create more capacity.

**Proposed local custom:** households leave a tide tally beside their doors, naming what they will carry first if protection fails. Inspectors must read these tallies on their rounds. A prosperous street lists furniture; a crowded lodging house lists people who need help down stairs. The custom gives Sera useful evacuation knowledge while making every assignment visibly particular. It also lets residents challenge the priorities an inspector assumes for them.

The series engine is a new disputed civic obligation in each book: a document confers a real benefit that cannot reach everyone entitled to it. Each mystery resolves its deception and immediate allocation, while consequences accumulate across Sera’s professional relationships.

The emotional promise is competence without innocence: Sera can become more trustworthy without becoming blameless, and reconciliation can require a public correction rather than a private apology.

## P2

Proposed whole-book plan; motives below are author-visible, unavailable to the opening viewpoint.

1. **Secure the contradiction.** At 17:00, Ivo refuses professional courtesy from the woman who once made his mistake public and her own part private. Sera acknowledges her responsibility in writing; he produces South’s original. Matching authentic seals establish registration of incompatible obligations, not the culprit. Sera surrenders her license to freeze the assignment. North remains protected, including her brother; she must answer to South for that choice.

2. **Follow the money before the bell.** Instead of spending the remaining minutes pursuing a theory, Sera takes both originals toward the lock and reads their payment endorsements en route. South’s fee was received against a balance marked already discharged. Setup: Ivo refuses to let her call the repeated balance number a copying mistake. He registered both papers and insists that his entries remain intact. She cannot yet tell whether he is protecting evidence or himself.

3. **Make the loss particular.** Arriving before 17:30, Sera finds South residents moving seed upstairs. They demand an assignment change. She explains the freeze and its price, admitting that her brother benefits. They organize salvage on their own terms; she supplies inspection knowledge and labor without claiming to have settled their grievance. At 18:00 North is protected. South floods. Some seed survives; much is lost. The discovery cannot reverse this loss.

4. **Build a case without a license.** At the public hearing, the treasurer calls the duplicate an unauthorized clerical act. Ivo could accept a quiet dismissal. Instead, he produces the payment book bearing the same balance number. Payoff: the repeated number joins South’s receipt to a North obligation already paid. Testimony establishes who ordered that allocation; seals alone never establish intent.

5. **Expose a defensible aim pursued fraudulently.** The treasurer spent North’s payment covering overdue civic wages, then sold South protection to conceal the resulting shortfall. He expected South to accept a refund before tide. Ivo admits registering the second obligation on that assurance, without obtaining South’s consent. His decision to testify costs him the promised protection of his post. Sera submits her own freeze decision for scrutiny alongside the fraud.

6. **Settle what can be settled.** The hearing assigns responsibility and restitution through recoverable funds and future assessments, with residents contesting the terms. Sera regains authority only after a finding on her conduct; Ivo’s accountability remains separate from hers. They undertake an inventory of the damaged seed together. The records become accurate. The missing seed remains missing.

## P3

Ivo had put the South certificate beneath his elbow. Sera could see the corner, dry and square, beyond the damp rim of his mug.

“Move your arm.”

He moved it onto the paper.

She kept her splint clear of the desk. “I need the original.”

“You used to say a copy was sufficient.”

“For checking an entry.”

“Yes.”

The little word had a familiar precision. She had taught him to leave a bad answer room to spoil itself.

She laid North’s certificate down with her right hand. He glanced at its seal, then at the empty chair beside her.

“There are two obligations,” she said. “There is one lock.”

“I can count.”

“I know.”

He lifted his mug. Beneath it lay a circular stain and an old correction slip. Her handwriting. He had kept that.

She read the first line upside down. Clerk’s failure to verify.

“You could have asked me to change it.”

Ivo set the mug back exactly within the stain.

Sera looked at the chair. Sitting would make getting up awkward, with her wrist like this. She sat.

“Give me a pen.”

He offered it point first, caught himself, turned it.

On the correction slip she crossed out Clerk’s and wrote Inspector’s instruction; clerk followed it. Below, she signed her name. Her right hand had no excuse for its poor work.

Ivo read it twice.

“I’ll put that in the book,” he said.

“Do.”

He slid South’s certificate free of his elbow but kept two fingers on it.

“On the desk,” he said. “Beside yours. You don’t take it away until we’ve both read it.”

“Beside mine,” she agreed.

He released the paper. She waited for him to turn it toward her.

## P4

The Registry clock had five minutes’ disagreement with the clock outside. Sera trusted the one outside. She inspected it twice a year.

Five o’clock. An hour until high tide.

Ivo had placed a mug beside the visitor’s chair. She moved it away from the edge before she remembered she was angry with him.

“You have South’s certificate.”

“Good afternoon.”

She took North’s original from her coat with her right hand. Her left wrist knocked the chair back. Pain traveled up to her elbow and stayed there, considering its accommodation.

“Put it beside this.”

Ivo looked at the paper. His hands remained flat on his desk.

“You can submit a request.”

“And receive an answer when?”

“That depends on the request.”

She turned North’s certificate toward him. The Registry seal caught on a ridge in the wood. She lifted the corner clear. There were ways to damage evidence while winning an argument.

“I’m asking to examine the second original.”

“You’re asking me to put it in your hand.”

“Yes.”

“And then you’ll leave.”

Outside, someone tested the shutters. One bang, then another. Ivo’s gaze followed the sound to the window, but he did not get up.

“I won’t remove it without a record,” she said.

“Your record.”

He had learned custody from her. He had also learned what happened when an inspector went home and a clerk remained behind the desk.

She drew the chair out with her good hand and sat.

“Yours, then.”

Ivo opened a book. He wrote the date, her name, the hour. At the entry for purpose, his pen stopped.

“Examination of disputed certificates,” she said.

“You’ve only examined one.”

“Suspected duplicate.”

He wrote that. He turned the book. She signed where he indicated, then laid the pen down within his reach.

Only then did he open the drawer.

South’s paper was the same size as North’s. Sera had expected something visibly wrong: a crowded line, a scraped number, a seal pressed crooked. Ivo laid it beside hers and moved his mug to the windowsill.

She read the basin name. The lock. The tide. She read them again, keeping her finger below the words so she would not cover what she needed to see.

“Both originals,” she said.

“Both registered.”

The seals were authentic. She knew the small break in the outer ring. That proved the Registry had received them. It did not make the Ninth Lock twice as wide.

“Who—”

Ivo closed the drawer.

She stopped. His name on an entry would not tell her why he had made it.

The clock outside struck the quarter’s approaching minutes with a dry internal rattle. Not yet. They had used less than ten.

“If I leave those here,” she said, “will you preserve them?”

“I preserved the second one before you arrived.”

She had no answer that improved on silence.

A freeze would keep North protected. Her brother’s stairs would stay dry. South’s communal seed store would have whatever its people could carry above the water. She could picture a sack well enough. She could not picture all of them.

“I’m disputing the assignment.”

Ivo drew the book back.

“A dispute isn’t a freeze.”

“I know the price.”

Her brass inspection key pressed against her knuckles as she searched her right coat pocket. She left it there and took out her license.

“North stays protected,” he said.

“Write it.”

He wrote. She read the entry before she signed it.

Then she slid her license across the desk and took her hand away.

Key remains in right coat pocket; left hand never grips. Motive unresolved. Proposed details: custody entry, clock discrepancy, seal’s identifying break.

## P5

Coverage: the entire supplied developmental excerpt, P1–P6, treated as the submitted narrative and ending. These findings do not assess any other phase’s output.

**DEV-01 — Priority 1; definite ending defect. P6.** The council “quietly bought a spare pump, which saved both basins.” This removes the governing constraint after Sera has paid to make a constrained decision. The reader’s investment in her choice becomes unnecessary: the solution arrives through purchasing power withheld from both character and reader. Smallest effective repair: remove the pump and let North’s protection and South’s flood stand. Carry forward the salvage already underway in P5, with specific limits on what survives. This preserves an ending without requiring a replacement rescue mechanism. Any compensation can address damage, not erase it.

**DEV-02 — Priority 2; definite causal/evidentiary defect. P3.** An unknown girl supplies the document’s location, culprit’s motive, and explanation; Sera then “immediately understood the entire fraud.” Authentic seals establish registration, not that this explanation is true. Attention shifts from Sera’s difficult negotiation to an unsolicited answer, and the mystery never earns the reader’s assent. Smallest effective repair: retain the girl as someone who knows one observable fact—where the certificate is—and make obtaining or preserving it require a choice from Sera and Ivo. Let comparison establish incompatible obligations. Debt and culpability need evidence later; silence is not corroboration.

**DEV-03 — Priority 3; definite missing consequence, with the emotional destination a matter of authorial choice. P6.** “Everyone had forgiven Ivo,” the offstage hearing, restored license, and mutual maxim compress distinct civic and personal conflicts into an uncontested resolution. Readers cannot assess responsibility or believe the estrangement has changed. Smallest effective repair: dramatize one contested hearing exchange and one limited act of renewed cooperation. Neither universal forgiveness nor complete alienation is required. Keep some disagreement alive and connect the license outcome to an actual finding.

Preserve P4’s wet ring spreading into the license photograph and Ivo covering it. Their practical responses carry conflicting feeling without naming it. Also preserve P5’s woman thanking Sera without looking up: South has work and priorities beyond validating the inspector.

## P6

Ivo put two mugs on the desk. Sera looked at the one beside her chair. He had remembered how she took her tea. She wished he had forgotten.

Her left wrist was a white parcel of pain inside its splint. She could not grip with that hand. With her right, she took the North certificate from her coat and laid it on the desk.

“That isn’t the only one,” she said quietly.

Something shifted in Ivo’s face, too slight for her to name. He opened the drawer and took out the South certificate. He placed it beside the first.

Paid for. All of it.

The two seals shone in the lamplight. Each confirmed registration. Neither promised there was room for the water.

Sera looked at him. He looked at the mugs.

She had come for the second certificate. Now it lay within reach of her good hand, and she left it there.

## P8

Two basins. One lock. Two authentic certificates promising its protection.

Lock inspector Sera Venn has an hour before high tide and a broken wrist that makes even handling evidence difficult. The second certificate belongs to South Basin. It is in the keeping of Ivo Renn, her estranged former apprentice, and he has no reason to trust her with it.

To freeze the disputed assignment, Sera must surrender her license. The freeze will preserve North’s protection—and her brother’s home. South holds the city’s communal seed store.

Someone has sold more safety than the Ninth Lock can give. Sera needs the second original to establish what the Registry has done. Once she has it, knowing the truth will leave her with a choice no seal can make for her.
