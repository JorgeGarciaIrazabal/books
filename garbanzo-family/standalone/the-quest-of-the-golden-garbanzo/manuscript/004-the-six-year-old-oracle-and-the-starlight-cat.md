# Chapter 4: The Six-Year-Old Oracle and the Starlight Cat

That afternoon at five, Supreme Wizard Clara convened an Emergency Council on her bedroom rug.

In attendance were three plush dragons, two shiny plastic ponies, a silver unicorn named Starlight, and a very nervous cardboard owl serving as Clerk of Adventure. Clara sat cross-legged in the center wearing purple velvet leggings, her favorite yellow rain boots, and a freshly sharpened paintbrush stuck behind her ear like a wand.

"Grown-ups think moving across an ocean is only about houses and giant metal coins," Clara told the circle, tapping a wooden block firmly on the rug for order. "That is completely incorrect. Nobody lives inside a spreadsheet. You cannot ice-skate on a budget."

She smoothed out a fresh sheet of poster paper and clicked open her thickest purple marker. As a six-year-old who had already survived three whole seasons of magical academy shows and read thick comic stories with proper big sentences, she refused to accept a dull move. If she was going to learn a brand-new school and speak twice as much Spanish, Spain needed serious qualifications.

She drew four bold boxes along the left side.

"Number one," Clara said aloud, sketching a swirling ribbon. "Dancing! With real spin room, not just kitchen tiles between the trash can and the fridge."

"Number two," she continued, drawing a fierce purple blade on a boot. "Ice skating! And horses! Real ones that sniff apples out of your pocket, not pretend horses on springs outside the supermarket."

"Number three," she added, carefully drawing a red bicycle beside two green trees, "places to bike where cars do not honk like angry geese. Plus books with real mysteries and wizards and dragons. Not baby books where a duck loses a sock for ten whole pages."

Starlight the unicorn tipped silently onto her side. Clara propped her upright with a sigh.

"And number four," Clara whispered, her purple marker pausing above the bottom corner of the page. "Friends."

She looked down at the wooden floorboards. The grown-ups were downstairs arguing cheerfully in Spanish and English over property listings and train maps, their mugs clinking in the kitchen. They sounded excited and brave. Clara knew she was supposed to feel excited too.

Instead, a strange hollow lump sat right under her ribs. Moving to Spain meant leaving behind her school corridor, the little park down the street, and every familiar tree on the corner. Even worse, one stubborn worry had kept nagging beneath her ribs since Monday morning, even after Papa tried to answer it at breakfast.

If they packed up their beds, their winter coats, and their copper kettle and flew across the giant dark Atlantic, what happened to Poki?

Back in September, when Poki had grown old and tired and crossed the quiet Silver Wall, Mama and Papa had let Clara tuck his favorite smooth gray stone next to his silver collar bell on the kitchen windowsill. Ever since, whenever twilight pooled purple along the backyard fence, Clara sometimes felt as if sleek gray-and-white fur brushed near her ankles. It made the yard feel guarded. What if a cat who lived beyond the Silver Wall only had a map of New Jersey?

Clara tucked the paintbrush-wand into her boot, pushed open the bedroom door, and tiptoed downstairs past the talking parents and straight out through the sliding porch glass into the cool November dusk.

Dead oak leaves whispered across the patio grass. Beyond the wooden fence, the neighbor's porch light flickered yellow. Clara stood shivering slightly in her unicorn sweater, staring up at the top rail where Poki used to sit before dark, watching squirrels like a tiny king.

"Poki?" she said softly, trying not to sound like she was crying. "Are you still here?"

For three seconds, only wind answered. Then the dusk along the far post shimmered, pale as frost on window glass and bright as spilled milk.

A sleek cat sat cleanly upon the rail.

He stood completely himself in his neat dark-and-silver coat, glistening with quiet starlight, his green eyes sharp and calm as two evening lamps.

Clara caught her breath and took two slow steps closer. She did not dare blink. "We are going to cross the big water," she told him urgently, holding up her four fingers. "To a town with cafés and a giant shopping bazaar and padel courts so Papa stops living inside the black glass tower. But I do not want to leave you in an empty yard."

Poki tilted his head once, whiskers twitching lightly against the chilly air. Then the starlight cat stepped silently along the rail, leaped down without a single crackle of dry leaves, and padded right up to Clara's yellow rain boots.

He did not talk. Cats had far too much dignity for speeches. Instead, he pressed his cool forehead gently against her mittened hand, leaned down to tap one smooth white pebble beside her toe, and curled a single bright silver thread of starlight twice around the handle of her paintbrush-wand before stepping backward into the evening mist.

Where his front paw tapped, the white pebble stayed faintly warm.

Clara picked up the warm white pebble and slid it deep into her pocket beside Starlight's spare ribbon. Behind her, the sliding glass door clicked open and Marissa leaned out with a scarf.

"Clara? Come inside before your ears freeze off! Who were you talking to out there?"

Clara turned around, smiling so wide her cheeks hurt. "Just checking my travel team, Mama. We are ready whenever the kettle is."
