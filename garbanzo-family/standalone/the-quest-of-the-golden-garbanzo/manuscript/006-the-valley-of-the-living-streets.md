# Chapter 6: The Valley of the Living Streets

Two hours north across the granite spine of Guadarrama, the fog of broken brochures gave way to wide Castilian sky.

Marissa rolled down her carriage window as they crossed into the valley of Valladolid, holding her rose-gold compass over the sill. She braced for another disappointing rattle. Instead, the small rose-gold dial began to hum like a warm kettle.

Through Arroyo de la Encomienda and La Flecha, along established avenues of Laguna de Duero, past the old stone ridge of Simancas and over toward Zaratán, the needle glowed steadier with every turn. Not a holiday ghost town. Real neighbors walked dogs under plane trees. Children shouted on neighborhood playgrounds in red sneakers. Cafés smelled of roasted coffee and toasted bread. Less than twenty minutes' smooth drive stood the Grand Bazaar of RÍO, glittering with cinemas, bookshops, restaurants, and warm rainy-day refuge.

"Look at those sidewalks," Marissa breathed, feeling a tight knot behind her ribs finally untangle. "People actually live here in November."

Clara pressed her nose against the glass. "I saw two horse riding signs, a bike path, and a bakery selling chocolate horns as big as my shoe!"

Jorge was squinting happily through his Cadastral Spectacles at real brick facades, paved streets, and rooftop solar panels, until they reached the edge of a lively sports club where four high glass walls flashed green in the late afternoon sun.

A tall local champion in spotless white wristbands leaned over the court rail and pointed straight at Jorge's satchel.

"Stranger with the spectacles!" the champion boomed joyfully. "No newcomer claims a house in this valley without surviving the Duel of the Padelsword!"

Jorge stopped dead. His eyes lit up like a kid spotting a fresh set of emerald GPUs. "Wait. Are you saying this neighborhood has glass padel courts right around the corner?"

"Two minutes from your front door, friend," the champion grinned, tossing a fuzzy yellow sphere across the barrier. "And our Tuesday evening league takes no excuses!"

"Hold my coffee," Jorge told Marissa, unstrapping his own scuffed Padelsword from his satchel before she could finish blinking.

What followed was not dignified high fantasy. Within three minutes Jorge was sprinting backward across blue turf, mistaking a wicked double-bounce for an enchanted fireball, diving headfirst toward the rear glass wall, and whipping a desperate spinning lob off two panes that soared three stories high, grazed a passing pigeon's dignity, and dropped dead inches inside the opposite net.

Clara cheered so loudly that three toddlers by the swings clapped along. Garbanzo poked his brass helmet out of Jorge's satchel and blasted a tiny triumphant trumpet fanfare completely out of tune.

"Good shot, neighbor!" laughed the champion, shaking Jorge's hand through the gate. "Come back next month when you bring your furniture!"

Marissa laughed until her ribs hurt as she brushed blue turf dust off Jorge's sleeve. "You are absurd," she said fondly, lifting the Compass of Living Streets once more. "Now lead us to a house that does not require tearing down three walls before Clara starts middle school."

Five streets away, where afternoon sunlight turned pale limestone gold, the compass chimed three clear bells in a row.

Right ahead sat a quiet, lived-in crescent of modern homes. Neighbors were watering flowerpots across a low hedge and waving to a passing cyclist. Between two occupied houses stood a single-story home with wide clear windows, clean insulation, solar tiles gleaming softly on the roof, and a genuine practical garage built for tools, bikes, and tinkering rather than wishful magic.

Through the iron garden gate, they stepped into an honest, sunlit backyard big enough for Clara to cartwheel, Marissa to sit over coffee with friends, and Jorge to wire patio lights without stepping into anyone else's olive tree. Three bright bedrooms wrapped around one sunlit living room, with zero rotting beams and a clean, verifiable key-handover date stamped right on the stone threshold.

Marissa unlatched the folding Brass Scale and set the tiny stucco house token into place, leaving the electric carriage, the travel compass, and the iron repairs shield squarely on the Desire Pan.

The Effort Pan rose calmly, stopping comfortably inside what ten hard years had already earned.

Clara tugged sharply on Marissa's sleeve. "Mama! Look up!"

Along the sunlit terracotta top of the back garden wall, a sleek silver-and-gray cat sat quietly in the slanting gold light. Tail curled neatly over crisp white paws, Poki blinked once toward the open garden doors before melting gently into the warm afternoon glare.

Marissa blinked back sudden tears, smiling fiercely as she looked from the sunlit garden to Jorge's bright eyes.

"Well," Marissa said softly. "I think we found our street."
