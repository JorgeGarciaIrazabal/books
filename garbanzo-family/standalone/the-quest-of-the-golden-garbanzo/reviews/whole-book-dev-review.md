# Independent developmental review | *The Quest of the Golden Garbanzo*

## Revision letter

The book delivers its dramatic, funny, magical family adventure. The moment that made me want the family to leave Manhattan was Clara taping a drawing over Jorge’s monitor. I had just seen him rescue an apprentice and settle a sneezing dragon. I understood why he might want to go back to work in the morning. Then came Clara’s six words, “WHEN DO WE GET YOUR TUESDAYS?” By Chapter 10, the answer is a school walk, a botched lantern repair he has time to try again, and two whole chapters of the creepy wizard book. That Tuesday feels earned.

The manuscript contains **8,204 body words** by my count, excluding chapter headings and counting hyphenated compounds as single words. That meets the 7,800–8,300 target and gives about **31 pages at 265 words per page**. The proportions suit the story. Chapter 4 earns its extra room by letting Clara work her way from an exuberant list to the one word she can barely say, “Friends.” Chapter 9 earns its shorter length by getting everyone from the farewell table to the plane while the pleasure of the cousins under the table is still fresh. Actual pagination remains unverified.

I would revise the passage from choosing the house through deciding to resign. The story makes the cost of *waiting* keenly felt. The cost of *going too soon* is equally vivid when a mountain castle knocks a spoon out of Clara’s bowl, but harder to follow when the family tests the house they actually want. At the crucial moment, I wanted to see what stayed on the scale, what they could give up in a bad year, and what convinced Marisa and Jorge that they were ready. Those answers can fit inside the existing scenes.

## What should survive revision

- **Marisa’s right to want a particular life.** “Where would I buy fresh bread on a Tuesday morning?” in Chapter 5 makes a deserted development feel lonely in one breath. In Chapter 6, her compass approves of the mountain community even though the family cannot afford the house there. Her judgment has its own integrity. By Chapter 10, Elena’s café invitation pays off the bakery question.
- **Clara and Poki’s private exchange.** “What if a cat who lived beyond the Silver Wall only had a map of New Jersey?” gives grief the exact shape it might take for this child. Poki presses his forehead against her mitten. When he appears on the Spanish wall in Chapter 6, the reader understands before anyone speaks. Keep that silence and let the nature of the visit stay open.
- **Comedy that a listener can see coming.** The Chapter 1 paper star lands in Jorge’s coffee. In Chapter 3, Clara catches the spoon the adults’ decision sends flying. By Chapter 10, Garbanzo hiding behind the rosemary and yelling “Abandon patio! Call the Citadel!” is funny twice over. Jorge has left the tower and his familiar still knows exactly whom to call.
- **The Siren’s second offer.** “What if freedom feels... small?” in Chapter 8 is more intimate than the bonus pitch. It takes Jorge’s pride in his craft seriously. Keep the scuffed padel ball, the copper wire, the small sound of the resignation stamp, and the ordinary afternoon outside.
