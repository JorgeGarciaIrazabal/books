# 1. The Empty Table

Mila had brought a whole pocketful of stars to the library, but the moon was missing.

At first, she thought she had the wrong table.

She checked the one beside the window. Scissors, string, a cup of cold tea. She checked the one beside the shelves. Three paper fish with very surprised faces.

“Dad,” she said. “Where's our moon?”

Dad set down his bag of battery lights. “Mrs. Bell?”

The librarian came out from behind a tower of books. A pencil stuck out of her hair.

“You're just who I need. Did you move the moon after lunch?”

Mila shook her head. Yesterday, she and Noah had helped cover a round frame with white tissue paper. The moon was so wide that Mila couldn't reach around it. Tonight, it would lead the lantern walk down Willow Lane.

She had practiced walking very slowly beside it.

“I left it right here,” she said.

Mrs. Bell looked at the empty table.

“So did I.”

Mila emptied her pocket. The paper stars fluttered onto the table. She had cut them out before lunch to hang beneath the moon.

They looked rather silly without it.

“It can't have gone far,” Dad said. “When did you last see it?”

“This morning,” said Mrs. Bell. “I put it in a box so the little lantern makers wouldn't poke holes in the paper. A big, flat box. It was still on this table when I went to eat my lunch.”

“With the moon inside?” Mila asked.

“Yes. I shut the lid myself.”

Mila looked under the table. There was a crayon, a bent star, and one of her own shoes. The shoe was still on her foot.

No box.

Dad checked behind the folding screen. Mrs. Bell checked the cupboard where they kept the cushions. Mila opened the door to the tiny kitchen.

“If it's in there,” Dad said, “ask it to make some tea.”

Mila almost smiled.

Noah arrived carrying a ruler and a small notebook.

“I worked out how long the star strings should be,” he said. Then he saw their faces. “What happened?”

“The moon's gone. And its box.”

He put the ruler down.

Mila ran her hand along the table edge. Something soft caught against her finger. A tiny yellow thread stuck out of a rough place in the wood.

“Wait.”

Mrs. Bell leaned closer. “Oh. I tied the box with yellow wool. We had a ball left from the pom-pom day.”

Mila bent until her nose was almost level with the thread. It was fuzzy, with a little curl at the end.

“Could the box have caught here?” she asked.

“It could,” said Mrs. Bell. “The knot hung over that edge.”

Mila wanted to pull the thread free. Instead, she left it where it was.

Noah opened his notebook. The first page held careful sums about stars.

“There's room on the next page,” he said.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

Mila wrote slowly with Noah's pencil.

*Moon in box. Both gone.*

Under that, she wrote, *Yellow wool on table.*

“Shall I put who took it?” Noah asked.

“We don't know who.”

“I meant leave a space.”

“Oh. Yes.”

She left a very large space.

Outside, someone shook out a string of little bells. They jingled all at once. People were already getting ready for tonight.

“Can we look in the yard?” Mila asked Dad.

“Together,” he said. “I'll help Mrs. Bell check the other rooms first. Then we'll go out.”

Mrs. Bell picked up one of Mila's paper stars. Its points were uneven. Mila had liked it best until now.

“We'll hang these somewhere,” the librarian said gently. “Even if we have to lead the walk with a fish.”

Mila glanced at the paper fish. A fish would be funny.

But she wanted their moon.

She tucked the stars into Dad's bag, and helped him open the next door.
