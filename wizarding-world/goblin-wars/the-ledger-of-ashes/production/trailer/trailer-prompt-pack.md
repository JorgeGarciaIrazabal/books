# The Ledger of Ashes — trailer prompt pack

**Cut:** 60-second cinematic book trailer, spoiler-safe first-act cut.
**Book:** *The Ledger of Ashes*, Book One of *The Goblin Wars*. Adult historical fantasy mystery, 1752. Unofficial fan fiction.

> **Assumption:** I read “does spoil things” as “doesn’t spoil things.” This cut sells the mystery without revealing the culprit, the mechanism, or the ending. The spoiler audit at the bottom lists exactly what is in and out. If you wanted a spoilery cut, there is a note at the end.

> **Canon note:** the character appearances below are trailer-only visual proposals. They are not manuscript canon and do not change the book.

---

## 1. The pitch

**Logline:** When the Ministry seizes the iron ledger that holds her people’s wages, a goblin accountant finds a charge that should not exist and a dead man who was charged twice.

**Tagline:** Every debt leaves a mark.

**Tone:** Cold institutional dread, warm lamplight, dry wit, and the thrill of someone doing arithmetic in a room full of people who want her to stop. *The Remains of the Day* meets a locked-room conspiracy, with goblins.

**The three questions the trailer must leave in the audience’s head:**

1. Who charged a dead man twice?
2. What else is in that book?
3. What happens when she opens it?

No answers. Only the promise.

## 3. Visual bible

### Global style suffix — append to every still prompt

```text
Grounded 1752 historical fantasy thriller, live-action cinematic still, 35mm film, anamorphic 2.39:1, natural lamp and candle light, soot-black shadows, wet stone, brass, iron, parchment and ink textures, muted umber and slate palette with ember-orange highlights, fine grain, shallow depth of field, production-design realism, no glossy CGI.
```

### Negative prompt — append or use as the negative field

```text
text, letters, watermark, logo, modern clothing, wristwatch, electric light, plastic, cartoon, anime, comic, glossy CGI, superhero pose, neon, lens flare, oversaturated, deformed hands, extra fingers, recognizable Harry Potter characters, Hogwarts, wizarding-world logos, antisemitic caricature, hooked nose, greed imagery, grotesque teeth
```

### Goblin design rule

Naturalistic, dignified goblin design. Broad cheekbones, long pointed ears, strong noses, weathered skin, 18th-century working clothes. They should look like a distinct, self-possessed people with an economy and a culture, not a gag or a caricature. Never use hooked noses, exaggerated greed, or grotesque teeth.

### Palette

- Ink black `#141210`
- Soot `#242422`
- Wet stone `#3A3F3C`
- Parchment `#E8DCC3`
- Brass `#B08A4A`
- Ember `#C4552D`
- Cold lamp `#D9C9A3`
- River blue-grey `#5B6B70`

### Camera grammar

- **Opening:** slow push-ins, locked frames, symmetry, bureaucratic calm.
- **Seizure:** handheld edge, doors banging, candle flames bending.
- **Investigation:** macro inserts, shallow focus, hands and paper.
- **Blackwater:** wide, misty, vertical head frame, small figures.
- **Hero:** low angle, slow tracking backward, clerks rising.
- **Never:** whip pans, drone swoops, superhero landings, magic sparkles.

## 4. Shot prompts

**For each still:** paste the shot prompt, then append the Global Style Suffix and the Negative Prompt.
**For each motion clip:** use the still as the first frame, paste the motion prompt, generate 4–6 seconds, 24 fps, no on-screen text.

## 8. Edit notes

- Assemble to the voiceover first, then cut picture to it.
- Cut on sound: the seal stamp, the lock click, the bell, the paper tear.
- Hold half a second of black before the title. Let the silence do the work.
- Hold the title for 4 seconds. Do not rush it.
- No crossfades except into the title. Hard cuts everywhere else.
- 24 fps. 16:9 master. A 2.39:1 letterbox is optional but suits the anamorphic look.
- Grade: keep the blacks rich but not crushed; protect faces in the lamp pools; keep the ember highlights warm, not orange.
- Captions: burned-in for social, SRT for YouTube.
- Loudness: −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP.
- End card: 5 seconds minimum.

## 10. Spoiler audit

**In the cut:**

- Dorrik’s death, the inciting incident
- The Ministry seizure of the iron ledger
- The duplicate charge for forty bars
- The hidden page and the torn strip
- Blackwater shaft
- The household’s unpaid wages
- Ragna’s decision to investigate
- The existence of a cover-up, not its author

**Not in the cut:**

- Who altered the ledger
- Merrok’s motive or the Ministry stockpile
- Ilva’s complicity
- The weigh-house violence
- Tavin’s arrest
- The burning ledger
- The public accounting and the ending
- The wider rebellion
- Any line that answers the mystery

**Borderline, use with care:**

- The torn strip reading “…water” points to Blackwater. It is a clue, not an answer. Keep it if you want the audience to feel smart; cut it if you want maximum mystery.
- “Against a dead man’s name” confirms the charge is tied to Dorrik. That is the hook, not the solution.
