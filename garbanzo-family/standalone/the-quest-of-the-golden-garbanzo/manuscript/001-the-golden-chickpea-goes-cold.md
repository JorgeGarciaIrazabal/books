# Chapter 1: The Golden Chickpea Goes Cold

At seven o'clock on a Monday morning in New Jersey, a tiny robot shaped like a chickpea marched across the kitchen counter. He sneezed green sparks and shouted, "We are rich! Pack the suitcases! Call the king!"

*POP!*

Shiny gold confetti snowed down into Jorge's coffee mug.

Jorge blinked at the soggy paper star floating on top of his coffee. "Garbanzo," he sighed, "it is seven in the morning. Your daily money report isn't supposed to run until ten."

The little bot was no bigger than a plum, built from tan plastic, four stubby green metal feet, and spare chips from Jorge's home computer. Nobody knew how, yet a tiny spark of real magic had woken him up one rainy weekend. Ever since, Garbanzo took his job way too seriously.

"Breaking financial news cannot wait for ten o'clock!" Garbanzo piped, waving his stubby arms. "Last Friday you moved fifty dollars from Checking to Savings! By my math, fifty plus fifty equals one hundred new dollars! Hooray! Buy a boat!"

Marissa walked into the kitchen in her warm fleece sweater, carrying a plate of fresh pancakes. "Did he confuse transfers with income again?"

"For the ninth time this month," Jorge said, fishing out the wet confetti with a spoon.

"I am not confused!" Garbanzo squeaked. "I am optimistic!"

Marissa calmly set the plate down well out of Jorge's reach. Everyone in the house knew the Big Kitchen Rule. Jorge could wire a garage outlet, fix leaky sink pipes, build a home computer, and keep giant data systems humming in Manhattan. Asking Jorge to flip a pancake, however, was a crime against breakfast. He happily let Marissa guard the stove while he handled every wire, screw, and computer bug in the house.

Across the table, six-year-old Clara swung her purple unicorn socks under her chair. She stopped chewing a bite of pancake and pointed her fork toward the back counter.

"Hey," Clara said softly. "Our lucky chickpea stopped glowing."

The room got quiet.

Sitting beside the toaster was a small copper pot with no water and no heat underneath. Inside rested one shiny golden garbanzo bean, smooth as a marble and as big as a walnut. Jorge and Marissa had dropped it there years ago when they started saving for a calmer life. Normally, the bean glowed warm gold like a cozy nightlight whenever the family made honest, patient steps toward freedom.

Right now, the copper pot rattled faintly against the tile. *Clack, clack, clack.*

Jorge lifted the lid. The golden chickpea sat completely dark and chilly as an ice cube from the freezer.

"Whoa," Clara whispered, leaning over the counter. "Why is it freezing?"

Garbanzo hurried over, peered inside the pot, and gasped. "Disaster! Doom! Quick, check if I forgot to count my own feet!"

Marissa stirred cinnamon into her coffee and gave Jorge a sideways look. She did not need magic to read her husband's guilty ears, which had turned bright pink.

"Jorge," she said gently, "before you run to catch your train into the city, tell me the truth. Did you stay up until midnight messing with our retirement spreadsheet?"

"I only tested one little idea," Jorge protested.

"Which idea?"

"Maybe... moving the target date up to January," Jorge mumbled. "And maybe shrinking our grocery estimate so a big house in the Spanish mountains fits on the screen."

"Ah," Marissa said dryly. "You trimmed our food budget to three crackers a week so we could own an overpriced drafty roof."

The copper pot gave one loud, grumpy *CLACK*.

"See?" Clara pointed. "Even the magic bean thinks three crackers is a rip-off."

Jorge rubbed the back of his neck and laughed despite himself. Marissa pulled a clean yellow legal pad from the counter drawer and slapped it down between the pancake syrup and Clara's crayons.

"Here is the deal," Marissa said, not angry at all, just wide awake. "For years we have worked hard, saved smart, and skipped flashy stuff so we could earn our mornings back while Clara is still small. We do not wreck all that work by faking numbers at midnight. Tonight, no spreadsheets. On Saturday morning, we sit down together and write out the real truth."

Clara raised her sticky fork like a wand. "I get a vote too! No boring towns! And wait a second."

Her smile dipped just a little. She looked past the sink toward the sunny kitchen window. On the sill sat a small silver cat collar bell beside a smooth gray river stone. Poki, their sleek, smart cat, had passed away peacefully back in September.

"If we move all the way across the ocean to Spain," Clara asked quietly, "will Poki still know where to find us?"

The kitchen went soft and still for a second. Beside the stove, the microwave clock clicked from 7:14 to 7:15.

Jorge walked around the table and kissed the top of Clara's head. "Poki knows his family, kiddo. Cats do not care about street addresses."

"Promise?"

"Promise," Marissa said, squeezing Clara's hand.

Outside, the morning train horn blew three blocks away. Jorge grabbed his backpack and caught Garbanzo gently by his round plastic waist before he could fire a second confetti cannon at the jam jar. Then he kissed Marissa goodbye at the door.

"Saturday morning," Jorge promised. "All real numbers."

Behind him, inside the chilly copper pot, the Golden Garbanzo stayed stubbornly dark, waiting to see if he meant it.
