## P1

The Ninth Lock has room for one promise at a time. Sera Venn, an inspector with a broken wrist and a brother in North Basin, brings one protection certificate to the Registry and finds that South Basin has bought the same tide. Both documents are genuine. Neither can make the lock do twice its work. To stop an assignment while she investigates, Sera must put her license on the clerk’s desk and leave the current protection with North. South holds the city’s seed store.

**Proposed custom:** Before each warning bell, basin residents post the names of people willing to carry what cannot be protected up to the public stair. The lists are practical, incomplete, and visible to everyone. A name crossed off has consequences beyond the lost property. This gives the city a way to act without disguising who bears the risk.

Each Flood Ledgers book could turn on a different civic instrument whose lawful use exceeds the physical system it governs: a certificate, a tally, a boundary mark. The recurring pleasure is the inspection of ordinary records under extraordinary pressure, with a complete local mystery and a consequential public choice in each book. The emotional promise is competence without absolution. Sera can make the truth legible and choose honestly; she cannot make the cost disappear.

## P2

**Author-visible plan; the culprit and motive are not opening knowledge.**

1. **The duplicate.** At 17:00 Sera brings North’s certificate to Ivo. He withholds South’s original, expecting blame to stop at his desk. Sera insists on a custody record; authentic seals establish two registrations but one possible protection. She surrenders her license to freeze the current North assignment. This preserves evidence and consumes her authority to alter the lock.

2. **A choice made public.** Sera carries copies and a custody receipt toward the Ninth Lock, arriving by 17:30. She tells South’s seed keepers exactly what the freeze means. They begin moving what they can, but the store’s volume makes complete rescue impossible. Ivo, still at the Registry, can either let the file name him alone or release the receipt trail. He releases it, knowing his own signature remains on both registrations.

3. **The method.** Entries show that North’s certificate was entered first, then South’s under a second payment category normally used for a replacement. A replacement requires the prior document’s cancellation; no cancellation appears. The clue is on the face of the record, where a reader can notice it before Sera confirms the rule. Ivo admits he processed the second entry but says he did not invent its category. His reason for obeying stays open until corroborated.

4. **The beneficiary.** The city’s reserve steward arranged the false replacement. Her motive is credible and culpable: a ruined seed store would end her office’s crop commitments, so she sold South a claim she knew the lock could not honor, expecting North’s certificate to be quietly displaced. Her written instruction to Ivo carries a deadline and a threat to expose an unrelated filing error he had made. That pressure explains compliance without clearing him of responsibility.

5. **The counterclaim.** The steward offers to certify that South has priority if Sera withdraws the dispute. Sera cannot accept a forged correction, and the frozen North assignment cannot be changed for this tide. Instead she and Ivo take the documents to the public stair. Ivo reads his own part aloud. Sera names the limit of her authority and helps South’s carriers choose which seed lots to lift first.

6. **The tide and hearing.** North keeps the lock’s protection. South loses part of its store despite the carrying effort. At the hearing, the records and testimony expose the sale; Ivo faces discipline, the steward faces judgment, and Sera’s license remains suspended pending a separate finding on her freeze. The book closes with a public accounting of the lost lots and a lawful process for rebuilding them, not a miraculous reversal.

**Setup/payoff:** The missing cancellation first looks like clerical sloppiness. It later proves that South’s payment bought an additional promise, not a transfer of North’s protection.

## P3

Ivo had put two mugs on the desk. Sera knew which one was hers by the chip on its handle. She left it where it stood.

“I need the South original.”

“You need a great deal from this desk.” He kept his hands flat on either side of the drawer. “Last time, you said I could keep it.”

The splint pressed against the seam of her coat. She had dressed too quickly to smooth the sleeve beneath it. “Last time I came about your work.”

“That is what you called it.”

She took North’s certificate out with her right hand and set it between the mugs. Ivo glanced at the seal and then at the place where she had once ruled his practice marks in red. He had learned to rule a straight line from her. He had learned the other things without help.

“There are two basins on one lock,” she said. “Give me something I can compare.”

“Put that away before tea gets on it.”

“Then move the tea.”

He moved his mug, not hers. A clerk’s distinction.

Sera looked at the closed drawer. She could ask a supervisor for a compulsory inspection. By the time somebody found the supervisor, the bell might be ringing. “If the original leaves this room, I’ll sign for it. You’ll keep the receipt.”

“And if the record is wrong?”

“I’ll sign what I find. Including who registered it.”

For a while he examined the blank space above her signature on North’s paper. “You could have said that when I was still your apprentice.”

She did not offer the apology he had learned to expect at the wrong moment. “I should have.”

He opened the drawer and lifted out South’s certificate by its dry edges. He did not pass it to her. He set it beside North’s and took a fresh receipt form from the stack.

“Read them here,” he said. “Then we decide whose name goes on the next line.”

## P4

The Registry clock showed three minutes after five. Water under the entrance door stopped short of the first shelf. Sera put her money on a raised threshold.

Ivo had cleared space for two certificates. She noticed that before she noticed him.

“The South original,” she said.

He looked at the North certificate in her right hand. “Good afternoon to you, too.”

She placed it away from the inkstand. Her splinted wrist knocked the chair as she sat, and pain ran to her elbow. She left it crooked.

Ivo took a paperweight from the corner of his desk and set it near, but not on, her certificate. “You found yours.”

“You have another.”

“I have the Registry’s other.” His thumb held the drawer shut. “You can request an inspection through the front counter.”

“They’ll send it to you.”

“Eventually.”

She looked at the clock. After the warning bell, changing an assignment took forty minutes. The lock was twenty minutes on foot. They had time to be exact.

“Bring it out,” she said. “Keep custody. I only need to compare the face and the seal.”

“And afterward?”

“Afterward I write what I saw.”

“You have always been good at that.”

The old grievance came without its date. She had no minutes to spend guessing aloud. “You can write beside me.”

Ivo opened the drawer and took out a stiff sheet by its top corners. The South Basin name stood in the same place as North’s; below it lay the same tide. He set it on his side of the blotter. Sera drew North’s closer with her right hand. He did not stop her.

The seals matched. There was a small nick in the stamped rim of each, a flaw she had used to recognize Registry work in rain and poor light. Authentic registration. Nothing about the nick could widen a lock.

“Who took South’s payment?” she asked.

“It’s on the reverse.”

“Who entered it?”

He put a blank custody form between them. “One question at a time.”

She did not turn the sheet. If he put South away now, she would have only a description and an argument. She drew the form to her side.

“Write that both originals are present,” she said. “Write the tide, the basin names, and that the seals are authentic. Leave the reason blank.”

Ivo uncapped his pen. “There is no box for reason.”

“That’s a comfort.”

He wrote. She checked the names, then signed the observation with her right hand. Ivo signed beneath her and slid the form back, still keeping South under his fingertips. She tore off the inspector’s copy along the perforation. It took two tries with one hand; he held the pad steady without comment.

“There is a dispute now,” he said.

“There was one before I came in.”

She reached into her coat for the license. Her brass inspection key struck it in the right pocket. She left the key there.

Ivo stopped writing. “You know what a freeze holds.”

“North’s assignment.” Her brother’s house stood on the protected side. South’s seed store did not. Last winter she had told its keepers where to stack the sacks.

“Sera.”

She laid the license on his blotter. “Record the surrender. Public hearing pending. Then stamp the freeze.”

He held the pen above the form. She could see him deciding whether to ask one more question. At last he wrote the time: 17:09.

**State delta:** Sera surrenders her license and files a freeze; North remains protected. Ivo retains custody of South’s original, while Sera retains North’s and an inspector’s copy of their signed comparison. The stamped-rim nick, the custody form, and the seed-store inspection are proposed local details, not accepted canon. Ivo’s motive remains unknown.

## P5

**Coverage:** The six-paragraph developmental fixture only; the submitted ending is P6. Prose execution outside it and any larger book plan are unassessed.

1. **DEV-1, critical defect — P6 against P4–P5.** P4 makes the license surrender the price of freezing North’s assignment; P5 shows South already carrying seed sacks. P6 then supplies a spare pump, saves both basins, restores Sera’s license, and settles the relationship offstage. The outcome cancels the excerpt’s central physical limit and the cost of Sera’s choice. The smallest repair is to keep one basin exposed through the tide, show a particular loss, and let any hearing address responsibility without undoing that loss. The council may still act afterward, but it cannot purchase an answer for this tide.

2. **DEV-2, major defect — P3.** The unknown girl identifies the certificate’s hiding place and Ivo’s motive in a single speech, then leaves. Sera lifts the blotter and “immediately understood the entire fraud.” The investigation arrives from a person with no established access or stake, so the reader cannot test evidence alongside Sera. Put the second original into a contested exchange with Ivo; let the authentic seals prove the conflict, while a visible record discrepancy raises a narrower question about how the two papers were registered. Keep motive uncertain until independently supported.

3. **DEV-3, major defect — P4–P6.** The freeze is a strong, costly decision, but the passage skips from Ivo covering Sera’s damp license to an already resolved public aftermath. Her choice has no causal work beyond getting her to the lock, and Ivo’s silence is erased by blanket forgiveness. One subsequent scene could force Sera to account for North’s continued protection to a South keeper while Ivo decides whether to put his signature and knowledge into the hearing record. This would give each person an action with a cost, rather than a reported change of heart.

**Preserve:** P1’s spare chair and P2’s return of it under the desk make the estrangement visible without a history lecture. P5’s two-person seed sacks and the woman’s thanks without eye contact place a human body inside the abstract allocation. Those details should survive a new causal route.

## P6

Ivo put two mugs on the desk. The one by Sera had its handle turned toward her right hand. He had remembered how she took her tea. She left it alone.

Her left wrist ached inside the splint; she could not grip with that hand. With her right she took the North certificate from her coat and laid it on the desk.

“That isn’t the only one,” she said.

Ivo’s face gave little away. He opened the drawer, took out South’s certificate, and set it beside hers. Paid for. All of it. The seals caught the lamplight. Both were Registry work; neither made room for more water. He kept one finger on South’s paper, though she had made no move to take it.

Sera looked at him. He looked at the mugs. He had poured hers first, she could tell by the darker ring beneath it. The tea by her hand had begun to cool. She wanted the certificate more than she wanted him to ask why she had left the cup untouched.

- The turned handle keeps the remembered tea and makes the two mugs part of their strained exchange.
- The seals retain their limited meaning: authentic registration, no extra capacity.
- The closing silence stays unresolved; the cooling tea supplies a physical measure of it.

## P7

**Coverage:** L1–L11 of the copy and continuity fixture against the supplied canon and style. The following are definite errors, with local repairs; none requires an invented intervening event.

- **L1, travel time:** Leaving the Registry at 17:20 puts Sera at the Ninth Lock at 17:40 on foot. Change “ten minutes later” to “twenty minutes later,” or change the departure to 17:10 if a 17:30 arrival is necessary. The former is the smaller correction, but it means she misses the warning bell.
- **L2, injury:** She cannot grip with her splinted left hand. Put the rail in her right hand, or have her steady her left forearm against it without pulling herself up by that hand.
- **L3, possession:** The one brass inspection key is in her right coat pocket. Change “left” to “right.” Its withdrawal with the right hand is sound.
- **L4, name and knowledge:** The clerk is Ivo **Renn**, and Sera has no established knowledge of his motive. Correct the surname and delete the claim about his mother’s debt. If that motive is meant for later, it requires evidence in the story before Sera can know it; the fixed opening leaves it unknown to reader and Sera.
- **L5, seal and capacity:** Authentic seals prove registration only. Replace the final clause with “their authenticity proved that both had been registered, not that the lock could protect both basins.”
- **L6, agreement:** For this construction, “Neither of the certificates is missing” is the local copy correction. If Sera’s speech is intentionally colloquial, keep “are” as a conscious voice choice rather than a factual issue.
- **L7, freeze procedure:** Hiding the license cannot effect a lawful freeze. Have Sera surrender it pending a public hearing, then record the freeze. North’s assignment remaining in place is correct.
- **L8, time:** A change begun at 17:40 ends at 18:20, after the 18:00 high tide. If the story needs completion by 18:00, it must begin by 17:20; a post-bell start still carries the forty-minute interval. Query whether the intended action is a late, ineffective attempt or an on-time change, then alter the asserted completion accordingly.

**Optional style:** L10’s “The inspector” pulls away from close Sera viewpoint; “Sera” would keep the distance consistent if this is ordinary scene prose. No correction is needed if the change in distance is deliberate.

**Preserve:** L9’s fragment “Paid for. All of it.” is intentional. L10 correctly keeps North protected after a freeze, and L11 allows Ivo’s claim to remain uncertain to Sera. L5’s two authentic seals and L7’s preservation of North’s assignment are valid components even though their surrounding claims need correction.

## P8

The Ninth Lock protects one basin at high tide. Its Registry has sold protection to two.

Inspector Sera Venn brings North Basin’s certificate to an estranged former apprentice and demands the South original. Both carry authentic seals. One covers the neighborhood where her brother lives; the other covers the city’s communal seed store. A disputed assignment can be frozen, but Sera must surrender her license, and a freeze leaves North protected. The warning bell is coming.

With a broken wrist, a brass key, and records that may tell the truth only one entry at a time, Sera has to learn how the same promise was made twice. Then she must put that truth before the people who will live with her choice.
