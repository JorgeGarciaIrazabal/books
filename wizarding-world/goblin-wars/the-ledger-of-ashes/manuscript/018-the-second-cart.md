# Chapter Eighteen: The Second Cart

Borr came back downriver on a Thursday, with a split lip and a load of damaged buckets.

Mere sent word to Sella's workshop. The message reached Ragna at the healer's, where Neri was sitting in a chair with her injured leg on a stool. A wooden splint held the foot still. The healer had told her the bones might be sound, but the swelling made it too early to be certain.

Neri read the message and handed it back. “Ask him about the first load as well.”

“The forty?”

“The twenty before it. He drove for Mere. People drive other people's carts when there's work.”

“You know he did?”

“I know he has. Don't put that in your account as an answer.”

Ragna smiled despite herself. Neri had a file and a small piece of brass in her lap, borrowed from the healer's husband. She had dropped the file twice and now gripped it too tightly.

“Give it here,” Sella said.

“No.”

“I'm not taking it away. I'm holding the brass.”

Neri let her. Ragna left them arguing about which side needed filing.

At Mere Foundry the charcoal pile had been covered with a tarpaulin. Hask was trying to repair the hinge of a furnace door with a pin that was too short. He showed Ragna the gap without being asked.

“Mere would like a word about the cart,” he said. “I would like a word about the fitter's tools. They are still at Thorn, and this pin will not grow because I swear at it.”

“I've written for them.”

“Write again. Use larger letters.”

Mere was in the yard with Borr. He sat on an overturned bucket, pressing a damp rag to his lip. His coat was torn under one arm. A horse stood beside him, eating from a sack hung around its neck.

“The checkpoint?” Ragna asked.

“Another carrier's elbow,” he said. “We both wanted the dry side of a doorway. He got it.”

Mere handed him a cup. “This is Ragna. She wants to ask about Blackwater and Thorn.”

“She's got a mouth. Let her.”

Ragna told him which journey concerned her. He remembered the forty bars and the narrow cart. He had waited at the upper gate while it was taken through, then been sent back without the vehicle. He could identify the driver from Blackwater; he had not seen the count inside Thorn.

“Kelm obtained the counterfoil,” Ragna said. “Dole signed it.”

“Then he's got more than I have.”

“Do you know who brought the earlier twenty?”

Borr put the rag down. “Earlier than the forty?”

“A fortnight before. There were two loads released under a workshop note.”

He looked at Mere. “That's the one I told you about.”

“You told me there was a load that paid badly.”

“It did.” He turned to Ragna. “I took twenty from Blackwater to Thorn. I didn't load them there. A second driver brought them to the yard, and I took them from her.”

“Who was the second driver?”

“Rell. She works the upper road when she can get it.”

“Where is she now?”

“Ask at the stables by the east bridge. If she isn't there, ask her horse. It knows where the food is.”

Mere took the rag from him and looked at his lip. He pulled away.

“Did Thorn give you a receipt?” Ragna asked.

“They gave me a chit for the empty cart. I gave it back to the man who lent me the cart.”

“Who received the iron?”

“A clerk called Sorn. Small man. Ink on his cuffs and a cough that made the pen jump. He counted while I held the tailboard.”

“You saw him count twenty?”

“I helped him. A bar had rolled under the canvas. He wanted to mark nineteen and I wouldn't let him.”

“Did you see what he wrote?”

“No. He turned the book away.”

Ragna took out her pencil. Borr watched it.

“Will you put my name on a wall?”

“No.”

“Where, then?”

She put the pencil down. “If you agree, I would like a statement for the partners and the inquiry. We would ask you before sending it anywhere else. They may still ask you to speak for yourself.”

“Can they make me?”

“I don't know what they will do. They have taken two people to Blackwater.”

Borr touched the torn seam of his coat. “Rell has children. Don't send anyone to her door saying I told you to.”

“I won't.”

He stood and took the horse's sack away. The animal lifted its head and followed him towards the shed. Ragna thought he had finished. At the door he called back, “You can write it. I want to see the words before you take them.”

She followed with the paper. He corrected her twice. Once because she had written that Sorn signed a receipt, when Borr had only seen him writing in the book. Once because she had described the iron as belonging to Sella's workshop.

“I didn't know whose it was,” he said. “I knew where it had come from.”

He put his name below the statement after Mere read it to him. She signed as witness to his signature. He kept one copy and let Ragna take the other to the partners.

At the east bridge stables, Rell was cleaning a harness. She listened without putting down the brush. She remembered taking a load from Blackwater to the riverside yard, but would not say where it had gone after Borr took it.

“Did it come under a note for Sella's workshop?” Ragna asked.

“I had a load number. I didn't have the workshop note.”

“Would you show it to me?”

“No.”

Ragna waited.

Rell hung the harness on a peg. “My son is due back from the north. The men at the road have been opening carts and making people tell them who gave them work. I don't know which answer is going to bring them to my house.”

“I can come back.”

“You can. I may tell you the same thing.”

She went on brushing the harness. Ragna left her to it.

On the road to the bank house she passed a wagon carrying three empty cages. Each was large enough for a person. A wizard walked beside the wagon with a list, counting the cages whenever the wheels crossed a rut. The driver stopped at the new notice and waited while the wizard checked it against the list. Neither paid attention to Ragna.

She stood a little too long. The wizard looked up.

“Is this the road to the court?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“Good. They sent us round the wrong bridge.”

He thanked her and went on. The last cage had a latch with a bent pin. Neri would have noticed it at once.

Issa received Ragna in the bank house rather than the counting room. There was a fire, though the afternoon was mild. A pair of boots stood beside it, one on its side.

“Halrik's,” Issa said when Ragna looked. “He went out in the rain and refused to put them back on until they dried.”

She read Borr's statement beside the supplier's attested invoice and the copy of Venn's dispatch. She did not ask for Kelm's original counterfoil. Ragna explained that Mere had an interest in it and Sella held it by agreement.

“Will he come here?” Issa asked.

“Borr agreed to be asked. I won't promise he will come.”

“I can accept a signed account for the examination. If Halrik wants to question him, he can travel.”

“That will not prove what was entered in the receiving book.”

“No. It gives me a carrier, a date, a destination and a person who counted with him. More than I had this morning.”

She put the papers in separate wrappers. Ragna knew that care with wrappers. She had once taken more comfort in it.

“Will you pay Sella?” she asked.

“I can put the first twenty aside as disputed. With Borr's statement and Venn's dispatch, I will ask Halrik to agree that a payment can be made without waiting for Dole's book.”

“When?”

“This afternoon.”

Ragna sat down. She had meant to ask about Tavin first. She had followed the figures to their end before asking after him.

Issa noticed. “I heard they allowed you to see him.”

“They allowed Harrow to make an offer.”

Ragna described it. Issa listened without taking notes. When Ragna came to Ilva's statement, the partner opened the drawer beside the fire and took out a small ledger of her own.

“His authority over the London accounts is under review,” she said. “I cannot remove it alone.”

“Halrik can sign with you.”

“He can. He has not agreed yet. He thinks Merrok can recover the Ministry advance if he stays in place.”

“And if he doesn't?”

“Then he thinks we may have to meet the loss ourselves.”

“It's already been met. By people who were charged for it.”

“I know.” Issa closed the ledger. “Bring me proof that the charge was transferred, and I can ask him to choose which loss he prefers.”

“Ilva checked the transfer.”

“She checked a transfer to the ledger. The reconciliation names the household posting, and Harrow has it.”

Ragna looked at the wrapper holding Borr's statement. It placed twenty bars at Thorn, but left the hidden page out of reach.

“Can you require the ledger back?”

“I have asked. Merrok says Harrow means to move it to the Ministry archive.”

“When?”

“He says he doesn't know. He says the ledger is sealed and the Ministry expects a comparison against the inventory before accepting it into the archive.”

“Do you believe him?”

Issa put Halrik's fallen boot upright. “I believe he would tell me if the date helped him.”

Ragna left with the promise of a meeting that afternoon. At the door a messenger arrived carrying a sealed Ministry order for Issa. He waited while she opened it. She read the first page and asked him to stay for an answer.

Ragna was halfway down the steps when she heard the door open behind her.

“Miss Ragna.”

Issa held the order out. The seal had been broken cleanly. The Ministry required the bank to surrender the remaining records of its workshop reserve for examination. The originals Arven had protected were among those listed.

“It says tomorrow at noon,” Issa said.

Ragna read the list twice. The agreed balances, the disputed balances, the withdrawal slips and the reserve book. Enough to reconstruct what the iron ledger had held. Enough, too, to tell the Ministry which families owed money and to whom.

“Will you give them up?”

“I have not decided.” Issa held out her hand for the order. “I'm going to find Halrik before he puts his boots back on.”

Ragna gave it to her. On the road below, the wagon of empty cages had stopped at the court. Two men were carrying the first one inside.
