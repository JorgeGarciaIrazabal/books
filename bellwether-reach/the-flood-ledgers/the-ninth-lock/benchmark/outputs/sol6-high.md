## P1

At the Ninth Lock, a certificate is a claim on a gate that can answer only one claimant. Sera Venn, an inspector who trusts the measurable part of things, finds two authentic claims for the same tide. The mystery is who made that legal, why the Registry let it stand, and what an honest inspector can still do with an hour left. The hook is civic fraud with a visible waterline: every discovery changes whose doors people must empty.

**Proposed custom:** Before the warning bell, basin households chalk the height of their last flood on their outer doors. Neighbors use the marks to decide what can be carried upstairs and whose steps need help. A mark is neither a guarantee nor an official prediction; erasing one to calm a street can cost somebody the time to move a bed. This gives the investigation witnesses who know the lock through labor, not merely through documents.

Across the series, each book can center on a public instrument that makes a private promise the city cannot wholly keep: a lock assignment, a land survey, a debt ledger. Sera and Ivo would follow the chain from form to hand to consequence, with a distinct case resolved each time and the deeper habits of the Registry accumulating. The emotional promise is competence without absolution. Sera can expose a lie and still have to look at the people who paid for her defensible choice.

## P2

1. **Two claims (17:00–17:10).** Sera brings North’s original to Ivo. He refuses a courtesy release of South’s, then shows it when she makes the request in writing. The seals match. She demands recorded custody for both originals. Their shared reservation number is the first clue. Sera does not yet know who ordered this or why Ivo complied.

2. **A useful lie.** South households present their certificate as workers move seed sacks; North households have prepared around their assignment. Sera traces the second fee to stair repairs at the South seed store. The earlier receipt date exposes a false explanation for the fee. Ivo keeps back the authorizing officer’s name; admitting he registered the pair could end his career.

3. **The author-visible cause.** The chief allocator sold South a legal certificate to fund the stairs after diverting maintenance money to cover a prior North failure. She believed the stairs might save seed, and counted on an ambiguous record to keep both basins preparing as if protected. Her method steals South’s chance to prepare for loss. Clues are the reused number, misdated invoice, and amendment in her hand. None makes the seal proof of capacity.

4. **Ivo chooses exposure.** Sera confronts him with the invoice. He gives the amendment to a Registry witness and signs a statement of his own registration. Their estrangement remains, but his custody of evidence is no longer a bargaining chip. The allocator can dispute intent at hearing; she cannot make two assignments fit one gate.

5. **The tide choice.** With too little time to change assignment safely after the warning bell, Sera surrenders her license and freezes North’s current protection. Her brother is there; she says so in the public notice. She also directs the time and hands still available toward carrying South’s seed above the expected waterline, without claiming this protects South Basin. People there may refuse her help. The seed store and homes take damage; some seed is saved by residents’ work.

6. **Accounting.** At the hearing, the original certificates and amendment establish how the conflict was made. The allocator loses authority; Ivo remains accountable for registering what he knew could not be fulfilled. Sera does not get her license back as a reward for being right. North’s survival and South’s loss both enter the record. **Setup/payoff:** the matching reservation number that first looked like a clerical repetition becomes the trace linking both sales to one available protection.

## P3

Ivo had put out two mugs. The one nearest Sera had no spoon; he remembered she hated the small clatter against the rim. She stayed on her feet.

“South’s original,” she said.

He moved a receipt from one side of the desk to the other. “You can request an inspection copy.”

“A copy won’t show me the edge under the seal.”

“Then request the original.” He opened the drawer where the request forms lived, not the one where he kept certificates.

Sera’s splint knocked the back of the chair when she reached for a form. Her left fingers closed on air. Ivo did not move to steady it. Once, he would have. She took the form with her right hand and laid it over the North certificate in her coat pocket.

“Reason?” she asked.

“You’ve usually had one by the time you come through that door.”

She wrote *Possible duplicate obligation*. The pen dragged where the paper crossed a gouge in the desk.

Ivo read it without touching the page. “That puts my name in the inquiry.”

“Your name is already on the registration.”

“That has always been your answer.”

It took her a moment to hear the other question in it. She had taught him to put his name beneath every judgment he made; when a judgment went wrong, she had let him stand beneath it alone. She wanted to tell him why she had kept her mouth shut. The tide did not care why.

Sera turned the form back and added her own name as requesting inspector, in the blank that would remain beside his in the public copy. “It can be mine too.”

Ivo watched the ink dry. Then he opened the other drawer and set South’s certificate between the mugs. He kept one finger on its corner. Sera did not reach for it. They read the seal together from opposite sides of the desk.

## P4

At five, the Registry floor still held grit from the flood carts. Ivo had put a second chair by his desk, too close for Sera to sit with the splint.

“I need the South certificate,” she said.

“Good afternoon.” He straightened a stack of receipts. “You’ll want a form.”

“I brought North’s.” With her right hand, she set it on the desk. The brass inspection key knocked against her right coat pocket. “An original for an original.”

Ivo looked at the folded paper without touching it. “I don’t exchange Registry property.”

“I’m asking to examine it here.”

“On what grounds?”

She could see the South drawer. The splint made reaching past him ridiculous, which was perhaps its only useful quality today.

“The Ninth Lock has two buyers,” she said.

“You have one certificate.”

“And you have the other.”

Ivo pulled a ruled request sheet from under the blotter and slid it toward her. “Put the accusation where someone else can read it.”

Sera wrote *Inspection of South Basin original. Suspected duplicate assignment.* She signed and pushed it back.

He drew South’s certificate from the drawer by two corners and laid it beside North’s. Same authentic seal. Same lock. South’s claim was dated later, but the seal said nothing about capacity. Ivo had registered both. The paper did not say why.

“Who told you there was room?” she asked.

“That is not what a seal says.”

“I know what a seal says.”

“Then you know you’ve seen what you asked to see.” He reached for South’s original.

She set the brass key between his fingers and the certificate. He stopped.

“I need both held here,” she said, “with an entry that says neither can be substituted before the hearing.”

“A disputed assignment?” He did not look at the key. “North holds the lock now.”

“I know.”

“Your brother holds a house in North.”

“I know that too.”

Outside, a cart wheel scraped the step. Less than half an hour remained before the warning bell. Somebody in South had paid for this paper. They would have planned around it.

Ivo drew out the dispute ledger. Its wet binding curled at the foot. “There is a place for the license number,” he said.

Sera took the license from her inner pocket. With her right hand, she placed it on the open ledger. “You’ll have to take the card itself.”

He opened his palm. She put it there. He copied the number, entered both certificate numbers and the current North assignment, then wrote *frozen pending public hearing*. Sera signed below him. Ivo set her license inside the ledger’s rear pocket and closed its flap. He wrapped the two originals together in a sheet, marked their numbers across the fold, and put the packet into the Registry’s document box in her sight.

He gave her the dispute entry to read. There was no line on it for what South had been promised, only a line for what the lock would do. North Basin remained protected. Sera took the pen and wrote South’s certificate number again in the margin, where a hearing clerk would have to see it when the page was opened.

“You can’t inspect the lock without that card,” Ivo said.

“Then you’ll have to tell them who kept it.”

She put the brass key back in her right pocket. It still fitted the lock. For the first time since her wrist broke, that was not enough.

**State delta (proposed where procedural):** Sera has seen both originals, surrendered her license and signed a freeze; North retains protection. Ivo holds the license and has boxed both originals under numbered custody. Sera retains the brass key. The dispute ledger, document box procedure, and marginal notation are proposed local practice. Neither Sera nor the reader knows Ivo’s motive.

## P5

**Coverage:** P1–P6 of the supplied developmental excerpt only. This is a review of the submitted excerpt and its ending; no wider manuscript or planning outline was assessed.

1. **Critical defect, P3 and P6 — the case is solved by a visitor the story has not earned.** The unnamed girl knows the duplicate’s location and Ivo’s supposed debt motive, then later explains the fraud at an offstage hearing. Sera “immediately understood the entire fraud” from two seals, although those establish registration, not motive or capacity. The reader loses the promised civic mystery and Sera’s investigative agency. Smallest repair: let the girl contribute one bounded, observable fact if she belongs in the story; make Sera compare the originals, identify a discrepancy, and pursue its cause through records and people. Keep the motive provisional until evidence supports it.

2. **Critical defect, P6 — the ending cancels the governing cost.** A spare pump quietly saves both basins, Sera’s license returns, Ivo is forgiven, and their relationship becomes an agreed lesson. The one-basin constraint and the price of freezing have no lasting force, so P4’s sacrifice and P5’s labor read as temporary stage business. Smallest repair: remove the pump and stage the hearing’s findings and losses. Keep North protected by the freeze; show South’s material damage and which seeds residents managed to lift. A ruling may establish responsibility without making everyone forgive it or automatically restoring Sera’s license.

3. **Major causal gap, P4–P5 — the choice is named but barely inhabited.** Sera surrenders the license and Ivo tells her North stays protected; the next paragraph has her at the lock. The excerpt does not show her weighing the brother in North against South’s store or communicating what the freeze means to people acting on the South certificate. The consequence arrives as an image but not as a decision others can contest. Smallest repair: give Sera one specific attempt to warn South before leaving the Registry, and let someone there object to the fact that she has fixed North’s assignment. This need not make her choice wrong.

**Preserve:** P5’s two-person seed sacks, split seam, and thanks without eye contact give South residents work and judgment of their own. They can carry the ending’s emotional weight without a declared moral.

## P6

Ivo set two mugs on the desk. He had remembered how she took her tea. The cup nearest her had a chip at the lip; he turned it so the broken place faced away. Sera left hers where it was.

Her left wrist ached inside the splint. She could have steadied the paper against the cup, but that would have meant moving it. She took the North certificate from her coat with her right hand and laid it on the wood.

“That isn’t the only one,” she said.

Ivo glanced at the paper, then opened the drawer. He brought out South’s certificate and set it beside North’s. Paid for. All of it.

Both seals caught the lamplight. Authentic Registry work, both of them. Neither said the lock had room for two basins. The tea had gone untouched long enough for a skin to form. Ivo watched the surface rather than the certificates.

Sera looked at him. He nudged one mug toward her with the back of a finger, careful to leave the certificates clear. She did not pick it up.

- The tea carries their history through a physical offer and refusal; the two mugs remain active in the scene.
- The seal’s limited meaning is clear without repeating the paid-for image, and Ivo’s motive remains unreadable.
- The short fragments and Sera’s guarded attention stay intact.

## P8

At the Registry, Sera Venn finds two original certificates for the Ninth Lock. Both bear the authentic seal. Both promise protection at the same high tide. The lock can keep faith with only one.

North Basin holds the current assignment, and Sera’s brother lives there. South Basin holds the city’s communal seed store. To freeze the dispute, Sera must surrender the inspector’s license that gives her a voice at the gate. To leave the papers alone is to let one basin prepare for a protection it will never receive.

With the warning bell approaching, Sera needs the second original from Ivo Renn, the former apprentice who registered it. He wants his own account heard. She needs evidence that will survive the tide—and a decision she can make in public afterward.
