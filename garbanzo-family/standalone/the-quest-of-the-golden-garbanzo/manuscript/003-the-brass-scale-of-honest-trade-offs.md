# Chapter 3: The Brass Scale of Honest Trade-Offs

By Saturday morning at nine, the dining table looked like the war tent of a slightly confused wizard army.

Three maps of Spain spilled across the placemats. Beside the marmalade sat the Brass Scale of Honest Trade-Offs, freshly polished by Marissa until its twin pans gleamed like sunset. On the left edge, Garbanzo perched on all four humming green crystals with a tiny quill clamped behind one brass ear, sworn under threat of a battery timeout to keep silent unless spoken to.

"Rule one of this council," Marissa said, pouring dark coffee into Jorge's mug and sliding a bowl of strawberries toward Clara, who had stationed her plush silver unicorn as Sergeant at Arms. "No fairy gold. Every coin we weigh has to come out of real hours we already earned or real sacrifices we agree to make."

"Agreed," Jorge said, setting down his Cadastral Spectacles beside a stack of vault ledgers. "Let us test the baseline first."

He placed two small carved wooden tokens onto the left pan, the Desire Pan: a tidy white stucco house with a sunny garden, plus a steady monthly basket of Spanish groceries, cafés, school books, and train tickets.

The Brass Scale dipped slightly and chimed a clean, comforting *ding*. On the right pan, the Effort Pan, a bundle of shimmering gold work-stones materialized, stopping comfortably shy of the mark they had already built over ten long years.

Jorge beamed. "Look! We made it! Pack the toothbrushes!"

"Hold on, Captain Impulsive," Marissa said dryly. "You only weighed half a life. Where is the family car? What happens when the roof leaks or the washing machine decides to retire before we do? And what about visiting Grandma or taking a real holiday instead of staring at four walls?"

She dropped three more brass tokens onto the Desire Pan: a sensible electric carriage, a travel compass, and a heavy iron shield stamped **UNEXPECTED REPAIRS**.

*Clank.*

The Effort Pan dropped an inch lower. Two glowing orange pebbles clattered into the scale out of thin air: **EXTRA MONTHS AT THE TOWERS**.

"Ouch," Jorge muttered, squinting at the pebbles. "Okay. Still manageable. What if we also put a mountain castle in Guadarrama with four balconies and a swimming pool heated by dragon breath?"

Before Marissa could catch his wrist, Jorge playfully tossed a glittering miniature castle onto the Desire Pan.

The Brass Scale shrieked like an outraged goose. *THUD! CLATTER! BOING!* Twelve towering blocks of **EXTRA WORK YEARS** rained out of the ceiling onto the Effort Pan, bouncing a spoon right out of Clara's cereal bowl.

"Papa!" Clara gasped, catching the spoon mid-flight while Garbanzo whistled in alarm. "The scale just threw Tuesday all the way to high school!"

"Point taken," Jorge said hastily, snatching the miniature castle back off the pan. The giant work blocks dissolved into faint dust. "Okay," he muttered, rubbing the back of his neck.

Marissa pulled out two matching silver pouches, one stamped with Jorge's monogram and one stamped with hers, and placed them side by side beside the scale. For weeks they had talked around how to make both partners genuinely independent without dragging every locked retirement chest through lawyers and dragon-tax tolls.

"If we cross an ocean," Marissa said steadily, "we stand on equal ground. Half yours, half mine. Leave the locked long-term vaults alone where they sit, and balance the rest cleanly out of shared coin so neither of us ever feels like a passenger."

Jorge looked at the twin pouches, then at her eyes. No violins played. Nobody tried to flatter the room with exaggerated poetry. He simply reached out and slid three heavy gold tokens from his pouch into hers until the small balancing needle quivered and settled dead center at **50 / 50**.

"Fair," Jorge said.

"Fair," Marissa echoed, resting her palm lightly beside the pouches.

Then she reached across the salt shaker and lifted the rose-gold Compass of Living Streets. "Now for my non-negotiable law. No cheap stone cottages three miles from a gravel road. No empty fields waiting ten years for neighbors to arrive. Wherever we go, the street has to breathe. Cafés, friends, schools, a proper shopping bazaar, and a real life within easy reach."

Clara raised both hands. "And padel for Papa! And dancing and horses for me!"

At that exact instant, the autumn sunlight slant across the maps shifted.

Neither parent moved a finger. Clara held both hands stiff in the air. Garbanzo was busy polishing his left crystal against a folded napkin.

Yet directly across the parchment map of northern Spain, a crisp, glowing silver paw print pressed lightly onto the rolling valley north of Madrid, right over a town cluster beside a winding river called the Duero, before fading smoothly toward the garden window.

Garbanzo dropped his polishing cloth. "Did anyone else see a ghost paw step on Castile?"

Clara grinned from ear to ear, hugging her silver unicorn tight. "Poki says we need better boots."
