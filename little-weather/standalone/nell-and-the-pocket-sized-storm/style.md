# Style and voice

Close third person centered on Nell; past tense; friendly, concrete English for an adult reading to a seven-year-old. Dialogue uses English double quotation marks. No em dashes. Sound effects are deliberately repeated for participation and emotional continuity. Paragraph lengths vary, with space for pauses and comic timing. Occasional vocabulary such as "peculiar" has immediate contextual support.

Nell is curious and practical, with a child's impulsiveness and capacity to apologize. Grumble is polite, literal, a little grand, and self-conscious about his squeaky thunder. Grandma is calm, dryly funny, and ready to join in. Avoid adult explanations of feelings already shown by an action. End with a specific visual joke, not a moral.

Original working voice sample: "I was bread-roll-sized and thirsty." Protect the direct, slightly indignant logic. Treat the garden band as shared play, not a public performance Grumble is forced to endure.

Spelling: US English, with "gray" and "toward". Keep familiar terms such as boots, garden, and saucepan. No author byline or production commentary in the story.
