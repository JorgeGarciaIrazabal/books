# 4. A Wheel with a Bite

“Low and wooden,” Mila told Mrs. Bell. “With four small wheels.”

The librarian pointed behind the open back door.

There, in a narrow space beside a broom, stood a low wooden trolley.

Mila stared at it. She had walked right past.

“We use it for book boxes,” Mrs. Bell said. “Mr. Moss borrowed it today. He was taking things to the garden.”

“Can we look underneath?” Noah asked.

Dad tipped the empty trolley onto its side and held it steady. Three wheels had smooth rubber edges. The fourth had a piece missing.

It looked as if a very small creature had taken one bite.

Noah brought out his ruler again. The back wheels were the right distance apart for the long tracks.

Mila crouched beside the damaged wheel.

“That would leave a gap.”

“Let's see,” said Noah.

Dad lowered the trolley. Mila moved her toes out of the way.

They wheeled the trolley to the pale muddy patch by the garden gate. Dad helped them roll just the back wheels through it, then onto a clean paving stone.

Two pale lines appeared. One had a little break.

The wheel turned again.

Another break.

Noah measured the space between the breaks, using his ruler twice. Then they went back to the old marks near the library door.

The spaces matched. So did the width of the wheel marks.

“I think this is the trolley,” Mila said.

She wrote that beneath Bea's words. This time, she added why.

Dad took a picture of the old tracks with his phone before anyone could step on them.

Noah drew the wheel with its missing bite in the notebook. Mila waited for him to finish.

“Does Mr. Moss have the moon?” she asked Dad.

“We can ask.”

He tapped a message into his phone while Noah wiped the ruler on a leaf.

<!-- PAGEBREAK -->

The answer came while Dad was returning the trolley to its place.

“He says he moved garden boxes and brought the trolley back,” Dad told them. “He didn't see a moon lantern. He's collecting plants for the garden now and will be back soon.”

“But it was in a box,” Mila said.

“I've told him that. He'll help us look when he gets here.”

Noah stood on the library step, looking down at the marks.

“Where did the trolley go?”

They could follow two pale lines across the first few stones. Near the folding tables, wheels had swung in a wide curve.

Mila walked beside the marks, keeping her shoes off them.

“Into the yard,” she said. “Not through the front door to the road.”

“At least this journey went into the yard,” Noah said.

She nodded. The trolley might have made other journeys. They had watched it make one themselves.

Still, this was somewhere to start.

A table leg stood across the next mark.

Dad moved the table a little, and Mila found the line again. It ran past a crushed blue paper flower and faded on a rough stone.

Beyond it, shoes and wheels had crossed the yard in every direction.

Mila could see neither a neat trail to the bakery nor one to the garden gate.

Noah opened the notebook to his drawing of the yard. “I think that table was—”

A drop of water struck the page.

Then another landed on Mila's nose.

Across the yard, the paper flowers began to shake. A woman gathering paint cups called for someone to bring the drying pictures inside.

Dad picked up his bag. “Under the roof, you two.”

Mila turned back once. Rain dotted the pale wheel marks. The little gaps in the line were getting harder to see.

She covered Noah's notebook with both hands and ran for the library door.
