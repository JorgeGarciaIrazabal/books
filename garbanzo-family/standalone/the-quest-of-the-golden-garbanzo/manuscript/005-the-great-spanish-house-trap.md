# Chapter 5: The Great Spanish House Trap

Early one Monday morning two weeks later, the family landed in Madrid after a forty-minute flight delay out of Newark. They dragged three puffy suitcases, one sleepy six-year-old, and one very loud mini robot zipped inside Jorge's backpack.

"Are we in Spain yet?" Garbanzo muffled against the zipper by the rental car desk. "Tell customs my four green metal feet count as emotional support!"

"Zip it, or you ride in the trunk," Jorge whispered, taking the keys to a small white hatchback that fit Marissa's yellow pad rules.

Their plan for the week was simple. Visit real Spanish towns in person, check true prices, and see if leaving New Jersey really worked outside a computer screen.

Unfortunately, their first tour guide was Señor Flashy Suit.

Real-estate agent Mateo Morales met them outside a bright glass office wearing pointed crocodile shoes, dark indoor sunglasses, and a grin wide enough to sell ice cubes in winter.

"Welcome, friends!" Mateo boomed, clapping Jorge's shoulder. "Forget boring research! Today I show you three unbelievable bargains that will melt your calculator!"

First, Mateo whipped out glowing tablet photos for sunny villas way down south in **Murcia**. On screen, white houses sparkled beside bright blue swimming pools for half the price of a New Jersey townhouse. Clara's jaw dropped at the giant water slides in the photos.

Jorge leaned forward eagerly.

Marissa nudged his elbow, and the next morning they caught an early fast train south to Murcia to see two sample neighborhoods in person. By four o'clock Tuesday afternoon, the truth hit like cold water. Row after row of pretty white townhouses sat silent behind locked metal shutters. Dry weeds poked through empty driveways. Half the owners clearly visited only for two warm weeks in August and vanished until next summer.

"Look around," Marissa said softly beside an empty playground swing creaking in the dry breeze. "Where would I buy warm bread on a wet Tuesday? Who does Clara play with after school when most of these doors stay locked nine months a year?"

"Next stop," Jorge sighed, closing the Murcia folder cleanly.

Back north on Wednesday, Mateo drove them up into the crisp pine mountains northwest of Madrid toward **Collado Mediano**. Here, everything smelled like wet pine needles, woodsmoke, and fresh mountain air. Kids walked down stone streets wearing thick jackets and school backpacks. Even Marissa smiled warmly at the corner bakery.

Then Mateo announced the asking price for an older stone house with steep stairs and mossy tiles. Stepping inside to check the rooms, Jorge turned an old bathroom faucet and winced at a loud rattle in the walls. He pulled a folded page from Saturday's yellow legal pad out of his jacket pocket and scribbled two quick sums on his knee.

"Whoa," Jorge said flatly, snapping his pen shut. "That roof looks fifty years old, the pipes rattle when you turn the sink on, and after fixing the heating we'd blow past our whole cushion by four hundred thousand dollars. Paying for this house means five extra freezing winters on the Manhattan train."

"No thanks," Marissa agreed at once, folding the page away. "Mountains are great to visit on weekends. Not at the cost of our mornings."

Señor Flashy Suit didn't even blink. "Ah! Smart buyers! I saved the absolute secret gem for last!"

With a big flourish, Mateo unrolled a glossy watercolor plan across the car hood. It showed a wide green lawn, a huge modern house, a basketball half-court, and a spotless two-car garage. Even the price looked shockingly low.

"Almost free!" Mateo beamed. "Vacant land ready to build right away! Put down a small deposit today, and our builders will take care of every tiny detail!"

Jorge paused. Years of reading messy spreadsheets in Manhattan had taught him one iron rule: never trust a shiny picture until you inspect the ugly raw numbers underneath.

He stepped into the tall weeds at the edge of the dirt lot. Pulling a yellow tape measure off his belt, he opened the official city land-map app on his tablet.

Two minutes later, he let out a sharp whistle.

"Marissa," Jorge called out, crouching beside a rusty wooden stake. "Come check out Señor Mateo's giant green lawn."

Clara and Marissa hurried over. On the city map screen, clean blue boundary lines sliced straight across Mateo's pretty watercolor dream like an X-ray.

The "wide estate" was actually a skinny little wedge smaller than a normal backyard, pinched tight along the curved curb. If anyone built the garage shown on Mateo's poster, ten concrete feet would slice straight into the neighbor's cypress hedge. Worse, the muddy dirt lacked sewer pipes, buried power cables, and paved sidewalks. Waiting for town hall building permits alone could stall them for three whole years.

"Whoa," Clara whispered, peeking at the tablet over Jorge's shoulder. "His math broke way worse than Garbanzo's."

"Hey!" squeaked Garbanzo from inside Jorge's zipped backpack. "My math is merely overly cheerful! That man is trying to sell us a mud triangle!"

Mateo cleared his throat loudly, his crocodile shoes shuffling backward over the gravel. "Minor paper details! Pure routine! Wait eighteen months, maybe thirty, give or take a water pipe, and..."

Jorge calmly stepped back, wiped mud off his tape measure with a tissue, and planted both sneakers solidly on the road. He asked the four Spanish words every shady house agent hates to hear:

"Show us the *entrega de llaves*."

Mateo blinked above his indoor sunglasses. "The... the exact day we hand you the keys? Well, uh, once the town council signs the summer permits, roughly..."

Jorge gently snapped the folder shut, handed it back with a polite smile, and ushered Marissa and Clara toward the white rental car.

"No real key date, no deal," Jorge said as the hatchback doors slammed shut.

Marissa pulled out her slightly crumpled map of Spain, smoothing the crease right over that green river valley north of the mountains where silver light had brushed two weeks ago.

"Mountains are too steep and coastal pools are too lonely," Marissa said, adjusting the mirror as they pulled back onto the highway. "Let's go see if **Valladolid** feels like real life."
