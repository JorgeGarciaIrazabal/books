# The Ledger of Ashes: creative brief

## Reader promise
A grown-up return to the Wizarding World through the lives of people largely seen from outside in the original stories: a mystery about stolen labour, an uneasy political alliance and the beginning of a rebellion. Familiar magic should feel lived in rather than serve as a parade of references. This is unofficial fan fiction, not an authorised Harry Potter book.

## Audience and form
Adult readers who grew up with Harry Potter; English-language historical fantasy with a mystery and political conflict. A complete short novel of approximately 48,900 words. No illustrations requested.

## Premise
In 1752, Ragna, a goblin accountant, finds a false charge on the day Ministry officials seize the iron ledger holding her community's wages and debts. Her effort to trace the charge puts her against a system that depends on the silence of both wizard officials and well-placed goblins. The immediate question is who diverted the iron and how to prove it before workers lose their livelihoods.

## Characters and stakes
Ragna is competent, cautious and protective of the records entrusted to her; she must learn that guarding information can also protect an injustice. Tavin, her apprentice, values openness and will not always accept her decisions. Ilva, a senior examiner, knows more than she admits and risks her position to leave Ragna a clue. Merrok, a bank official, has signed the seizure order and is implicated in a concealed transaction; his full motive remains to be developed. The dead worker Dorrik leaves a family and colleagues whose needs are immediate, not merely a clue.

## Ending promise
Expose the specific diversion and secure a practical remedy for the wage theft, at real personal cost. The seizure and the public response set up a wider conflict without making the first mystery depend on a sequel for its answer.

## Voice and constraints
Close third person, primarily Ragna, past tense. Concrete detail, dry humour, morally complex adult choices, no imitation of a living author's distinctive prose. Dialogue in double quotation marks; no em dashes by default. Build goblin society through differences of trade, family and politics. Magical limitations are discovered in scenes rather than announced as universal rules.

## Assumptions
1752 and the trilogy are reversible creative choices. Official sources supply broad political facts, not the plot. Publication, rights, and creative direction belong to the author. The complete manuscript has received full developmental, line, copy and internal-continuity review; publication and adaptation rights remain unestablished.
