# Chapter 4: Clara's Rules and a Silver Tail

Right before dinner, at twilight, Clara's bedroom looked like a stuffed-animal city hall.

Twelve plush dragons sat in a semi-circle on the rug, guarded by two sparkly unicorns and a shiny silver plastic crown. At the center sat Clara in purple leggings, slapping her paint-stained paintbrush against a shoebox lid like a judge calling court to order.

"Quiet, dragons!" Clara announced firmly. "This is an Official Kid Inspection. My parents want to move across the big ocean. Since nobody asked **us** until Saturday morning, here are our non-negotiable terms!"

Jorge and Marissa sat obediently cross-legged on the floor beside the bookshelf. Even Garbanzo perched neatly on his four green metal feet atop Clara's toy chest, wearing a bright, slightly crooked unicorn sticker Clara had stuck onto his golden-tan shell.

"First rule," Clara declared, pointing her paintbrush straight at Jorge's nose. "Real books! No boring baby books with three dumb words on a page like 'See Spot Hop.' I am six! I want big readable stories about girl-wizards, sneaky cats, and giant ice caves!"

"Understood, Your Honor," Jorge said solemnly, scribbling `BIG ADVENTURE BOOKS` on the back of an envelope.

"Second rule," Clara continued, ticking points off on her small fingers. "Dancing class. Ice skating where I do not fall on my butt every five seconds. A smooth sidewalk for biking. Horseback riding trails. And real friends who live close enough to walk over for painting!"

"Got it," Marissa grinned, nudging Jorge's arm. "Funny how her list matches my Living Streets rule almost word for word."

"Third rule," Clara added, staring fiercely at Garbanzo. "Nobody cooks dinner by throwing cereal in a pan when Mom steps out."

"Hey!" Jorge protested. "Wait, why is everyone looking at me?"

Garbanzo snickered softly like a rattling tin box. "Even the Supreme Kid Judge knows the kitchen rule, Boss."

When the loud giggling died down, Marissa tucked a stray curl behind Clara's ear. "Did we pass inspection, Judge?"

Clara tapped her chin with the wooden end of her paintbrush. "Pretty close. Just... give me five minutes outside before dinner. Gotta check something."

She slipped into her yellow raincoat, stepped quietly down the back stairs, and pushed open the sliding glass door onto the chilly autumn lawn.

Down near the far wooden fence, the grass felt damp under her sneakers. Overhead, the first white stars poked through deep blue November sky. Away from the bright kitchen lights and laughing voices inside, that stubborn worry she had carried since Monday breakfast came creeping back into her chest.

Moving sounded giant. Giant airplanes. New Spanish words at a brand-new school. New walls, new stairs, new bedrooms.

Clara crouched by the flowerbed and picked up a smooth, round white pebble resting near the clover. She turned it between her cold thumbs. Inside the house sat Poki's gray river stone and little silver collar bell on the windowsill. Those belonged to the whole family. Yet nobody had explained how an invisible house cat could possibly cross three thousand miles of dark ocean water without buying an airplane ticket.

"Hey, Poki," Clara whispered into the quiet garden air, hugging her knees tight. "If we fly far away next year, you won't get lost looking for our old backyard, right?"

For a few seconds, only wind rustled dry oak leaves along the wooden slats.

Then the dusk shadows along the top rail of the fence shifted.

A soft ribbon of pale silver light spilled quietly across the gray cedar wood, and there sat Poki.

Sleek as spilled ink, Poki sat on the rail with only his glossy dark fur and steady amber eyes. He curled his neat tail calmly around his paws and looked down at Clara.

Clara didn't dare breathe. Her heart bumped like a trapped moth, half scared he would vanish if she blinked.

"Are you coming too?" she asked softly.

Poki tilted his head once, just the way he used to whenever Marissa rattled a treat bag or Jorge dropped a stray screw under the fridge. Light as dandelion fluff, the cat stepped down from the fence rail and padded quietly across the damp grass. He touched one gentle white-tipped paw onto the smooth white pebble cupped in Clara's hand.

A warm, gentle tingle shot through the small stone, like sunlight trapped inside a beach rock all summer.

Then Poki blinked slowly at her, a quiet cat kiss, turned cleanly, and melted back into the cool dusk breeze before the back porch light clicked on.

"Clara?" Jorge called through the cracked sliding door. "Dinner's ready! Aunt Lidia texted photos of the chickpea stew she's testing for our next big family party!"

Clara closed her small fist tightly around the warm white pebble and slipped it deep into her raincoat pocket.

"Coming, Papa!" she shouted, sprinting back across the grass with a grin big enough to split her cheeks. "Tell Aunt Lidia to save extra chickpeas! And tell Mom we're definitely moving!"
